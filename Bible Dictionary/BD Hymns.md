# Hymns

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/hymns?lang=eng)

There are various references in the New Testament to the use of hymns, such as in the account of the Last Supper ([[Matthew 26.30|Matt. 26:30]]), where the hymn would be part of the Hallel ([[Psalms 113.1|Ps. 113–18]][[Psalms 113.2|]][[Psalms 113.3|]][[Psalms 113.4|]][[Psalms 113.5|]][[Psalms 113.6|]][[Psalms 113.7|]][[Psalms 113.8|]][[Psalms 113.9|]]; [[Acts 4.24|Acts 4:24]]; [[Acts 16.25|16:25]]; [[1 Corinthians 14.15|1 Cor. 14:15, 26]][[1 Corinthians 14.26|]]; [[Ephesians 5.19|Eph. 5:19]]; [[Colossians 3.16|Col. 3:16]]; [[James 5.13|James 5:13]]). Psalmody and hymnody were highly developed in the religious services of the Jews and were thus easily continued in Christian worship. The Gospel hymns, such as the Magnificat ([[Luke 1.46|Luke 1:46–55]][[Luke 1.47|]][[Luke 1.48|]][[Luke 1.49|]][[Luke 1.50|]][[Luke 1.51|]][[Luke 1.52|]][[Luke 1.53|]][[Luke 1.54|]][[Luke 1.55|]]), the Benedictus ([[Luke 1.68|1:68–79]][[Luke 1.69|]][[Luke 1.70|]][[Luke 1.71|]][[Luke 1.72|]][[Luke 1.73|]][[Luke 1.74|]][[Luke 1.75|]][[Luke 1.76|]][[Luke 1.77|]][[Luke 1.78|]][[Luke 1.79|]]), the Gloria in Excelsis ([[Luke 2.14|2:14]]), and the Nunc Dimittis ([[Luke 2.29|2:29–32]][[Luke 2.30|]][[Luke 2.31|]][[Luke 2.32|]]), have from early times been used in public worship.
