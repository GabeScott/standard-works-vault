# Agriculture

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/agriculture?lang=eng)

First mentioned in [[Genesis 4.2|Gen. 4:2]]; some laws pertaining to it are found in [[Leviticus 19.9|Lev. 19:9–10, 19]][[Leviticus 19.10|]][[Leviticus 19.19|]]; [[Leviticus 23.10|23:10–14, 22]][[Leviticus 23.11|]][[Leviticus 23.12|]][[Leviticus 23.13|]][[Leviticus 23.14|]][[Leviticus 23.22|]]; [[Leviticus 25.3|25:3–7, 11, 18–22]][[Leviticus 25.4|]][[Leviticus 25.5|]][[Leviticus 25.6|]][[Leviticus 25.7|]][[Leviticus 25.11|]][[Leviticus 25.18|]][[Leviticus 25.19|]][[Leviticus 25.20|]][[Leviticus 25.21|]][[Leviticus 25.22|]]; [[Deuteronomy 24.19|Deut. 24:19–22]][[Deuteronomy 24.20|]][[Deuteronomy 24.21|]][[Deuteronomy 24.22|]]; [[Deuteronomy 25.4|25:4]]; [[Deuteronomy 26.1|26]]. Cultivation of the vine and olive, two of the most important products of Palestine, necessarily implies a settled life and could not be practiced by wandering tribes. In addition to the vine and olive, various kinds of cereal grains were extensively cultivated. The plowshare and the pruninghook together represent agriculture ([[Isaiah 2.4|Isa. 2:4]]; [[Joel 3.10|Joel 3:10]]). Among other implements of husbandry mentioned are the sickle, the threshing instrument, the winnowing shovel, and the winnowing fork. Grain was either simply parched ([[Ruth 2.14|Ruth 2:14]]), pounded in a mortar ([[Proverbs 27.22|Prov. 27:22]]), or ground in a handmill made of two slabs of stone that were placed one upon another. The work of grinding was usually performed by slaves, often by female slaves, and hence came to be regarded as degrading ([[Judges 16.21|Judg. 16:21]]; [[Lamentations 5.13|Lam. 5:13]]). Thus Isaiah, addressing Babylon, bids her descend from the throne and grind meal ([[Isaiah 47.2|Isa. 47:2]]). Mention is also made of millstones ([[Matthew 18.6|Matt. 18:6]]); large and heavy, they were turned by the use of animals. See also *[[BD Rain|Rain]].*
