# Syria

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/syria?lang=eng)

Originally known as Aram (or “the highlands”), a general name for the country north and northeast of Palestine. The people were of Semitic origin ([[Genesis 10.22|Gen. 10:22]]; [[Genesis 22.21|22:21]]) and were descended from the same stock as the Israelites. (See *[[BD Aram|Aram]].*) At the beginning of the Hebrew monarchy Syria was divided into a number of small kingdoms, such as Damascus ([[2 Samuel 8.5|2 Sam. 8:5–6]][[2 Samuel 8.6|]]), Maachah and Geshur ([[2 Samuel 15.8|2 Sam. 15:8]]; [[1 Chronicles 19.6|1 Chr. 19:6]]), Beth-Rehob ([[2 Samuel 10.6|2 Sam. 10:6]]), and Zobah ([[2 Samuel 10.6|2 Sam. 10:6, 8]][[2 Samuel 10.8|]]). As the Assyrian kingdom grew stronger, Syria grew weaker, and Damascus was destroyed ([[2 Kings 16.7|2 Kgs. 16:7–9]][[2 Kings 16.8|]][[2 Kings 16.9|]]). Under Seleucus Nicator (312 ) Syria again became a powerful kingdom with Antioch as its capital. Antiochus the Great (223 ) increased its strength, and Palestine became a Syrian province. Then followed the Maccabean rebellion (see *[[BD Maccabees|Maccabees]]*). In 66  Syria was conquered by Pompey and became a Roman province; as such it is mentioned in the New Testament in [[Matthew 4.24|Matt. 4:24]]; [[Luke 2.2|Luke 2:2]]; [[Acts 15.41|Acts 15:41]]; [[Acts 18.18|18:18]]; [[Acts 21.3|21:3]]; [[Galatians 1.21|Gal. 1:21]].
