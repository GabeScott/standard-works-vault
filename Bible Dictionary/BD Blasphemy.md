# Blasphemy

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/blasphemy?lang=eng)

Generally denotes contemptuous speech concerning God or concerning something that stands in a sacred relation toward God, such as His temple, His law, or His prophet. Our Lord was on several different occasions charged by the Jews with speaking blasphemy, because He claimed the right to forgive sins ([[Matthew 9.3|Matt. 9:3]]; [[Luke 5.21|Luke 5:21]]), because He called Himself Son of God ([[John 10.22|John 10:22–36]][[John 10.23|]][[John 10.24|]][[John 10.25|]][[John 10.26|]][[John 10.27|]][[John 10.28|]][[John 10.29|]][[John 10.30|]][[John 10.31|]][[John 10.32|]][[John 10.33|]][[John 10.34|]][[John 10.35|]][[John 10.36|]]), and because He said they would see Him “sitting at the right hand of power, and coming in the clouds of heaven” ([[Matthew 26.64|Matt. 26:64–65]][[Matthew 26.65|]]). These charges would have been true if He had not actually been all that He said He was. The charge brought against Him by the false witnesses at the trial before the Sanhedrin ([[Matthew 26.59|Matt. 26:59–61]][[Matthew 26.60|]][[Matthew 26.61|]]; [[John 19.7|John 19:7]]) was one of blasphemy against God’s temple. Our Lord’s Apostles, on the other hand, regarded the behavior of the Jews toward Him and toward themselves as blasphemy ([[Matthew 27.39|Matt. 27:39]]; [[Luke 22.65|Luke 22:65]]; [[Luke 23.39|23:39]]; [[Acts 13.45|Acts 13:45]]; [[Acts 18.6|18:6]]; [[Acts 26.11|26:11]]). A false accusation of blasphemy was also brought against Naboth ([[1 Kings 21.9|1 Kgs. 21:9–13]][[1 Kings 21.10|]][[1 Kings 21.11|]][[1 Kings 21.12|]][[1 Kings 21.13|]]) and against Stephen ([[Acts 6.11|Acts 6:11]]).

The punishment for willful and intentional blasphemy was death by stoning ([[Leviticus 24.11|Lev. 24:11–16]][[Leviticus 24.12|]][[Leviticus 24.13|]][[Leviticus 24.14|]][[Leviticus 24.15|]][[Leviticus 24.16|]]; [[John 10.31|John 10:31–33]][[John 10.32|]][[John 10.33|]]; [[Acts 7.58|Acts 7:58]]). Blasphemy against the Holy Ghost, which is willfully denying Christ after having received a perfect knowledge of Him from the Holy Ghost, is the unforgivable sin ([[Matthew 12.31|Matt. 12:31–32]][[Matthew 12.32|]]; [[Mark 3.28|Mark 3:28–29]][[Mark 3.29|]]; [[D&C 132.27|D&C 132:27]]).
