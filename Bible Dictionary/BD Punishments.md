# Punishments

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/punishments?lang=eng)

The object of the punishments of the Mosaic law was the extirpation of evil in Israel ([[Deuteronomy 19.19|Deut. 19:19–21]][[Deuteronomy 19.20|]][[Deuteronomy 19.21|]]), and they were apportioned on the principle of righteous retribution. It was life for life, wound for wound, beast for beast ([[Exodus 21.23|Ex. 21:23–25]][[Exodus 21.24|]][[Exodus 21.25|]]; [[Leviticus 24.17|Lev. 24:17–21]][[Leviticus 24.18|]][[Leviticus 24.19|]][[Leviticus 24.20|]][[Leviticus 24.21|]]). The ordinary capital punishment was stoning ([[Deuteronomy 17.5|Deut. 17:5]]), the witnesses, after laying their hands on the head of the condemned, casting the first stone. Executions took place outside the city ([[Leviticus 24.14|Lev. 24:14]]; [[1 Kings 21.13|1 Kgs. 21:13]]; [[Acts 7.58|Acts 7:58]]). The dead body was sometimes burned with fire ([[Leviticus 20.14|Lev. 20:14]]; [[Leviticus 21.9|21:9]]; [[Joshua 7.25|Josh. 7:25]]) or hanged on a tree ([[Deuteronomy 21.22|Deut. 21:22]]). In the latter case it must be cut down and buried the same day ([[Deuteronomy 21.23|Deut. 21:23]]). The inhabitants of a city given to idolatry were to be slain with the edge of the sword ([[Deuteronomy 13.15|Deut. 13:15]]). Minor punishments were: beating with a rod—not more than 40 strokes could be administered ([[Deuteronomy 25.2|Deut. 25:2–3]][[Deuteronomy 25.3|]]; [[Proverbs 10.13|Prov. 10:13]]); suffering the same injury that the wrongdoer had inflicted ([[Leviticus 24.17|Lev. 24:17–22]][[Leviticus 24.18|]][[Leviticus 24.19|]][[Leviticus 24.20|]][[Leviticus 24.21|]][[Leviticus 24.22|]]); fines in money or kind as compensation for the injury done ([[Exodus 22.4|Ex. 22:4–9]][[Exodus 22.5|]][[Exodus 22.6|]][[Exodus 22.7|]][[Exodus 22.8|]][[Exodus 22.9|]]; [[Deuteronomy 22.19|Deut. 22:19]]); or in default, the delinquent might be sold as a slave ([[Exodus 22.3|Ex. 22:3]]). Neither imprisonment nor banishment was a punishment recognized by the law, but offenders were imprisoned under the kings (compare Micaiah, Jeremiah, and the various allusions to prisons), and Ezra was authorized by Artaxerxes to punish lawbreakers by imprisonment and banishment ([[Ezra 7.26|Ezra 7:26]]). Torture was not allowed in any case. Punishments were as a rule inflicted on the offender alone, and not on his wife and family ([[Deuteronomy 24.16|Deut. 24:16]]).
