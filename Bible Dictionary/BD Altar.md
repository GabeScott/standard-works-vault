# Altar

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bd/altar?lang=eng)

Used for sacrifices and offerings and for sacred ordinances of the gospel. Altars are first mentioned in [[Genesis 8.20|Gen. 8:20]]; animal sacrifice was offered on altars ([[Genesis 12.7|Gen. 12:7–8]][[Genesis 12.8|]]; [[Genesis 13.4|13:4, 18]][[Genesis 13.18|]]; [[Genesis 22.9|22:9]]; [[Genesis 26.25|26:25]]; [[Genesis 33.20|33:20]]; [[Genesis 35.7|35:7]]; [[Numbers 23.1|Num. 23:1, 29]][[Numbers 23.29|]]; [[1 Samuel 14.35|1 Sam. 14:35]]; [[1 Kings 18.30|1 Kgs. 18:30–32]][[1 Kings 18.31|]][[1 Kings 18.32|]]). At the four corners were four horns; on these, the loftiest points of the altar, the blood of the sin offerings was put, that the atonement be brought nearer to God. At times these horns were also a place of refuge for fugitives ([[1 Kings 1.50|1 Kgs. 1:50]]; [[1 Kings 2.28|2:28]]). The materials of an altar were either of raised earth ([[Exodus 20.24|Ex. 20:24]]; [[2 Kings 5.17|2 Kgs. 5:17]]) or unhewn stones ([[Exodus 20.25|Ex. 20:25]]; [[Deuteronomy 27.5|Deut. 27:5]]; [[Joshua 8.31|Josh. 8:31]]). The Israelites often enclosed the earth or stones in a wooden case to preserve the proper shape ([[Exodus 27.1|Ex. 27:1]]). The wood again might be overlaid with brass or gold ([[Exodus 39.39|Ex. 39:39]]; [[Exodus 40.26|40:26]]). In the tabernacle the Altar of Burnt Offering stood in a court outside the tent; its outer frame was acacia wood overlaid with brass ([[Exodus 27.1|Ex. 27:1–2]][[Exodus 27.2|]]; [[Exodus 39.39|39:39]]). Around it, halfway up, was a ledge ([[Exodus 27.5|Ex. 27:5]]), with rings and staves by which it was carried. In the Holy Place, before the veil and the mercy seat ([[Exodus 30.6|Ex. 30:6]]) was the Altar of Incense, similar in construction to the Altar of Burnt Offering, but smaller and overlaid with gold. On it incense was burned morning and evening (no animal sacrifices); and on its horns once a year, on the Day of Atonement, the blood of the sin offering was put ([[Exodus 30.10|Ex. 30:10]]). In the Holy of Holies stood the Ark of the Covenant, and over it the mercy seat. These also served as a type of sin offering on the Day of Atonement ([[Leviticus 16.14|Lev. 16:14–15]][[Leviticus 16.15|]]). The furniture of Solomon’s Temple was similar to that of the tabernacle. The Altar of Incense was of cedarwood overlaid with gold. In the outer court stood the brazen altar made after the former pattern, but much larger ([[2 Chronicles 4.1|2 Chr. 4:1]]). Ahaz replaced it by an altar apparently of a Damascus pattern ([[2 Kings 16.11|2 Kgs. 16:11–16]][[2 Kings 16.12|]][[2 Kings 16.13|]][[2 Kings 16.14|]][[2 Kings 16.15|]][[2 Kings 16.16|]]). Under the Law ([[Deuteronomy 12.5|Deut. 12:5, 11–14]][[Deuteronomy 12.11|]][[Deuteronomy 12.12|]][[Deuteronomy 12.13|]][[Deuteronomy 12.14|]]) the people were forbidden to build an altar except in the place where God should choose to put His Name—the temple at Jerusalem; but until the days of Hezekiah sacrifices apparently were offered in other places as well. Altars are also mentioned in [[Matthew 5.23|Matt. 5:23]]; [[Acts 17.23|Acts 17:23]]; [[Hebrews 13.10|Heb. 13:10]]; [[Revelation 6.9|Rev. 6:9]]; [[Revelation 8.3|8:3]]; [[Revelation 9.13|9:13]]; [[Revelation 11.1|11:1]]; [[1 Nephi 2.7|1 Ne. 2:7]]; [[Alma 15.17|Alma 15:17]]; [[D&C 135.7|D&C 135:7]]; [[Abraham 1.8|Abr. 1:8–13]][[Abraham 1.9|]][[Abraham 1.10|]][[Abraham 1.11|]][[Abraham 1.12|]][[Abraham 1.13|]].
