# December 13–19. “He That Overcometh Shall Inherit All Things”: Revelation 15–22

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/51?lang=eng)

The book of Revelation begins with the Savior declaring “I am … the beginning and the ending” ([[Revelation 1.8|Revelation 1:8]]). Fittingly, it ends with the same words (see [[Revelation 22.13|Revelation 22:13]]). But what does that mean? The beginning and the ending of what? The book of Revelation testifies that Jesus Christ is the beginning and end of everything—including the Father’s great plan of salvation. He is “the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world” ([[Revelation 13.8|Revelation 13:8]]). And He is the King of kings who brings an end to wickedness, sorrow, and death, ushering in “a new heaven and a new earth” ([[Revelation 21.1|Revelation 21:1]]).

Before Christ brings this new heaven and new earth, we have much to overcome: worldliness, temptation, spiritual and physical danger. But Jesus Christ is with us during this middle part too. He is the “bright and morning star” that shines in the dark sky as a promise that dawn is coming soon ([[Revelation 22.16|Revelation 22:16]]). And it is coming soon. He is coming. Even as He invites us, “Come unto me” ([[Matthew 11.28|Matthew 11:28]]), He also comes to us. “I come quickly,” He declares. And with hope and faith that has been purified in the fires of latter-day adversity, we answer, “Even so, come, Lord Jesus” ([[Revelation 22.20|Revelation 22:20]]).

### Jesus Christ will return.

[[Revelation 19.6|Revelation 19:6–9]][[Revelation 19.7|]][[Revelation 19.8|]][[Revelation 19.9|]]; [[Revelation 21.2|21:2]] compares the Second Coming of Jesus Christ to “the marriage of the Lamb.” As you read these verses, ponder what this comparison teaches you about the Savior’s return.

- How is your covenant relationship with the Savior similar to a marriage?

- How can you make yourself ready, like a bride preparing for marriage?

### All of God’s children will be judged out of “the book of life.”

Suppose an author offered to write a book about your life. What details or experiences would you want included? If you knew that your future actions would also be recorded, how would you approach your life differently? Think about this as you read about “the book of life” in [[Revelation 20.12|Revelation 20:12–15]][[Revelation 20.13|]][[Revelation 20.14|]][[Revelation 20.15|]].

Now suppose that author is Jesus Christ—it is called “the Lamb’s book of life,” after all ([[Revelation 21.27|Revelation 21:27]]). With that in mind, ponder the questions at the beginning of President Camille N. Johnson’s message “[[2021 October - Invite Christ to Author Your Story.md|Invite Christ to Author Your Story]]” (*Liahona*, Nov. 2021, 80). Perhaps you could take a moment to write a few paragraphs of your book—focusing especially on how you let God prevail in your story.

As you think about your book, you might also ponder these thoughts from Elder Dieter F. Uchtdorf:

“That Day of Judgment will be a day of mercy and love—a day when broken hearts are healed, when tears of grief are replaced with tears of gratitude, when all will be made right.

“Yes, there will be deep sorrow because of sin. Yes, there will be regrets and even anguish because of our mistakes, our foolishness, and our stubbornness that caused us to miss opportunities for a much greater future.

“But I have confidence that we will not only be satisfied with the judgment of God; we will also be astonished and overwhelmed by His infinite grace, mercy, generosity, and love for us, His children” (“[[2016 October - O How Great the Plan of Our God!.md|O How Great the Plan of Our God!]],” *Ensign* or *Liahona*, Nov. 2016, 21).

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[Revelation 15.2|Revelation 15:2–4]][[Revelation 15.3|]][[Revelation 15.4|]]
### I can praise God by singing.

- As you read [[Revelation 15.2|Revelation 15:2–4]][[Revelation 15.3|]][[Revelation 15.4|]] to your children, they could do actions that go with the words, like pretending to play a harp or direct a choir. Then you could share with each other some of your favorite songs about Heavenly Father and Jesus Christ. Sing a few of the songs together, and talk with each other about the Spirit you feel when you sing praises to Them.

[[Revelation 19.7|Revelation 19:7–8]][[Revelation 19.8|]]; [[Revelation 20.2|20:2–3]][[Revelation 20.3|]]; [[Revelation 21.1|21:1–4]][[Revelation 21.2|]][[Revelation 21.3|]][[Revelation 21.4|]]; [[Revelation 22.1|22:1–5]][[Revelation 22.2|]][[Revelation 22.3|]][[Revelation 22.4|]][[Revelation 22.5|]]
### Jesus Christ’s Second Coming will be joyful.

- To help your children understand the symbolism of [[Revelation 19.7|Revelation 19:7–8]][[Revelation 19.8|]], you could look at wedding pictures from your family history or talk about a time when your family attended a wedding celebration. Why are people happy at a wedding? Then you could talk about how the Savior’s Second Coming is like a marriage between “the Lamb” (Jesus Christ) and His Church (or all of us). How does a bride get ready for marriage? How do we get ready for the Savior’s return? Help your children find answers in President Russell M. Nelson’s message “[[2024 October - The Lord Jesus Christ Will Come Again.md|The Lord Jesus Christ Will Come Again]],” especially [[2024 October - The Lord Jesus Christ Will Come Again.md|the last five paragraphs]] (*Liahona*, Nov. 2024, 121–22).

- Share with each other why you are looking forward to Jesus’s Second Coming. You could sing together a song about the Second Coming, such as “[When the Savior Comes Again](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/music/hymns-for-home-and-church/when-the-savior-comes-again?lang=eng)” (*Hymns—For Home and Church*, Gospel Library). You could also work together to find other ways to complete the sentence “When the Savior comes again, .” Use words you find in these scriptures: [[Revelation 20.2|Revelation 20:2–3]][[Revelation 20.3|]]; [[Revelation 21.1|21:1–4]][[Revelation 21.2|]][[Revelation 21.3|]][[Revelation 21.4|]]; [[Revelation 22.1|22:1–5]][[Revelation 22.2|]][[Revelation 22.3|]][[Revelation 22.4|]][[Revelation 22.5|]].

[[Revelation 20.12|Revelation 20:12–13]][[Revelation 20.13|]]
### All of us will be judged out of “the book of life.”

- After reading about “the book of life” ([[Revelation 20.12|Revelation 20:12]]), you and your children could make a small book (even if it’s just a few pages folded together). You could write in it some ways your children have followed the Savior. You could also talk about how the Savior has helped you repent when you’ve needed to change things about your “book of life.”

[[Revelation 22.16|Revelation 22:16]]
### Jesus is my “bright and morning star.”

- If possible, it could be quite meaningful to read [[Revelation 22.16|Revelation 22:16]] early in the morning, just before sunrise. See if you can find the “morning star” (see also the picture at the end of this outline). How is Jesus Christ like that star?
