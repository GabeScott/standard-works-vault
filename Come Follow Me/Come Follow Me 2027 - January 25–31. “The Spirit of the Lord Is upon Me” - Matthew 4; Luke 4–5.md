# January 25–31. “The Spirit of the Lord Is upon Me”: Matthew 4; Luke 4–5

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/05?lang=eng)

From His youth, Jesus learned that He had a unique, sacred mission. And yet, as Jesus prepared to begin His earthly ministry, the adversary sought to plant doubt in the Savior’s mind. “*If* thou be the Son of God,” Satan said ([[Luke 4.3|Luke 4:3]]; emphasis added). But the Savior had communed with His Father in Heaven. He knew the scriptures, and He knew who He was. To Him, Satan’s offer—“All this power will I give thee” ([[Luke 4.6|Luke 4:6]])—was a hollow one, for the Savior’s lifelong preparation allowed Him to receive “the power of the Spirit” ([[Luke 4.14|Luke 4:14]]). So, despite temptation, trials, and rejection, Jesus Christ never wavered from His appointed work: “I must preach the kingdom of God … : for therefore am I sent” ([[Luke 4.43|Luke 4:43]]).

## Ideas for Learning at Home and at Church

[[Matthew 4.1|Matthew 4:1–2]][[Matthew 4.2|]]
### Communing with God prepares me to serve Him.

To prepare for His mission, Jesus went into the wilderness to fast and “to be with God” (Joseph Smith Translation, Matthew 4:1 \[in [[Matthew 4.1|Matthew 4:1]], footnote *b*\]). Why do you think fasting was part of His preparation? Think of what you do to feel close to God. How have those experiences helped you prepare to serve the Lord?

### Jesus Christ is the Savior whom God promised to send.

If you were asked to describe why Jesus Christ was sent to earth, what would you say? In a synagogue in His hometown of Nazareth, Jesus described His own mission using the words of Isaiah (see [[Isaiah 61.1|Isaiah 61:1–2]][[Isaiah 61.2|]]; [[Luke 4.18|Luke 4:18–19]][[Luke 4.19|]]).

- What do you learn about His mission as you read these verses?

- What are some ways the Savior invites you to participate in His work?

Although the Jews had been waiting for centuries for Isaiah’s prophecy to be fulfilled, many did not accept that Jesus was the Savior. As you read [[Luke 4.20|Luke 4:20–30]][[Luke 4.21|]][[Luke 4.22|]][[Luke 4.23|]][[Luke 4.24|]][[Luke 4.25|]][[Luke 4.26|]][[Luke 4.27|]][[Luke 4.28|]][[Luke 4.29|]][[Luke 4.30|]] (see also [[Mark 6.1|Mark 6:1–6]][[Mark 6.2|]][[Mark 6.3|]][[Mark 6.4|]][[Mark 6.5|]][[Mark 6.6|]]), try to put yourself in the place of the people of Nazareth.

- What might have made it hard for them to accept Jesus as the Messiah?

- What experiences have helped you believe that Jesus Christ is your personal Savior?

### As I trust in the Lord, He can help me reach my divine potential.

Sometimes the direction the Lord gives us doesn’t make sense at first. That happened to Simon Peter in [[Luke 5.1|Luke 5:1–11]][[Luke 5.2|]][[Luke 5.3|]][[Luke 5.4|]][[Luke 5.5|]][[Luke 5.6|]][[Luke 5.7|]][[Luke 5.8|]][[Luke 5.9|]][[Luke 5.10|]][[Luke 5.11|]]; look for what the Savior asked of him.

- Why might Peter have doubted Jesus’s instructions?

- How might this experience have affected Peter’s feelings about the Savior and himself? How might it have prepared him for His calling?

- What has the Savior asked you to forsake? How has He blessed you for doing so?

- Why is it important to obey the Lord, even when His commandments don’t make sense to us at first?

President Ezra Taft Benson taught, “Men and women who turn their lives over to God will discover that He can make a lot more out of their lives than they can” (*Teachings of Presidents of the Church: Ezra Taft Benson* \[2014\], [42](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/teachings-of-presidents-of-the-church-ezra-taft-benson/chapter-1-the-great-commandment-love-the-lord?lang=eng&id=p38#p38)). How did the Lord do that for Simon Peter and his fellow fishermen? How has He done it for you?

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[Matthew 4.1|Matthew 4:1–2]][[Matthew 4.2|]]
### Fasting can help me feel close to Heavenly Father.

- Reading about Jesus’s efforts to “be with God” in [[Matthew 4.1|Matthew 4:1–2]][[Matthew 4.2|]] (including the Joseph Smith Translation revisions found in the footnotes) could be an opportunity to teach your children about fasting. You could write questions about fasting (such as why, when, or how we fast) on pieces of paper and invite your children to pick a question. They could look for answers in Topics and Questions, “[Fasting and Fast Offerings](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/gospel-topics/fasting-and-fast-offerings-study-guide?lang=eng)” (Gospel Library). What experiences can you or the children share about fasting?
