# November 8–14. “Be Ye Doers of the Word, and Not Hearers Only”: James

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/46?lang=eng)

Sometimes just one verse of scripture can change the world. [[James 1.5|James 1:5]] is one of these. It seems like a simple bit of counsel—if you need wisdom, ask God. But when 14-year-old Joseph Smith read that verse, “it seemed to enter with great force into every feeling of \[his\] heart” ([[Joseph Smith History 1.12|Joseph Smith—History 1:12]]). And Joseph wasn’t just a “hearer” of the word; he was a “doer” (see [[James 1.21|James 1:21–22]][[James 1.22|]]). He acted on what he read and sought wisdom from God through prayer. God did indeed give liberally, granting Joseph a vision that changed the course of his life and led to the Restoration of the Church of Jesus Christ on the earth. All of us are blessed today because Joseph Smith read and acted on [[James 1.5|James 1:5]].

What will you find as you study the Epistle of James? Perhaps a verse or two will change you or someone you love. You might find encouragement to speak kindly or to be more patient. You might feel prompted to better align your actions with your faith. Whatever inspires you, let these words “enter … into every feeling of \[your\] heart.” And then, when you “receive with meekness the … word,” as James wrote ([[James 1.21|James 1:21]]), be a doer, not a hearer only.

For more about the Epistle of James, see Guide to the Scriptures, “[[GS James, Brother of the Lord|James, Brother of the Lord]],” Gospel Library.

## Ideas for Learning at Home and at Church

[[James 1.2|James 1:2–4]][[James 1.3|]][[James 1.4|]]; [[James 5.7|5:7–11]][[James 5.8|]][[James 5.9|]][[James 5.10|]][[James 5.11|]]
### “Let patience have her perfect work.”

Ponder a time when you had to be patient. What did you learn from this experience? Think about that as you search [[James 1.2|James 1:2–4]][[James 1.3|]][[James 1.4|]]; [[James 5.7|5:7–11]][[James 5.8|]][[James 5.9|]][[James 5.10|]][[James 5.11|]]. Also consider questions like these:

- What would you say is the “perfect work” of patience? ([[James 1.4|James 1:4]]).

- Who has been “an example … of patience” for you? ([[James 5.10|James 5:10]]).

- What experiences with patience have revealed the Lord’s “tender mercy” to you? ([[James 5.11|James 5:11]]).

### “Faith without works is dead.”

Sometimes people emphasize belief in Christ so much, they make it sound like works are not needed. Others emphasize works so much, they seem to neglect belief. Notice how James balances these two important principles. In particular, study [[James 1.3|James 1:3–8, 21–25]][[James 1.4|]][[James 1.5|]][[James 1.6|]][[James 1.7|]][[James 1.8|]][[James 1.21|]][[James 1.22|]][[James 1.23|]][[James 1.24|]][[James 1.25|]]; [[James 2.14|2:14–26]][[James 2.15|]][[James 2.16|]][[James 2.17|]][[James 2.18|]][[James 2.19|]][[James 2.20|]][[James 2.21|]][[James 2.22|]][[James 2.23|]][[James 2.24|]][[James 2.25|]][[James 2.26|]]; [[James 4.17|4:17]] with these questions in mind:

- Why does faith require works?

- Why do works require faith?

- What does it mean to have faith in Jesus Christ? You can find additional insights in “[Faith in Jesus Christ](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/preach-my-gospel-2023/04-chapter-3/10-chapter-3-lesson-3?lang=eng&id=title21-p63#title21),” in *Preach My Gospel: A Guide to Sharing the Gospel of Jesus Christ* (2023), 65–67.

It might also help to think of ways to complete this sentence: “Faith without works is like  without .”

What can you do to show your faith in Jesus Christ?

See also [[Matthew 7.21|Matthew 7:21–23]][[Matthew 7.22|]][[Matthew 7.23|]]; “[A Poor Wayfaring Man of Grief](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/hymns/a-poor-wayfaring-man-of-grief?lang=eng),” *Hymns*, no. 29.

[[James 1.5|James 1:5–6]][[James 1.6|]]
### When I “ask in faith,” God gives “liberally.”

The principles taught in [[James 1.5|James 1:5–6]][[James 1.6|]] led Joseph Smith to a life-changing spiritual experience. As you study these verses, ponder questions like these:

- What influence has [[James 1.5|James 1:5–6]][[James 1.6|]] had in your life?

- What has Joseph Smith’s experience with these verses taught you about finding answers to your own questions? (see [[Joseph Smith History 1.10|Joseph Smith—History 1:10–17]][[Joseph Smith History 1.11|]][[Joseph Smith History 1.12|]][[Joseph Smith History 1.13|]][[Joseph Smith History 1.14|]][[Joseph Smith History 1.15|]][[Joseph Smith History 1.16|]][[Joseph Smith History 1.17|]]).

- What experiences have taught you that “the testimony of James \[is\] true”? ([[Joseph Smith History 1.26|Joseph Smith—History 1:26]]).

[[James 1.26|James 1:26–27]][[James 1.27|]]
### “Pure religion” includes taking care of people in need.

Consider watching the video “True Christianity” (Gospel Library). Then read James’s definition of “pure religion” in [[James 1.26|James 1:26–27]][[James 1.27|]]. What can you do to make your practice of religion more authentic?

### My words have the power to hurt or bless others.

Among the rich imagery James used in his epistle, some of his most vivid language is found in his counsel about language. Consider making a list of the ways James described the tongue or mouth. What do you learn from each comparison? Think of something you can do to bless someone with your words (see [[D&C 108.7|Doctrine and Covenants 108:7]]).

See also Ronald A. Rasband, “[[2024 April - Words Matter.md|Words Matter]],” *Liahona*, May 2024, 70–76.

[[James 2.1|James 2:1–9]][[James 2.2|]][[James 2.3|]][[James 2.4|]][[James 2.5|]][[James 2.6|]][[James 2.7|]][[James 2.8|]][[James 2.9|]]
### True disciples of Jesus Christ love all people, regardless of their circumstances.

James warned the Saints specifically against favoring the rich and despising the poor. But his warning can apply to any biases or prejudices we have toward others. As you prayerfully study [[James 2.1|James 2:1–9]][[James 2.2|]][[James 2.3|]][[James 2.4|]][[James 2.5|]][[James 2.6|]][[James 2.7|]][[James 2.8|]][[James 2.9|]], consider replacing phrases in these verses, such as “a poor man in vile raiment” ([[James 2.2|verse 2]]), with words or phrases that describe someone you might judge unfairly.

See also Quentin L. Cook, “[[2024 April - Be One with Christ.md|Be One with Christ]],” *Liahona*, May 2024, 49–52.

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[James 1.5|James 1:5–6]][[James 1.6|]]
### I can ask Heavenly Father to help me learn what is true.

- Help your children learn that [[James 1.5|James 1:5]] inspired Joseph Smith to seek Heavenly Father in prayer. Perhaps they could memorize the phrase “If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God.”

- You could then show a picture of the First Vision (*Gospel Art Book*, no. [90](https://assets.churchofjesuschrist.org/21/50/2150f03ca9bf62fc17fe9ed669f497f622501ddb/first_vision_joseph_smith_kapp.png)) while you read [[Joseph Smith History 1.10|Joseph Smith—History 1:10–14]][[Joseph Smith History 1.11|]][[Joseph Smith History 1.12|]][[Joseph Smith History 1.13|]][[Joseph Smith History 1.14|]]. Your children could listen for things Joseph Smith did to receive answers to his questions (see also “[Joseph Smith’s First Vision](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/doctrine-and-covenants-stories-2025/03-joseph-smiths-first-vision?lang=eng),” in *Doctrine and Covenants Stories*, 9–12). Share with each other experiences when God answered your prayers.

> **Seeing multiple examples will help children understand eternal truth.** As you teach your children [[James 1.5|James 1:5–6]][[James 1.6|]], they could search the *Gospel Art Book* for other examples of people who received answers to prayer (see, for example, nos. [72](https://assets.churchofjesuschrist.org/c9/94/c994fad34ac4959be4d18b3c2ccd9c9d91536ea5/and_my_soul_hungered_enos.jpeg), [85](https://assets.churchofjesuschrist.org/45/fd/45fdd10a2e79218905763b007bfa4d0532559626/brother_jared_seeing_finger_lord.jpeg), [97](https://assets.churchofjesuschrist.org/da/26/da268fd09b98ec3eb86ef724fee1d1849d05af3b/joseph_in_liberty_jail_swindle.jpeg)). In each example, what wisdom did the person lack? How did God answer their prayers?


[[James 1.22|James 1:22–27]][[James 1.23|]][[James 1.24|]][[James 1.25|]][[James 1.26|]][[James 1.27|]]; [[James 2.14|2:14–18]][[James 2.15|]][[James 2.16|]][[James 2.17|]][[James 2.18|]]
### “Faith without works is dead.”

- Consider showing your children a flashlight without batteries, a pencil without lead, or something else that is missing something it needs to work. Maybe your children can help think of examples. Ask your children to read [[James 2.14|James 2:14–17]][[James 2.15|]][[James 2.16|]][[James 2.17|]]. How are these objects like belief without works?

- After you and your children read [[James 1.22|James 1:22–27]][[James 1.23|]][[James 1.24|]][[James 1.25|]][[James 1.26|]][[James 1.27|]]; [[James 2.14|2:14–18]][[James 2.15|]][[James 2.16|]][[James 2.17|]][[James 2.18|]], they could talk about how to show that they are “doers of the word.” For instance, how do they keep their baptismal covenant? Do they know someone who is sick or lonely who they might visit? How could they serve their families?

### I can speak with kindness.

- How can your children learn that their words have power, for good or bad? Maybe they could draw a picture of something they find in [[James 3.1|James 3:1–13]][[James 3.2|]][[James 3.3|]][[James 3.4|]][[James 3.5|]][[James 3.6|]][[James 3.7|]][[James 3.8|]][[James 3.9|]][[James 3.10|]][[James 3.11|]][[James 3.12|]][[James 3.13|]] about controlling our words (see also “A Letter from James,” in *New Testament Stories*). You could also sing together a song about using kind words, such as “[Let Us Oft Speak Kind Words](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/hymns/let-us-oft-speak-kind-words?lang=eng)” (*Hymns*, no. 232).> 3:56


- After reviewing [[James 3.1|James 3:1–13]][[James 3.2|]][[James 3.3|]][[James 3.4|]][[James 3.5|]][[James 3.6|]][[James 3.7|]][[James 3.8|]][[James 3.9|]][[James 3.10|]][[James 3.11|]][[James 3.12|]][[James 3.13|]] together, you could review the standards for language in *For the Strength of Youth: A Guide for Making Choices*, 30. Help the children set a goal to improve the way they speak to others. Encourage them to ask Heavenly Father to help them with this goal.

[[James 5.7|James 5:7–11]][[James 5.8|]][[James 5.9|]][[James 5.10|]][[James 5.11|]]
### Receiving God’s blessings requires patience.

- To teach your children about patience, you could summarize [[James 5.7|James 5:7]] in your own words and show a picture of a seed or seedling. Why do we need patience when we grow plants? What would happen if we tried to pull on the seedling to make it grow faster?

- You could also share the video “Continue in Patience” (Gospel Library). Or share the story of Job, who is mentioned in [[James 5.11|James 5:11]] as an example of patience (see “[Job](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/old-testament-stories-2022/job?lang=eng),” in *Old Testament Stories*, 145–47). How does God bless us for being patient?
