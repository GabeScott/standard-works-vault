# January 4–10. We Have Come to Worship Him: Matthew 2; Luke 2

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/02?lang=eng)

From the day of His birth, it was clear that Jesus was no ordinary child. It wasn’t just the new star or the angels that made Jesus’s birth so remarkable. It was also the fact that such a variety of faithful people—from different nations, professions, and backgrounds—felt drawn to Him. Even before He said, “Come, follow me,” they came ([[Luke 18.22|Luke 18:22]]). Not everyone came, of course. There were many who hardly noticed Him, and an envious ruler even sought His life. But the humble, pure, devoted seekers of righteousness saw Him for who He was—their promised Messiah. Often “they found him in the temple” ([[Luke 2.46|Luke 2:46]]), where all sincere seekers find Him. Their devotion inspires our own, for the “good tidings of great joy” brought to the shepherds were for “all people,” and the “Saviour, which is Christ the Lord,” was born that day unto all of us ([[Luke 2.10|Luke 2:10–11]][[Luke 2.11|]]).

### The events of the Savior’s birth testify of His divine identity and mission.

The details of the Savior’s birth are so familiar that we might take them for granted. But have you ever wondered why things happened the way they did? Why was He born in Bethlehem rather than His mother’s home in Nazareth? Why was He laid in a manger? Why were shepherds the first to worship Him?

As you read about the Savior’s birth, ponder the possible spiritual symbolism of the following details. For each one, ask yourself, “What does this teach me about the Savior, His roles, and His divine mission?”

- Bethlehem ([[Luke 2.4|Luke 2:4]]). In Hebrew, *Bethlehem* means “house of bread.” See also [[John 6.31|John 6:31–35]][[John 6.32|]][[John 6.33|]][[John 6.34|]][[John 6.35|]].

- Manger ([[Luke 2.7|Luke 2:7, 12]][[Luke 2.12|]]). *Manger* comes from a Latin word that means “to chew” or “to eat.” See also [[John 6.48|John 6:48–51]][[John 6.49|]][[John 6.50|]][[John 6.51|]].

- Shepherds ([[Luke 2.8|Luke 2:8]]). See also [[John 10.11|John 10:11–15]][[John 10.12|]][[John 10.13|]][[John 10.14|]][[John 10.15|]].

- New star ([[Matthew 2.2|Matthew 2:2]]). See also [[Luke 2.32|Luke 2:32]]; [[John 8.12|John 8:12]].

What other details from the Savior’s birth teach you something about Him and His mission?

### Parents can receive revelation to protect their families.

How could Joseph protect young Jesus from powerful enemies like King Herod? The same way faithful parents today protect their children—with heaven’s help. Like the Wise Men, Joseph received a revelation that warned him of danger, and he acted on it. As you read about Joseph’s experience in [[Matthew 2.13|Matthew 2:13–23]][[Matthew 2.14|]][[Matthew 2.15|]][[Matthew 2.16|]][[Matthew 2.17|]][[Matthew 2.18|]][[Matthew 2.19|]][[Matthew 2.20|]][[Matthew 2.21|]][[Matthew 2.22|]][[Matthew 2.23|]], think about the physical and spiritual dangers you face today.

- How has God guided you to safety from those dangers?

- What can you do to receive His guidance in the future?

See also Dieter F. Uchtdorf, “[[2023 April - Jesus Christ Is the Strength of Parents.md|Jesus Christ Is the Strength of Parents]],” *Liahona*, May 2023, 55–59; “The First Christmas Spirit” (video), Gospel Library.

### Even as a youth, Jesus was focused on doing His Father’s will.

Luke gives us one of our earliest glimpses of the character of young Jesus. As you read [[Luke 2.40|Luke 2:40–51]][[Luke 2.41|]][[Luke 2.42|]][[Luke 2.43|]][[Luke 2.44|]][[Luke 2.45|]][[Luke 2.46|]][[Luke 2.47|]][[Luke 2.48|]][[Luke 2.49|]][[Luke 2.50|]][[Luke 2.51|]], ponder these questions:

- What do you learn about the Savior from His interaction with the teachers in the temple? (see the Joseph Smith Translation in [[Luke 2.46|verse 46]], footnote *c*).

- What do you learn about Him from His interaction with His mother?

- How would you describe the “Father’s business”?

### Jesus Christ set a pattern for personal growth.

Luke summarizes young Jesus’s growth in one verse that speaks volumes. As you read [[Luke 2.52|Luke 2:52]], ponder questions like these, and compare the suggested sections from *For the Strength of Youth: A Guide for Making Choices* (2026):

- What do you think it means to increase in favor with God? (“Walk in God’s Light,” 22–26).

- How does the gospel of Jesus Christ help you increase in favor with others? (“Love God, Love Your Neighbor,” 28–32).

### Jesus grew “in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man.”

- Reading together [[Luke 2.40|Luke 2:40, 52]][[Luke 2.52|]] and explaining what the words mean can give your children an opportunity to talk about ways Jesus “increased,” or grew. They could then do simple activities for each. For example, they could open a book (wisdom). You could measure each child’s height (stature). They could share their favorite scripture (favor with God) and act out helping someone in need (favor with man). Help your children see how they are growing like Jesus.

- You and your children could talk about what Jesus might have been like when He was their age. How would He have treated His family? You could also sing “[Jesus Once Was a Little Child](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/jesus-once-was-a-little-child?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 55).
