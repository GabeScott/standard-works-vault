# October 19–25. “Before I Formed Thee in the Belly I Knew Thee”: Jeremiah 1–3; 7; 16–18; 20

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-old-testament-2026/43?lang=eng)

At first, Jeremiah didn’t think he would make a good prophet. “Behold, I cannot speak,” he protested ([[Jeremiah 1.6|Jeremiah 1:6]]). But the Lord reassured him, “I have put my words in thy mouth” ([[Jeremiah 1.9|verse 9]]). Jeremiah felt that he was an inexperienced “child” ([[Jeremiah 1.6|verse 6]]), but the Lord explained that he was actually more prepared than he realized—he had been ordained to this calling even before he was born (see [[Jeremiah 1.5|verse 5]]). So Jeremiah set aside his fears and accepted the call. He warned Jerusalem’s kings and priests that their pretended holiness would not save them. The “child” who thought he could not speak came to feel God’s word “in \[his\] heart as a burning fire” and could not be silent ([[Jeremiah 20.9|Jeremiah 20:9]]).

Jeremiah’s story is also your story. God knew you, too, before you were born and prepared you for your mission in life. Among other things, that mission includes something Jeremiah foresaw: gathering God’s people, one by one, to “bring \[them\] to Zion” ([[Jeremiah 3.14|Jeremiah 3:14]]). And even if you don’t know exactly what to do or say, you can “be not afraid … ; for I am with thee, saith the Lord” ([[Jeremiah 1.8|Jeremiah 1:8, 19]][[Jeremiah 1.19|]]).

For an overview of the book of Jeremiah, see “[[BD Jeremiah|Jeremiah]]” in the Bible Dictionary.

### Prophets are called to speak God’s word.

Imagine you’re talking to a friend who has never heard of prophets, and you decide to explain the concept using Jeremiah’s call to be a prophet in [[Jeremiah 1.4|Jeremiah 1:4–19]][[Jeremiah 1.5|]][[Jeremiah 1.6|]][[Jeremiah 1.7|]][[Jeremiah 1.8|]][[Jeremiah 1.9|]][[Jeremiah 1.10|]][[Jeremiah 1.11|]][[Jeremiah 1.12|]][[Jeremiah 1.13|]][[Jeremiah 1.14|]][[Jeremiah 1.15|]][[Jeremiah 1.16|]][[Jeremiah 1.17|]][[Jeremiah 1.18|]][[Jeremiah 1.19|]]. What do you find in these verses that teaches something about prophets of God? You could also look in [[Jeremiah 7.1|Jeremiah 7:1–7]][[Jeremiah 7.2|]][[Jeremiah 7.3|]][[Jeremiah 7.4|]][[Jeremiah 7.5|]][[Jeremiah 7.6|]][[Jeremiah 7.7|]]; [[Jeremiah 20.8|20:8–9]][[Jeremiah 20.9|]] and a hymn like “[We Listen to a Prophet’s Voice](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/hymns/we-listen-to-a-prophets-voice?lang=eng)” (*Hymns*, no. 22).

You might also want to teach your friend about today’s living prophet. Is there anything in these verses that might help? For example, what do prophets in our day “root out” or “pull down”? What do they “build” and “plant”? ([[Jeremiah 1.10|Jeremiah 1:10]]). How have you come to know that our living prophet is called of God?

[[Jeremiah 1.5|Jeremiah 1:5]]
### God knew me before I was born.

What did Jeremiah learn about himself in [[Jeremiah 1.5|Jeremiah 1:5]]? As a newly called prophet, why would that information be important for him to know?

Much of what Jeremiah learned about himself in [[Jeremiah 1.5|Jeremiah 1:5]] is also true about you. God also knew you before you were born and prepared you for specific responsibilities. To learn more about your life before you were born, read [[Alma 13.1|Alma 13:1–4]][[Alma 13.2|]][[Alma 13.3|]][[Alma 13.4|]]; [[D&C 138.53|Doctrine and Covenants 138:53–56]][[D&C 138.54|]][[D&C 138.55|]][[D&C 138.56|]]; and [[Abraham 3.22|Abraham 3:22–23]][[Abraham 3.23|]]. You might make a list of truths you discover. Why is this information important for you to know?

To learn about how these truths affected the life of Elder Ahmad S. Corbitt, study his message “[[2021 April - You Can Gather Israel!.md|You Can Gather Israel!]]” (*Liahona*, May 2021, 61–63). How does knowing about your premortal life affect the way you live your mortal life?

Here is what President Russell M. Nelson said about your premortal life:

“Your Heavenly Father has known you for a very long time. You, as His son or daughter, were chosen by Him to come to earth at this precise time, to be a leader in His great work on earth. You were chosen *not* for your bodily characteristics but for your *spiritual* attributes, such as bravery, courage, integrity of heart, a thirst for truth, a hunger for wisdom, and a desire to serve others.

“You developed some of these attributes premortally. Others you can develop here on earth as you persistently seek them” (“[[2013 October - Decisions for Eternity.md|Decisions for Eternity]],” *Ensign* or *Liahona*, Nov. 2013, 107).

Think of some situations you might face in which it would be important to remember these truths about your premortal life. What can you do to remind yourself of these truths?

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[Jeremiah 1.5|Jeremiah 1:5]]
### Heavenly Father knew me before I was born.

- To introduce the important truth taught in [[Jeremiah 1.5|Jeremiah 1:5]], you could show your children a picture of a baby (or, if possible, you could look at pictures of your children when they were babies). Ask your children if they know where they lived before they were born. You could also sing together a song that teaches about our premortal life, such as “[I Lived in Heaven](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/i-lived-in-heaven?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 4).

- If your children would like to learn more, you could help them find “[Premortal Life](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/gs/premortal-life?lang=eng)” in the Guide to the Scriptures. They could look for answers to “who,” “when,” and “what” questions about the premortal life.
