# December 28–January 3. “Be It unto Me According to Thy Word”: Matthew 1; Luke 1

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/01?lang=eng)

From a mortal perspective, it was impossible. A virgin could not conceive—and neither could a woman who had never been able to have children and was now well past childbearing years. But God had a plan for the birth of His Son and the birth of John the Baptist, so both Mary and Elisabeth, against all earthly odds, became mothers. It’s helpful to remember their miraculous experiences whenever we face something that seems impossible. Can we overcome our weaknesses? Can we touch the heart of an unresponsive family member? Gabriel could easily have been speaking to us when he reminded Mary, “With God nothing shall be impossible” ([[Luke 1.37|Luke 1:37]]). And Mary’s response can also be ours when God reveals His will: “Be it unto me according to thy word” ([[Luke 1.38|Luke 1:38]]).

For more about Matthew and Luke, see Guide to the Scriptures, “[[GS Matthew|Matthew]],” “[[GS Luke|Luke]],” Gospel Library.

### I can remain faithful to God even when my righteous desires are unfulfilled.

If you’ve been waiting for a blessing for a long time, the experience of Elisabeth and Zacharias may be meaningful to you. Try to see yourself in [[Luke 1.5|Luke 1:5–25]][[Luke 1.6|]][[Luke 1.7|]][[Luke 1.8|]][[Luke 1.9|]][[Luke 1.10|]][[Luke 1.11|]][[Luke 1.12|]][[Luke 1.13|]][[Luke 1.14|]][[Luke 1.15|]][[Luke 1.16|]][[Luke 1.17|]][[Luke 1.18|]][[Luke 1.19|]][[Luke 1.20|]][[Luke 1.21|]][[Luke 1.22|]][[Luke 1.23|]][[Luke 1.24|]][[Luke 1.25|]].

- What do you learn from Elisabeth and Zacharias about waiting for a blessing? What evidence do you find that they remained faithful to God despite this challenge?

- Can you think of other examples of people who remained faithful even though their righteous desires seemed distant?

What message does the Lord have for you in the following statement by President Jeffrey R. Holland? “While we work and wait together for the answers to some of our prayers, I offer you my apostolic promise that they are heard and they are answered, though perhaps not at the time or in the way we wanted. But they are *always* answered at the time and in the way an omniscient and eternally compassionate parent should answer them” (“[[2020 October - Waiting on the Lord.md|Waiting on the Lord]],” *Ensign* or *Liahona*, Nov. 2020, 115–16).

- What do you feel the Lord expects of you while you wait for the blessing you hope for?

- What impresses you about Elisabeth’s and Zacharias’s expressions of faith when their righteous desires were finally fulfilled? (see [[Luke 1.57|Luke 1:57–80]][[Luke 1.58|]][[Luke 1.59|]][[Luke 1.60|]][[Luke 1.61|]][[Luke 1.62|]][[Luke 1.63|]][[Luke 1.64|]][[Luke 1.65|]][[Luke 1.66|]][[Luke 1.67|]][[Luke 1.68|]][[Luke 1.69|]][[Luke 1.70|]][[Luke 1.71|]][[Luke 1.72|]][[Luke 1.73|]][[Luke 1.74|]][[Luke 1.75|]][[Luke 1.76|]][[Luke 1.77|]][[Luke 1.78|]][[Luke 1.79|]][[Luke 1.80|]]). ### I can accept the Lord’s will with trust in Him.

Mary is revered as the mother of Jesus Christ, the Son of God. Long before she was born, prophets spoke about her life and mission (see [[1 Nephi 11.13|1 Nephi 11:13–20]][[1 Nephi 11.14|]][[1 Nephi 11.15|]][[1 Nephi 11.16|]][[1 Nephi 11.17|]][[1 Nephi 11.18|]][[1 Nephi 11.19|]][[1 Nephi 11.20|]]; [[Mosiah 3.8|Mosiah 3:8]]; [[Alma 7.10|Alma 7:10]]).

- What insights do you gain about Mary from her conversation with the angel in [[Luke 1.27|Luke 1:27–38]][[Luke 1.28|]][[Luke 1.29|]][[Luke 1.30|]][[Luke 1.31|]][[Luke 1.32|]][[Luke 1.33|]][[Luke 1.34|]][[Luke 1.35|]][[Luke 1.36|]][[Luke 1.37|]][[Luke 1.38|]]? - What words from the angel help you know that the Lord is aware of your life circumstances?

- What do you learn about Him from her words?

- What “great things” has the Lord done for you?

- What might it mean for your soul to “magnify the Lord”?

- When the angel said, “With God nothing shall be impossible” ([[Luke 1.37|Luke 1:37]]), how might these words have helped Mary? How do they help you?

### With God nothing is impossible.

- To help your children build their faith that God can do things that seem impossible, you could help them look for the impossible things He did for Elisabeth and Mary, as described in [[Luke 1.27|Luke 1:27–37]][[Luke 1.28|]][[Luke 1.29|]][[Luke 1.30|]][[Luke 1.31|]][[Luke 1.32|]][[Luke 1.33|]][[Luke 1.34|]][[Luke 1.35|]][[Luke 1.36|]][[Luke 1.37|]]. ### Jesus Christ is the Son of Heavenly Father and Mary.

- Do your children know who your parents are? Maybe you could show pictures and talk about similarities between you and your parents (or you and your children). Then you could read [[Luke 1.30|Luke 1:30–35]][[Luke 1.31|]][[Luke 1.32|]][[Luke 1.33|]][[Luke 1.34|]][[Luke 1.35|]] to learn about Jesus’s parents, Mary and Heavenly Father. You could talk about how being Mary’s son meant Jesus could have human experiences like the rest of us and being Heavenly Father’s Son meant He could overcome death.
