# For Primary—Instructions for Singing Time and the Annual Children’s Sacrament Meeting Presentation

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/55-appendix-c?lang=eng)

Primary music is a powerful way to help children learn about the gospel of Jesus Christ. These sacred songs are a vital part of children’s spiritual foundation, and the words and music will stay in their hearts and minds throughout their lives. As Primary presidencies and music leaders follow the Spirit’s guidance and testify of the gospel truths in sacred music, they will bless, strengthen, and guide the lives of their Primary children and help them feel the Savior’s love.

## Instructions for Singing Time

*5 minutes (Primary presidency): Opening prayer, scripture or article of faith, and one talk, given by children*

*20 minutes (music leader): Singing time*

The Primary presidency and music leader select songs for each month to reinforce principles the children are learning in their classes and at home. A list of songs that reinforce these principles is included below.

As you teach songs to the children, invite them to share what they have already learned about the stories and doctrinal principles that the songs teach. Invite the children to share their thoughts and feelings about the truths found in the songs.

The *Children’s Songbook* is the basic resource for music in Primary. Hymns from the hymnbook and songs from the *Friend* are also appropriate. You can also use songs from *Hymns—For Home and Church*. The use of any other music in Primary must be approved by the bishopric (see *General Handbook: Serving in The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints*, [12.3.4](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/general-handbook/12-primary?lang=eng&id=title_number19-p145#title_number19), Gospel Library).

## Using Music to Teach Doctrine

Singing time can help your children feel the influence of the Holy Ghost as they learn gospel truths. The following ideas can help with this:

*Fill in the blank*. On the board, you could write a verse of the song you are teaching and leave out several key words. Then ask the children to sing the song while listening for words that fill in the blanks. Afterward, you might invite your children to fill in each blank with a missing word. What gospel principles do these words teach us?

*Use quotations from Church leaders*. Perhaps you could invite the children to listen to a quotation from a Church leader that teaches the same gospel principle as the song they are learning. You might ask the children to raise their hands when they hear something that helps them understand that gospel principle. Ask them to share what they heard.

*Testify*. You can help strengthen your children’s faith by testifying of the gospel truths found in the Primary songs. You can also help your children understand that singing is one way they can bear testimony of Jesus Christ and feel the Spirit.

*Use pictures*. You could invite the children to draw pictures about a gospel principle that the song teaches and talk about the principles before singing. Or you could invite the children to participate in an activity or game that uses pictures. For example, if you are teaching “[I Will Follow God’s Plan](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/i-will-follow-gods-plan?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 164–65), you could put pictures around the room that depict words from the song (such as *gift*, *heaven*, *home*, *earth*, and *birth*). Then you could ask the children to gather the pictures. Ask them to hold their pictures up in the correct order as you sing the song together.

*Share an object*. You could use an object that relates to a song. For example, the song “[Faith](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/faith?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 96–97) mentions a little seed. You could show the children a seed to help them understand that a small amount of faith in Jesus Christ can grow over time.

*Connect the song with personal experiences*. You could help the children connect the principles taught in a Primary song with experiences they have had with those principles. For example, before singing “[I Love to See the Temple](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/i-love-to-see-the-temple?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 95), you could ask the children to raise their hands if they have seen or been to a temple. You might then invite them to talk about what the temple looks like and how they feel when they see one.

*Ask questions*. There are many questions you can ask as you sing songs. For example, you can ask the children what they learn from each verse in the song. You can also ask them to think of questions that the song answers.

*Listen for answers*. You might ask the children to listen for answers to questions such as “how?” “what?” “where?” “when?” or “why?” in the songs you teach. For example, in the song “[Baptism](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/baptism?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 100–101), they could listen for who baptized Jesus and where, when, how, and why He was baptized. You could also ask the children to listen for key words or to count on their fingers how many times they sing a certain word.

*Use hand actions*. To help the children remember the words and messages of a song, you might invite them to think of simple hand actions they can do while singing. For example, you might ask the children what actions they could do when singing “[Reverently, Quietly](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/reverently-quietly?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 26). Based on their ideas, you could decide together to fold your arms, bow your heads, or touch your hearts when singing the relevant lyrics.

## Guidelines for the Children’s Sacrament Meeting Presentation

Each year, the Primary children have the opportunity to give a special sacrament meeting presentation. This presentation can help children share their testimonies of the Savior with your ward through music, talks, stories, and scriptures. It can also uplift listeners and help them come closer to Jesus Christ.

The children’s sacrament meeting presentation is planned by the Primary presidency and music leader in collaboration with the bishopric counselor who oversees the Primary under the bishop’s direction. It is recommended that these leaders meet at the beginning of the year to start planning the presentation, which is normally held during the fourth quarter of the year. Primary leaders can complete the plans for the program and seek the counselor’s approval.

This annual presentation gives children the opportunity to present what they and their families have learned from the New Testament at home and in Primary. Prayerfully consider which gospel principles and Primary songs best support what the children have learned. Throughout the year, you might want to keep notes of children’s talks and personal experiences to possibly use in the presentation. You can invite the children to share these or other messages, in their own words, in the program. As you plan, consider how the presentation might teach the congregation about Heavenly Father, Jesus Christ, and Their gospel.

If your unit has a small number of children, you might consider how family members can participate in the presentation with their children. If desired, a member of the bishopric may conclude the meeting with brief remarks.

As you prepare the presentation, remember the following guidelines:

- Practices for the presentation should not take unnecessary time away from Primary classes or families.

- Visuals, costumes, and media presentations are not appropriate for sacrament meeting.
