# August 9–15. “Overcome Evil with Good”: Romans 7–16

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/33?lang=eng)

When he opened his epistle to the Romans, Paul told Church members they were “beloved of God, called to be saints” ([[Romans 1.7|Romans 1:7]]). Even though Paul wrote to correct many false ideas and flawed behaviors, it seems he also wanted to assure these new Christian converts that they were Saints whom God loved. His tender counsel blesses us when we struggle to feel God’s love or when being called a Saint feels out of reach. With humble empathy, Paul acknowledged that he too sometimes felt like a “wretched man” ([[Romans 7.24|Romans 7:24]]), but Jesus Christ had given him power to overcome sin. The Savior offers that same power to you so that you can “overcome evil with good” ([[Romans 12.21|Romans 12:21]]).

### Jesus Christ can help me “walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Have you ever felt frustrated because, even though you want to obey the commandments, you continue to sin? Paul felt that way. He compared it to a war within himself, and it made him feel “wretched” ([[Romans 7.24|Romans 7:24]]). Look for phrases that describe that inner conflict in [[Romans 7.14|Romans 7:14–25]][[Romans 7.15|]][[Romans 7.16|]][[Romans 7.17|]][[Romans 7.18|]][[Romans 7.19|]][[Romans 7.20|]][[Romans 7.21|]][[Romans 7.22|]][[Romans 7.23|]][[Romans 7.24|]][[Romans 7.25|]]. You might also think about ways you’ve experienced it in your own life. Then, while reading [[Romans 8.1|Romans 8:1–14]][[Romans 8.2|]][[Romans 8.3|]][[Romans 8.4|]][[Romans 8.5|]][[Romans 8.6|]][[Romans 8.7|]][[Romans 8.8|]][[Romans 8.9|]][[Romans 8.10|]][[Romans 8.11|]][[Romans 8.12|]][[Romans 8.13|]][[Romans 8.14|]], ponder how Jesus Christ resolves this war between your spiritual desires and your physical weakness. How has He helped you overcome the “sin that dwelleth in \[you\]” so that “the Spirit of God \[can\] dwell in you”? ([[Romans 7.20|Romans 7:20]]; [[Romans 8.9|8:9]]).

### As covenant children of God, we can become “joint-heirs with Christ.”

When you feel like “the sufferings of this present time” are heavy—or you know someone who does—consider reading [[Romans 8.16|Romans 8:16–28]][[Romans 8.17|]][[Romans 8.18|]][[Romans 8.19|]][[Romans 8.20|]][[Romans 8.21|]][[Romans 8.22|]][[Romans 8.23|]][[Romans 8.24|]][[Romans 8.25|]][[Romans 8.26|]][[Romans 8.27|]][[Romans 8.28|]].

- What do you find there that affects how you think about suffering?

- What does it mean to you to be a joint-heir with Christ?

- Which aspects of God’s plan give you hope and patience in your trials? (see [[Romans 8.18|verses 18, 22–25]][[Romans 8.22|]][[Romans 8.23|]][[Romans 8.24|]][[Romans 8.25|]]).

- What experiences have taught you that “all things work together for good to them that love God”? ([[Romans 8.28|verse 28]]).

- What do God’s promises teach you about who He is and how He feels about you?

See also Dieter F. Uchtdorf, “[[2021 April - God among Us.md|God among Us]],” *Liahona*, May 2021, 8–10.

### “Who shall separate us from the love of Christ?”

As you read [[Romans 8.31|Romans 8:31–39]][[Romans 8.32|]][[Romans 8.33|]][[Romans 8.34|]][[Romans 8.35|]][[Romans 8.36|]][[Romans 8.37|]][[Romans 8.38|]][[Romans 8.39|]], think of a time when the Savior helped you during a trial or challenge. You might consider replacing some of the words in [[Romans 8.35|verses 35]] and [[Romans 8.38|38–39]][[Romans 8.39|]] with words representing forces or trials in your life that have tried to separate you from God’s love. How did He make His love known to you, even when you were experiencing adversity? How did your adversity help you turn to Him?

### I can become a true Saint and follower of Jesus Christ.

The last five chapters of Romans contain instructions about living as Saints. You could make a list of some of those instructions as you read [[Romans 12.1|Romans 12–16]][[Romans 12.2|]][[Romans 12.3|]][[Romans 12.4|]][[Romans 12.5|]][[Romans 12.6|]][[Romans 12.7|]][[Romans 12.8|]][[Romans 12.9|]][[Romans 12.10|]][[Romans 12.11|]][[Romans 12.12|]][[Romans 12.13|]][[Romans 12.14|]][[Romans 12.15|]][[Romans 12.16|]][[Romans 12.17|]][[Romans 12.18|]][[Romans 12.19|]][[Romans 12.20|]][[Romans 12.21|]] and choose one or two principles to remember (for example, [[Romans 12.10|Romans 12:10–21]][[Romans 12.11|]][[Romans 12.12|]][[Romans 12.13|]][[Romans 12.14|]][[Romans 12.15|]][[Romans 12.16|]][[Romans 12.17|]][[Romans 12.18|]][[Romans 12.19|]][[Romans 12.20|]][[Romans 12.21|]]; [[Romans 14.10|14:10–13]][[Romans 14.11|]][[Romans 14.12|]][[Romans 14.13|]]; [[Romans 15.1|15:1–7]][[Romans 15.2|]][[Romans 15.3|]][[Romans 15.4|]][[Romans 15.5|]][[Romans 15.6|]][[Romans 15.7|]]). What could you do to apply these principles in your life? How could Heavenly Father help guide your efforts?

[[Romans 13.12|Romans 13:12–14]][[Romans 13.13|]][[Romans 13.14|]]
### “Put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ.”

There are different ways we can, as Paul said, “put … on the Lord Jesus Christ.” One way is through baptism. Another is by partaking of the sacrament. We also symbolically put on Jesus Christ when we wear the temple garment, or the garment of the holy priesthood. As you read [[Romans 13.12|Romans 13:12–14]][[Romans 13.13|]][[Romans 13.14|]], consider how putting on the Lord Jesus Christ has blessed you. Questions like these might help:

- How does keeping your baptismal and temple covenants bring you strength, protection, and power?

- How is putting on Jesus Christ like putting on an “armour of light”? ([[Romans 13.12|verse 12]]).

- What might you need to “take off” in order to “put on” Christ?

See also J. Anette Dennis, “[[2024 April - Put Ye On the Lord Jesus Christ.md|Put Ye On the Lord Jesus Christ]],” *Liahona*, May 2024, 10–13.

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[Romans 8.16|Romans 8:16–18]][[Romans 8.17|]][[Romans 8.18|]]
### Heavenly Father wants me to receive everything that He has because I am His child.

- You could read [[Romans 8.16|Romans 8:16–18]][[Romans 8.17|]][[Romans 8.18|]] with your children and explain that an “heir” is someone who receives, or inherits, what their parents have. What does Heavenly Father have that He could give us? To emphasize that Heavenly Father is our King, and He wants us to receive His kingdom one day, you and your children could make paper crowns to wear. How can we show Heavenly Father that we want to be His heirs?

- To teach your children that Heavenly Father created a plan so we can live with Him again and receive everything He has, you could sing “[I Will Follow God’s Plan](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/i-will-follow-gods-plan?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 164–65). What does this song teach about how we can prepare to receive God’s blessings?

[[Romans 8.35|Romans 8:35–39]][[Romans 8.36|]][[Romans 8.37|]][[Romans 8.38|]][[Romans 8.39|]]
### Nothing can separate me from God’s love.

- To teach your children about God’s love, you could invite them to draw a picture of the sun. Then you could talk about how the sun is like Heavenly Father’s love, using President Dieter F. Uchtdorf’s words in “[[2017 October - Bearers of Heavenly Light.md|A Metaphor for Spiritual Darkness]]” (in his message “[[2017 October - Bearers of Heavenly Light.md|Bearers of Heavenly Light]],” *Ensign* or *Liahona*, Nov. 2017, 79–80). You and your children could then share experiences when you knew that God’s love was there for you, even though you couldn’t see Him.

> **Let your children express their creativity.** “When you invite children to draw, build, color, or write something related to a gospel principle, you help them better understand the principle, and you give them a tangible reminder of what they have learned” (*Teaching in the Savior’s Way*, 32). For example, their drawings of the sun (suggested above) could be a reminder that nothing can separate them from God’s love.


- As you read together [[Romans 8.35|Romans 8:35–39]][[Romans 8.36|]][[Romans 8.37|]][[Romans 8.38|]][[Romans 8.39|]], you and your children could think of examples of things that are hard to separate. Maybe they could even look for things around the room or the house that are inseparable. Then you could share with each other things that help you feel God’s love.

[[Romans 10.17|Romans 10:17]]
### Faith comes by hearing the word of God.

- After reading [[Romans 10.17|Romans 10:17]] together, you could hide a set of scriptures, a picture of the President of the Church, and a copy of the [Friend](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/magazines/friend?lang=eng) magazine and invite your children to look for these items. Then you could talk about how these things help us hear God’s word and build our faith.

[[Romans 14.10|Romans 14:10, 13]][[Romans 14.13|]]
### “Let us not … judge one another.”

- In [[Romans 14.10|Romans 14:10, 13]][[Romans 14.13|]], your children could count how many times Paul used the word *judge*. What does it mean to judge someone? Why should we be careful about judging others? To illustrate this, you could show a picture of a boy or girl and ask your children to list some things we don’t know about this person just by looking at them. This could lead to a discussion about why the Lord should be our judge (see [[1 Samuel 16.7|1 Samuel 16:7]]).
