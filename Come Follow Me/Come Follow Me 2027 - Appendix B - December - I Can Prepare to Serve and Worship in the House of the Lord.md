# December: I Can Prepare to Serve and Worship in the House of the Lord

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/54-appendix-b/012-temple?lang=eng)

It might be a few years before the children in your class are old enough to enter the temple. And it might be even longer before they’re ready to make temple covenants. But it’s not too early for them to develop a love for the Lord’s holy house. They can learn now that the temple and everything we do there point us to Jesus Christ. They can plan now to make sacred temple covenants with Him someday. And they can prepare now to keep those covenants throughout their lives.

See also Topics and Questions, “[Temples](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/gospel-topics/temples-study-guide?lang=eng),” Gospel Library.

## The temple is the house of the Lord.

- You might start a conversation about the house of the Lord by telling the children about your own house. How would a visitor to your house know it is yours? You might ask the children a similar question about their homes. Then you could show the children some pictures of temples and ask them how we know that the temple is the house of the Lord. For example, you might point out that each temple has this inscription: “Holiness to the Lord—The House of the Lord.” Help the children ponder what that might mean by asking questions like these:

If you or any of the children have been to a temple, share with each other how you felt. How did you know it was the Lord’s holy house? (See “[I Love to See the Temple](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/i-love-to-see-the-temple?lang=eng),” *Children’s Songbook*, 95.)

## In the temple, we can be baptized and confirmed for ancestors who have died.

- To teach the children why baptism for the dead is needed, you could help them think of things that little children can’t do for themselves (such as drive a car or cook a meal). Who helps them? This could lead to a conversation about ancestors who need our help because they died without being baptized. Then you could read [[1 Corinthians 15.29|1 Corinthians 15:29]] to find out how we can help them.

- Maybe the children could draw a tree on the board, including the roots and branches. Label the roots *Parents and Grandparents*, label the branches *Children and Grandchildren*, and label the trunk *You*. Consider sharing a picture of a loved one who died without receiving the restored gospel of Jesus Christ. You could show how they’re connected to you on your family tree and explain how you can take their name to the temple so they can be baptized and confirmed (see [familysearch.org/temple/ordinances-ready](https://www.familysearch.org/en/church/ordinances-ready)).

- You might invite one or more youth who have been baptized for their ancestors to share what their experience was like. How did doing this work for their ancestors make them feel?

## In the temple, families can be sealed for eternity.

- In the Lord’s house, a husband and wife and their children can be united for eternity through the sealing ordinance. “[Eternal Marriage and Families](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/doctrine-and-covenants-stories-2025/42-eternal-marriage-and-families?lang=eng)” (in *Doctrine and Covenants Stories*, 165–67) can help you teach the children about the temple sealing ordinance. You could also sing together “[Families Can Be Together Forever](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/families-can-be-together-forever?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 188–89). Maybe the children could draw pictures of their families and share with each other what they love about each family member. This could lead to a conversation about why Heavenly Father wants families to be eternal.> 2:9
