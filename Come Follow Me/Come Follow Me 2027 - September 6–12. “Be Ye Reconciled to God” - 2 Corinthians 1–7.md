# September 6–12. “Be Ye Reconciled to God”: 2 Corinthians 1–7

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/37?lang=eng)

Even when our intentions are good—when we want to live holy, Christlike lives—we all fall short. When that happens, it’s tempting to throw up our hands and say things like “That’s just the way I am!” or “I guess I’m not good enough”—or even “I’m just being authentic to my true self.” Of course, the Savior loves honesty and authenticity. But He also loves growth, and He loves you. He sees not just who you are but also who you can become. Yes, in this mortal life “we are absent from the Lord” ([[2 Corinthians 5.6|2 Corinthians 5:6]]), but Christ came to close that gap, to reconcile us to God. In that reconciliation, it’s not God who must change—it’s us. Paul called it becoming “a new creature” in Christ ([[2 Corinthians 5.17|2 Corinthians 5:17]]). But in truth, this new creature is the real, authentic you—the one who chose to follow Christ before you were even born (see [[Revelation 12.7|Revelation 12:7–11]][[Revelation 12.8|]][[Revelation 12.9|]][[Revelation 12.10|]][[Revelation 12.11|]]). And one day, when we see His glory, we will also see that we “are changed into the same image from glory to glory” ([[2 Corinthians 3.18|2 Corinthians 3:18]]).

## Ideas for Learning at Home and at Church

[[2 Corinthians 1.3|2 Corinthians 1:3–5]][[2 Corinthians 1.4|]][[2 Corinthians 1.5|]]; [[2 Corinthians 4.6|4:6–10, 17–18]][[2 Corinthians 4.7|]][[2 Corinthians 4.8|]][[2 Corinthians 4.9|]][[2 Corinthians 4.10|]][[2 Corinthians 4.17|]][[2 Corinthians 4.18|]]; [[2 Corinthians 7.4|7:4–7]][[2 Corinthians 7.5|]][[2 Corinthians 7.6|]][[2 Corinthians 7.7|]]
### Because of Jesus Christ, I can have hope during my trials.

The scriptural record of Paul’s life confirms that he was “troubled on every side,” “persecuted,” and “cast down.” So, after all he had suffered, why was he also “not distressed” and “not in despair” but instead “filled with comfort”? ([[2 Corinthians 4.8|2 Corinthians 4:8–9]][[2 Corinthians 4.9|]]; [[2 Corinthians 7.4|7:4]]). As you read [[2 Corinthians 1.3|2 Corinthians 1:3–5]][[2 Corinthians 1.4|]][[2 Corinthians 1.5|]]; [[2 Corinthians 4.6|4:6–10, 17–18]][[2 Corinthians 4.7|]][[2 Corinthians 4.8|]][[2 Corinthians 4.9|]][[2 Corinthians 4.10|]][[2 Corinthians 4.17|]][[2 Corinthians 4.18|]]; and [[2 Corinthians 7.4|7:4–7]][[2 Corinthians 7.5|]][[2 Corinthians 7.6|]][[2 Corinthians 7.7|]], ponder what helped Paul remain hopeful during his trials.

- What do you learn from his experiences that might help you find comfort in your challenges?

- How do you feel inspired to comfort others during their times of need?

See also Dale G. Renlund, “[[2021 April - Infuriating Unfairness.md|Infuriating Unfairness]],” *Liahona*, May 2021, 41–45; “[It Is Well with My Soul](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/music/hymns-for-home-and-church/it-is-well-with-my-soul?lang=eng),” *Hymns—For Home and Church*, Gospel Library.

[[2 Corinthians 2.5|2 Corinthians 2:5–11]][[2 Corinthians 2.6|]][[2 Corinthians 2.7|]][[2 Corinthians 2.8|]][[2 Corinthians 2.9|]][[2 Corinthians 2.10|]][[2 Corinthians 2.11|]]
### Forgiveness is a gift I can both give and receive.

We don’t know much about the man Paul referred to in [[2 Corinthians 2.5|2 Corinthians 2:5–11]][[2 Corinthians 2.6|]][[2 Corinthians 2.7|]][[2 Corinthians 2.8|]][[2 Corinthians 2.9|]][[2 Corinthians 2.10|]][[2 Corinthians 2.11|]]—only that he had transgressed (see [[2 Corinthians 2.5|verses 5–6]][[2 Corinthians 2.6|]]) and that Paul wanted the Saints to forgive him (see [[2 Corinthians 2.7|verses 7–8]][[2 Corinthians 2.8|]]). As you read [[2 Corinthians 2.5|2 Corinthians 2:5–11]][[2 Corinthians 2.6|]][[2 Corinthians 2.7|]][[2 Corinthians 2.8|]][[2 Corinthians 2.9|]][[2 Corinthians 2.10|]][[2 Corinthians 2.11|]], look for counsel from Paul about how we should treat someone who has offended us. Questions like these might help:

- Why is it important to forgive others? (see [[2 Corinthians 2.7|verses 7, 10–11]][[2 Corinthians 2.10|]][[2 Corinthians 2.11|]]).

- How have you been blessed by giving or receiving forgiveness?

- Who needs forgiveness from you? How can you “confirm your love” to them? (see [[2 Corinthians 2.8|verse 8]]).

See also Topics and Questions, “[Forgiveness](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/gospel-topics/forgiveness-study-guide?lang=eng),” Gospel Library.

[[2 Corinthians 5.6|2 Corinthians 5:6–8]][[2 Corinthians 5.7|]][[2 Corinthians 5.8|]]
### I walk by faith.

Paul declared, “We are confident,” even though “we walk by faith, not by sight” ([[2 Corinthians 5.7|2 Corinthians 5:7–8]][[2 Corinthians 5.8|]]). How is it possible to walk confidently when we can’t see?

Consider reading Elder Ulisses Soares’s message “[[2024 April - Covenant Confidence through Jesus Christ.md|Covenant Confidence through Jesus Christ]]” (*Liahona*, May 2024, 17–21). Look for things that can help you walk confidently in Christ, even though you don’t walk “by sight.”

You might also ponder this teaching by President Emily Belle Freeman: “The Lord will answer each of us in our day of distress if we choose to tether our life with His. He has promised to walk *with us* in the way” (“[[2023 October - Walking in Covenant Relationship with Christ.md|Walking in Covenant Relationship with Christ]],” *Liahona*, Nov. 2023, 77).

- How have you noticed the Lord helping you when you walk by faith?

- How are you striving to walk with the Savior through your trials?

[[2 Corinthians 5.14|2 Corinthians 5:14–21]][[2 Corinthians 5.15|]][[2 Corinthians 5.16|]][[2 Corinthians 5.17|]][[2 Corinthians 5.18|]][[2 Corinthians 5.19|]][[2 Corinthians 5.20|]][[2 Corinthians 5.21|]]
### Through the Atonement of Jesus Christ, I can be reconciled to God.

As you read [[2 Corinthians 5.14|2 Corinthians 5:14–21]][[2 Corinthians 5.15|]][[2 Corinthians 5.16|]][[2 Corinthians 5.17|]][[2 Corinthians 5.18|]][[2 Corinthians 5.19|]][[2 Corinthians 5.20|]][[2 Corinthians 5.21|]], think about what it means to be reconciled to God.

- How are you becoming “a new creature” by being reconciled to God? ([[2 Corinthians 5.17|verse 17]]). In what ways have you seen this process in others?

- How has Christ invited you to participate in “the ministry of reconciliation”? ([[2 Corinthians 5.18|verse 18]]).

- In what ways can you be an “\[ambassador\] for Christ”? ([[2 Corinthians 5.20|verse 20]]).

[[2 Corinthians 7.8|2 Corinthians 7:8–11]][[2 Corinthians 7.9|]][[2 Corinthians 7.10|]][[2 Corinthians 7.11|]]
### Godly sorrow leads to true repentance.

We don’t usually think of sorrow as a good thing. But it can be when it is “godly sorrow” and it leads us to true repentance ([[2 Corinthians 7.10|2 Corinthians 7:10]]; see also [[D&C 59.8|Doctrine and Covenants 59:8]]). Read the following scriptures, and ponder what you learn from them about godly sorrow: [[2 Corinthians 7.8|2 Corinthians 7:8–11]][[2 Corinthians 7.9|]][[2 Corinthians 7.10|]][[2 Corinthians 7.11|]]; [[Alma 36.16|Alma 36:16–21]][[Alma 36.17|]][[Alma 36.18|]][[Alma 36.19|]][[Alma 36.20|]][[Alma 36.21|]]; [[Mormon 2.11|Mormon 2:11–15]][[Mormon 2.12|]][[Mormon 2.13|]][[Mormon 2.14|]][[Mormon 2.15|]].

- Why is godly sorrow necessary to receive the blessings of Jesus Christ’s Atonement?

- How is godly sorrow different from embarrassment or shame?

- How has godly sorrow helped you find joy?

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[2 Corinthians 1.3|2 Corinthians 1:3–4]][[2 Corinthians 1.4|]]
### God comforts me, and I can comfort others.

- To introduce Paul’s teachings in [[2 Corinthians 1.1|2 Corinthians 1]][[2 Corinthians 1.2|]][[2 Corinthians 1.3|]][[2 Corinthians 1.4|]][[2 Corinthians 1.5|]][[2 Corinthians 1.6|]][[2 Corinthians 1.7|]][[2 Corinthians 1.8|]][[2 Corinthians 1.9|]][[2 Corinthians 1.10|]][[2 Corinthians 1.11|]][[2 Corinthians 1.12|]][[2 Corinthians 1.13|]][[2 Corinthians 1.14|]][[2 Corinthians 1.15|]][[2 Corinthians 1.16|]][[2 Corinthians 1.17|]][[2 Corinthians 1.18|]][[2 Corinthians 1.19|]][[2 Corinthians 1.20|]][[2 Corinthians 1.21|]][[2 Corinthians 1.22|]][[2 Corinthians 1.23|]][[2 Corinthians 1.24|]], you could show your children things that provide comfort, like a blanket or bandage. Maybe they could think of other examples and talk about how these things make them feel. Then you could read [[2 Corinthians 1.3|2 Corinthians 1:3–4]][[2 Corinthians 1.4|]] together (explain that “tribulation” is another word for hard problems). Share with each other how Heavenly Father has comforted you during your hard times.

> **Share object lessons.** Some gospel concepts can be difficult to understand. Consider using pictures or objects—like things that provide comfort in the activity above—to help your children understand the principles you discover in the scriptures. (See *Teaching in the Savior’s Way*, [32](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/teaching-in-the-saviors-way-2022/12-part-3/13-suggestions-for-a-variety-of-teaching?lang=eng&id=p12#p12)).


- After reading [[2 Corinthians 1.4|2 Corinthians 1:4]], you and your children could list ways God comforts us. Perhaps your children could think of someone they know who is “in any trouble.” How can we comfort them with the comfort God has given us? The song “[I Feel My Savior’s Love](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/i-feel-my-saviors-love?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 74–75), especially the last verse, can help you teach this principle.

[[2 Corinthians 2.7|2 Corinthians 2:7–8, 10]][[2 Corinthians 2.8|]][[2 Corinthians 2.10|]]
### I can forgive others.

- It might help to explain that in [[2 Corinthians 2.1|2 Corinthians 2]][[2 Corinthians 2.2|]][[2 Corinthians 2.3|]][[2 Corinthians 2.4|]][[2 Corinthians 2.5|]][[2 Corinthians 2.6|]][[2 Corinthians 2.7|]][[2 Corinthians 2.8|]][[2 Corinthians 2.9|]][[2 Corinthians 2.10|]][[2 Corinthians 2.11|]][[2 Corinthians 2.12|]][[2 Corinthians 2.13|]][[2 Corinthians 2.14|]][[2 Corinthians 2.15|]][[2 Corinthians 2.16|]][[2 Corinthians 2.17|]], Paul wanted the Corinthian Saints to forgive a man who had sinned. After reading [[2 Corinthians 2.7|verses 7–8 and 10]][[2 Corinthians 2.8|]][[2 Corinthians 2.10|]] together, your children could role-play ways to respond when someone does something unkind. Let them take turns saying, “I forgive you.” How do we help people know that we forgive them? (see [[2 Corinthians 2.8|verse 8]]). Why is it important for us to forgive others? The video “The Goal: A Story of Faith, Friendship and Forgiveness” (Gospel Library) provides a helpful example.

[[2 Corinthians 5.6|2 Corinthians 5:6–7]][[2 Corinthians 5.7|]]
### I “walk by faith, not by sight.”

- After reading [[2 Corinthians 5.6|2 Corinthians 5:6–7]][[2 Corinthians 5.7|]], talk about what it means to “walk by faith.” To illustrate this principle, you could invite your children to close their eyes and walk across the room, following your directions. How does the Savior help us walk by faith? (See Carl B. Cook, “[[2023 April - Just Keep Going—with Faith.md|Just Keep Going—with Faith]],” *Liahona*, May 2023, 13–15.)

- Consider showing pictures of scripture stories in which people walked by faith. Examples could include Moses parting the Red Sea, Nephi building a boat, or someone coming to the Savior to be healed. Or maybe you can share examples from your own lives. What do we learn about walking by faith from these stories?

[[2 Corinthians 5.17|2 Corinthians 5:17]]
### Jesus Christ can help me change.

- To introduce Paul’s teachings in [[2 Corinthians 5.17|2 Corinthians 5:17]], perhaps you and your children could think of examples of God’s creations that change in amazing ways and become like “new creatures”—such as caterpillars becoming butterflies or seeds becoming trees. Maybe you can find pictures of these things to look at. Use these examples to share your testimony that Jesus Christ can help us change and improve.
