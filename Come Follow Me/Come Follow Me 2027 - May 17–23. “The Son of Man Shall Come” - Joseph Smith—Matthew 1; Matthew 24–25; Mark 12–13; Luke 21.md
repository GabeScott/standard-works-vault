# May 17–23. “The Son of Man Shall Come”: Joseph Smith—Matthew 1; Matthew 24–25; Mark 12–13; Luke 21

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/21?lang=eng)

Jesus’s disciples must have found His prophecy startling: The mighty temple of Jerusalem, the spiritual and cultural center of the Jewish people, would be destroyed so completely that “there \[would\] not be left … one stone upon another.” Naturally, the disciples wanted to know more. “When shall these things be?” they asked. “And what is the sign of thy coming?” (See [[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.2|Joseph Smith—Matthew 1:2–4]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.3|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.4|]].) The Savior’s answers revealed that the great destruction coming to Jerusalem—a prophecy fulfilled in AD 70 (see [[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.5|verses 5–20]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.6|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.7|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.8|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.9|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.10|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.11|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.12|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.13|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.14|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.15|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.16|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.17|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.18|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.19|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.20|]])—would be small compared to the signs of His coming in the last days (see [[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.21|verses 21–37]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.22|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.23|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.24|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.25|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.26|]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.27|]]). Things that seem even more stable than the temple in Jerusalem will prove to be temporary—the sun, the moon, the stars, the nations, and the sea. Even “the powers of heaven shall be shaken” ([[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.33|verse 33]]). But if we are willing, all this commotion can teach us to put our trust in something truly permanent. Jesus promised: “Heaven and earth shall pass away; yet my words shall not pass away. … And whoso treasureth up my word, shall not be deceived” ([[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.35|verses 35, 37]][[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.37|]]).

### I can look forward to the Savior’s Second Coming with faith and hope.

Reading about the events leading up to the Savior’s Second Coming can be troubling—and yet Jesus said to His disciples, “Be not troubled” ([[Joseph Smith Matthew 1.23|Joseph Smith—Matthew 1:23]]). - What reasons do you find to be hopeful in these verses?

### Heavenly Father expects me to use His gifts wisely.

In the Savior’s time, a “talent” referred to money, but the parable of the talents has much deeper meaning. While reading [[Matthew 25.14|Matthew 25:14–30]][[Matthew 25.15|]][[Matthew 25.16|]][[Matthew 25.17|]][[Matthew 25.18|]][[Matthew 25.19|]][[Matthew 25.20|]][[Matthew 25.21|]][[Matthew 25.22|]][[Matthew 25.23|]][[Matthew 25.24|]][[Matthew 25.25|]][[Matthew 25.26|]][[Matthew 25.27|]][[Matthew 25.28|]][[Matthew 25.29|]][[Matthew 25.30|]], you could consider what the Lord has trusted you with. If you have a patriarchal blessing, you might refer to it as well.

- Who could you bless with what God has given you?

- What stops you from using these gifts or blessings the way God intends? What can you do to ensure they are not “buried”?

- Why would magnifying your gifts and blessings help prepare you to “enter … into the joy of thy lord”? ([[Matthew 25.21|verse 21]]).

For additional insights on what a “talent” might mean, study [[Ether 12.35|Ether 12:35]]; [[D&C 60.2|Doctrine and Covenants 60:2, 13]][[D&C 60.13|]].

See also Topics and Questions, “[Gifts of the Spirit](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/gospel-topics/gifts-of-the-spirit-study-guide?lang=eng),” Gospel Library.
