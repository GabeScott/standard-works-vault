# November 29–December 5. “Glory, and Power, Be unto … the Lamb For Ever”: Revelation 1–5

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/49?lang=eng)

Have you ever struggled to tell someone what you felt during a powerful spiritual experience? Everyday language just isn’t enough to describe some spiritual feelings and impressions. Maybe that’s why John included so much symbolism and imagery to describe his majestic revelation. He could have simply said that he saw Jesus Christ, but to help us understand his experience, he described the Savior using words like these: “His eyes were as a flame of fire,” “out of his mouth went a sharp twoedged sword,” and “his countenance was as the sun shineth in his strength” ([[Revelation 1.14|Revelation 1:14]], [[Revelation 1.16|16]]). As you read the book of Revelation, try to discover the messages John wanted you to learn and feel, even if you don’t understand every symbol. Why might he have compared Church congregations to candlesticks, Satan to a dragon, and Jesus Christ to a lamb? Ultimately, you don’t have to understand every symbol in Revelation to understand its themes, including its most important theme: Jesus Christ and His followers will triumph over Satan, sin, and worldliness.

For more about the book of Revelation, see Guide to the Scriptures, “[[GS Revelation of John|Revelation of John]],” Gospel Library; see also “[Thoughts to Keep in Mind: Reading the Book of Revelation](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/48a?lang=eng).”

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[Revelation 1.20|Revelation 1:20]]
### I can shine the Savior’s light.

- A picture of a candlestick could help your children visualize [[Revelation 1.20|Revelation 1:20]], where the Savior compared His Church to a candlestick. You could discuss how we, as members of the Savior’s Church, can be like the light of a candle. Why does the Savior want us to shine His light? You might share how you have seen your children share the Savior’s light with people around them. What are some other ways we can share His light?

[[Revelation 3.15|Revelation 3:15–16]][[Revelation 3.16|]]
### I can be committed to the gospel of Jesus Christ.

- To teach your children what “lukewarm” means in [[Revelation 3.15|Revelation 3:15–16]][[Revelation 3.16|]], you could read these verses while they take turns feeling lukewarm water. Or you could show pictures of food or drinks that are best enjoyed hot or cold. This could lead to a discussion about what it means to be “lukewarm” in our commitment to the Savior and His gospel. You could read together examples of people who were not lukewarm in Elder Dale G. Renlund’s message “[[2019 October - Unwavering Commitment to Jesus Christ.md|Unwavering Commitment to Jesus Christ]]” (*Ensign* or *Liahona*, Nov. 2019, 22–25). Why does the Savior not want us to be lukewarm in our commitment to the gospel? What can we do to be committed to Him?

[[Revelation 3.20|Revelation 3:20]]
### I can invite Jesus Christ into my life.

- To introduce [[Revelation 3.20|Revelation 3:20]], you might ask your children to think about a time when they were excited for someone to visit their home. What was it like waiting for that person to knock on the door? How would that person feel if no one opened it? Then, as you read [[Revelation 3.20|Revelation 3:20]], show your children the picture of the Savior at the door at the end of this week’s outline. How do we let Jesus into our lives? How does having Him in our lives affect the way we live? (see “If the Savior Stood Beside Me,” *Hymns—For Home and Church*, Gospel Library).

- After reading [[Revelation 3.20|Revelation 3:20]] together, your children could take turns knocking on a door. Why does Jesus knock instead of just coming inside? How are we blessed when we invite Him into our lives?
