# Thoughts to Keep in Mind: Reading the Book of Revelation

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/48a?lang=eng)

We call the book “Revelation,” but that’s an incomplete title. The [[Revelation 1.1|first verse]] of the first chapter introduces the book as “the Revelation of Jesus Christ.” And maybe that’s a helpful way to approach this book that—fair or not—has a reputation for being hard to understand. In Revelation, you’ll read about trumpets and vials and plagues and beasts with multiple heads. But that’s not what the book is really about—it’s a revelation about Jesus Christ.

So as you read, try looking for Him.[^1] Let this revelation reveal Him to you. Sometimes His presence is obvious, like in His appearance to John in [[Revelation 1.1|chapter 1]][[Revelation 1.2|]][[Revelation 1.3|]][[Revelation 1.4|]][[Revelation 1.5|]][[Revelation 1.6|]][[Revelation 1.7|]][[Revelation 1.8|]][[Revelation 1.9|]][[Revelation 1.10|]][[Revelation 1.11|]][[Revelation 1.12|]][[Revelation 1.13|]][[Revelation 1.14|]][[Revelation 1.15|]][[Revelation 1.16|]][[Revelation 1.17|]][[Revelation 1.18|]][[Revelation 1.19|]][[Revelation 1.20|]] or the prophecy about His Second Coming in [[Revelation 19.1|chapter 19]][[Revelation 19.2|]][[Revelation 19.3|]][[Revelation 19.4|]][[Revelation 19.5|]][[Revelation 19.6|]][[Revelation 19.7|]][[Revelation 19.8|]][[Revelation 19.9|]][[Revelation 19.10|]][[Revelation 19.11|]][[Revelation 19.12|]][[Revelation 19.13|]][[Revelation 19.14|]][[Revelation 19.15|]][[Revelation 19.16|]][[Revelation 19.17|]][[Revelation 19.18|]][[Revelation 19.19|]][[Revelation 19.20|]][[Revelation 19.21|]]. Other times, you’ll need to look for Him in symbols, like the Lamb introduced in [[Revelation 5.1|chapter 5]][[Revelation 5.2|]][[Revelation 5.3|]][[Revelation 5.4|]][[Revelation 5.5|]][[Revelation 5.6|]][[Revelation 5.7|]][[Revelation 5.8|]][[Revelation 5.9|]][[Revelation 5.10|]][[Revelation 5.11|]][[Revelation 5.12|]][[Revelation 5.13|]][[Revelation 5.14|]] or the tree of life described in [[Revelation 22.1|chapter 22]][[Revelation 22.2|]][[Revelation 22.3|]][[Revelation 22.4|]][[Revelation 22.5|]][[Revelation 22.6|]][[Revelation 22.7|]][[Revelation 22.8|]][[Revelation 22.9|]][[Revelation 22.10|]][[Revelation 22.11|]][[Revelation 22.12|]][[Revelation 22.13|]][[Revelation 22.14|]][[Revelation 22.15|]][[Revelation 22.16|]][[Revelation 22.17|]][[Revelation 22.18|]][[Revelation 22.19|]][[Revelation 22.20|]][[Revelation 22.21|]]. You might notice His titles, like “Faithful and True” or “The Word of God” ([[Revelation 19.11|Revelation 19:11, 13]][[Revelation 19.13|]]). You could see Him reflected in the actions of His devoted followers, like the people in white robes mentioned in [[Revelation 7.1|chapter 7]][[Revelation 7.2|]][[Revelation 7.3|]][[Revelation 7.4|]][[Revelation 7.5|]][[Revelation 7.6|]][[Revelation 7.7|]][[Revelation 7.8|]][[Revelation 7.9|]][[Revelation 7.10|]][[Revelation 7.11|]][[Revelation 7.12|]][[Revelation 7.13|]][[Revelation 7.14|]][[Revelation 7.15|]][[Revelation 7.16|]][[Revelation 7.17|]]. You might even learn some things about the Savior by noticing His *absence*—for example, in the oppression and worldliness in Babylon described in [[Revelation 17.1|chapter 17]][[Revelation 17.2|]][[Revelation 17.3|]][[Revelation 17.4|]][[Revelation 17.5|]][[Revelation 17.6|]][[Revelation 17.7|]][[Revelation 17.8|]][[Revelation 17.9|]][[Revelation 17.10|]][[Revelation 17.11|]][[Revelation 17.12|]][[Revelation 17.13|]][[Revelation 17.14|]][[Revelation 17.15|]][[Revelation 17.16|]][[Revelation 17.17|]][[Revelation 17.18|]].

As you look for the Savior in Revelation, a comparison to appreciating visual art might help. The book of Revelation is a work of art, after all. When you look at a painting, sometimes the best way to understand the artist’s intent is to take in the whole composition, to notice how all the elements work together to create the full masterpiece. While there’s value in zooming in on a detail, it’s important not to lose sight of the whole painting. A similar principle can apply to finding Christ in Revelation. Try not to get lost in the details of John’s symbols and imagery. Rather, take the time to see how those symbols work together to create a larger testimony of Christ and His role in God’s majestic overall plan.

See what you can learn about Jesus Christ’s character in this book. Notice how He interacts with His people—how He gathers them, protects them, cares for them. Ponder what it means to have a *sacrificial Lamb* as our King and Deliverer. What does that suggest about how victory is achieved over sin, persecution, and evil? What does it mean for you as you face the spiritual dangers that the book of Revelation foretells?

In other words, as you read the book of Revelation, let it strengthen your faith in Jesus Christ, your confidence in His power, and your testimony of Him. Remember that John wrote this book in the spirit of prophecy—and the spirit of prophecy is “the testimony of Jesus” ([[Revelation 19.10|Revelation 19:10]]).
