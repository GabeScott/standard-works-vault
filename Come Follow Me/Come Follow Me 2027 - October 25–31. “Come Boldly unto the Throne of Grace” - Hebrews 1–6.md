# October 25–31. “Come Boldly unto the Throne of Grace”: Hebrews 1–6

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/44?lang=eng)

We each have to give up something to accept the gospel of Jesus Christ—bad habits, incorrect beliefs, or something else. For Gentiles in the early Christian Church, conversion often meant giving up false gods. For the Hebrews (or Jews), conversion was, if not more difficult, a little more complicated. Going back hundreds of years, their cherished beliefs and traditions were rooted in worshipping the true God and following the teachings of His prophets. Yet Jesus Christ had fulfilled the law of Moses and given a higher law. Would following Christ mean that the Hebrews had to give up their earlier beliefs and history? The Epistle to the Hebrews sought to help settle such questions by teaching that the law of Moses, the prophets, and the ordinances are all important, but Jesus Christ is greater (see [[Hebrews 1.1|Hebrews 1:1–4]][[Hebrews 1.2|]][[Hebrews 1.3|]][[Hebrews 1.4|]]; [[Hebrews 3.1|3:1–6]][[Hebrews 3.2|]][[Hebrews 3.3|]][[Hebrews 3.4|]][[Hebrews 3.5|]][[Hebrews 3.6|]]; [[Hebrews 7.23|7:23–28]][[Hebrews 7.24|]][[Hebrews 7.25|]][[Hebrews 7.26|]][[Hebrews 7.27|]][[Hebrews 7.28|]]). In fact, all these things point to and testify of Christ as the Son of God and the promised Messiah the Jewish people had been waiting for. In those early days and today, conversion means making Jesus Christ the center of our worship and our lives. It means holding onto truth and letting go of things that distract us from Him, for He is “the author of eternal salvation unto all them that obey him” ([[Hebrews 5.9|Hebrews 5:9]]).

For more about Hebrews, see Guide to the Scriptures, “[[GS Hebrews, Epistle to|Hebrews, Epistle to]],” Gospel Library.

### I can learn about Jesus Christ from His names and titles.

To help Jewish Christians build their faith in Christ, the Epistle to the Hebrews used several of His names and titles. Look for these names in [[Hebrews 1.1|Hebrews 1–5]][[Hebrews 1.2|]][[Hebrews 1.3|]][[Hebrews 1.4|]][[Hebrews 1.5|]][[Hebrews 1.6|]][[Hebrews 1.7|]][[Hebrews 1.8|]][[Hebrews 1.9|]][[Hebrews 1.10|]][[Hebrews 1.11|]][[Hebrews 1.12|]][[Hebrews 1.13|]][[Hebrews 1.14|]]. When you find each one, ask yourself:

- What does this name or title teach me about Jesus Christ’s roles in my life?

- What does it reveal about His attributes?

You can start by searching [[Hebrews 1.1|Hebrews 1:1–3, 10]][[Hebrews 1.2|]][[Hebrews 1.3|]][[Hebrews 1.10|]]; [[Hebrews 2.9|2:9–10, 17]][[Hebrews 2.10|]][[Hebrews 2.17|]]; [[Hebrews 3.1|3:1]]; [[Hebrews 5.8|5:8–10]][[Hebrews 5.9|]][[Hebrews 5.10|]]. Consider writing down some of the names and putting them where you will see them to remind you of Jesus Christ’s role in your life.

See also “[Precious Savior, Dear Redeemer](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/hymns/precious-savior-dear-redeemer?lang=eng),” *Hymns*, no. 103.

### Jesus Christ suffered all things so that He can understand and help me when I suffer.

Have you ever felt that no one understood what you were going through? Or maybe you’ve felt that you didn’t deserve God’s love or help because of your mistakes? The Epistle to the Hebrews can help with both of these concerns.

What do you find in [[Hebrews 2.9|Hebrews 2:9–18]][[Hebrews 2.10|]][[Hebrews 2.11|]][[Hebrews 2.12|]][[Hebrews 2.13|]][[Hebrews 2.14|]][[Hebrews 2.15|]][[Hebrews 2.16|]][[Hebrews 2.17|]][[Hebrews 2.18|]]; [[Hebrews 4.12|4:12–16]][[Hebrews 4.13|]][[Hebrews 4.14|]][[Hebrews 4.15|]][[Hebrews 4.16|]]; [[Hebrews 5.7|5:7–8]][[Hebrews 5.8|]] that strengthens your confidence that Jesus Christ can help you and is willing to? Consider recording your thoughts and feelings about the Savior’s compassion and understanding for you. What does it mean to you to “come boldly unto the throne of grace” and seek mercy? ([[Hebrews 4.16|Hebrews 4:16]]).

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[Hebrews 1.1|Hebrews 1:1–10]][[Hebrews 1.2|]][[Hebrews 1.3|]][[Hebrews 1.4|]][[Hebrews 1.5|]][[Hebrews 1.6|]][[Hebrews 1.7|]][[Hebrews 1.8|]][[Hebrews 1.9|]][[Hebrews 1.10|]]; [[Hebrews 2.8|2:8–10, 17–18]][[Hebrews 2.9|]][[Hebrews 2.10|]][[Hebrews 2.17|]][[Hebrews 2.18|]]
### I believe in Jesus Christ.

- You could help your children find truths about Jesus Christ in [[Hebrews 1.1|Hebrews 1:1–10]][[Hebrews 1.2|]][[Hebrews 1.3|]][[Hebrews 1.4|]][[Hebrews 1.5|]][[Hebrews 1.6|]][[Hebrews 1.7|]][[Hebrews 1.8|]][[Hebrews 1.9|]][[Hebrews 1.10|]]; [[Hebrews 2.8|2:8–10, 17–18]][[Hebrews 2.9|]][[Hebrews 2.10|]][[Hebrews 2.17|]][[Hebrews 2.18|]] and write them on strips of paper. Then you could hide the papers around the room and invite your children to find them. After they find each truth, talk together about what it means to you. Your children can find more truths about Jesus Christ in “[I Believe in Christ](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/hymns/i-believe-in-christ?lang=eng)” (*Hymns*, no. 134). Let them share why they love Jesus.

[[Hebrews 3.7|Hebrews 3:7–19]][[Hebrews 3.8|]][[Hebrews 3.9|]][[Hebrews 3.10|]][[Hebrews 3.11|]][[Hebrews 3.12|]][[Hebrews 3.13|]][[Hebrews 3.14|]][[Hebrews 3.15|]][[Hebrews 3.16|]][[Hebrews 3.17|]][[Hebrews 3.18|]][[Hebrews 3.19|]]
### I can receive Heavenly Father’s blessings as I “harden not” my heart.

- To learn about the difference between a hard heart and a soft heart, you and your children could find an object that is soft and absorbent (like a sponge or a washcloth) and something hard (like a rock). Your children could touch the objects and describe how the objects feel. Then they could put a few drops of water on each object and notice how the water soaks into the absorbent object better than into the hard one. Then you could read together [[Hebrews 3.8|Hebrews 3:8]] and discuss what it means to have a hard heart. What can we do to soften our hearts?

[[Hebrews 4.14|Hebrews 4:14–16]][[Hebrews 4.15|]][[Hebrews 4.16|]]
### I can turn to Jesus Christ for help.

- You might introduce these verses by asking your children who they ask for help when they are sick or hurt. Why do we go to these people? You could then read together [[Hebrews 4.14|Hebrews 4:14–16]][[Hebrews 4.15|]][[Hebrews 4.16|]]. You might share with your children a time when you turned to Jesus Christ and found “grace to help in \[your\] time of need.”

- You could also share what President Camille N. Johnson said about rocks in a backpack in “[[2023 April - Jesus Christ Is Relief.md|Jesus Christ Is Relief]]” (*Liahona*, May 2023, 81–82). What “rocks” do we carry? How does Jesus help us?

[[Hebrews 5.1|Hebrews 5:1–4]][[Hebrews 5.2|]][[Hebrews 5.3|]][[Hebrews 5.4|]]
### A person must be called of God to do His work.

- This outline has a picture titled *Moses Calls Aaron to the Ministry*. Consider showing it to your children while you read [[Hebrews 5.4|Hebrews 5:4]] to them. Help them identify who is in the picture, and discuss that the Aaronic Priesthood was named after Aaron.

- You could talk about different ways people get authority. For example, how does a teacher, doctor, or political leader get the authority to do their jobs? How does God give us authority to do His work in His Church? Your children could think about this question as they read [[Hebrews 5.4|Hebrews 5:4]] and [[Articles of Faith 1.5|the fifth article of faith]]. Tell your children about times when you’ve received authority to do God’s work—for example, when you were set apart to serve in a calling.
