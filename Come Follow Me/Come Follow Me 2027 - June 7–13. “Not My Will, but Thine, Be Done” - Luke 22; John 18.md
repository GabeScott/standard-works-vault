# June 7–13. “Not My Will, but Thine, Be Done”: Luke 22; John 18

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/24?lang=eng)

In the Garden of Gethsemane, Jesus took upon Himself the sins, pains, and sufferings of every person who ever lived and who ever would live. And yet almost no one alive at that time knew what was happening. There were only three mortal witnesses of this event, and they slept through much of it. Eternity’s most important events often pass without much worldly attention. But God the Father knew. He heard the pleading of His faithful Son: “Father, if thou be willing, remove this cup from me: nevertheless not my will, but thine, be done. And there appeared an angel unto him from heaven, strengthening him” ([[Luke 22.42|Luke 22:42–43]][[Luke 22.43|]]). While we were not there to witness this act of selflessness and submission, we are witnesses of the Atonement of Jesus Christ. Every time we repent and receive forgiveness of our sins, every time we feel the Savior’s strengthening power, we can testify of the reality of what happened in the Garden of Gethsemane.

### Conversion to Jesus Christ takes a lifetime of effort and learning.

Think about the experiences Peter had with the Savior—the miracles he witnessed and the teachings he heard. Why then would the Savior say to Peter, “*When* thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren”? ([[Luke 22.32|Luke 22:32]]; emphasis added). As you read [[Luke 22.31|Luke 22:31–34, 54–62]][[Luke 22.32|]][[Luke 22.33|]][[Luke 22.34|]][[Luke 22.54|]][[Luke 22.55|]][[Luke 22.56|]][[Luke 22.57|]][[Luke 22.58|]][[Luke 22.59|]][[Luke 22.60|]][[Luke 22.61|]][[Luke 22.62|]] (see also [[John 18.17|John 18:17–27]][[John 18.18|]][[John 18.19|]][[John 18.20|]][[John 18.21|]][[John 18.22|]][[John 18.23|]][[John 18.24|]][[John 18.25|]][[John 18.26|]][[John 18.27|]]), consider your own conversion to Christ. Have you ever felt so committed that, like Peter, you were “ready to go with \[the Savior\], both into prison, and to death”? ([[Luke 22.33|Luke 22:33]]). Why do those feelings sometimes fade?

There are daily opportunities to either deny or witness of the Savior; what will you do to be a daily witness of Him?

What other lessons do you learn from Peter’s experience?

What other questions do you have about the Atonement of Jesus Christ?

It might be interesting to know that Gethsemane was a garden of olive trees. It likely would have included an olive press, used to crush olives and extract oil. Olive oil was used for lighting, food, and healing (see [[Luke 10.34|Luke 10:34]]). How might the process of extracting olive oil symbolize what the Savior did for us in Gethsemane? For some ideas, see Elder D. Todd Christofferson’s message “[[2016 October - “Abide in My Love”.md|Abide in My Love]]” (*Ensign* or *Liahona*, Nov. 2016, 50–51).

> **Use music to invite the Spirit.** Sometimes we don’t have adequate words to express what we feel or understand about the Atonement of Jesus Christ. Sacred music can testify of truth in ways that words alone cannot. If you are teaching about the Savior’s suffering in Gethsemane, consider giving learners a copy of the lyrics to a song like “[I Stand All Amazed](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/hymns/i-stand-all-amazed?lang=eng)” (*Hymns*, no. 193) or “[Gethsemane](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/music/hymns-for-home-and-church/gethsemane?lang=eng)” (*Hymns—For Home and Church*, Gospel Library) so they can sing or follow along while you play a recording of the music. Encourage them to pay attention to what they feel as they reflect on the words and music.

### Jesus Christ responded to contentious situations with patience and kindness.

During His arrest and trials, Jesus Christ showed how we can respond to tense situations. As you read about His arrest (see [[Luke 22.47|Luke 22:47–53]][[Luke 22.48|]][[Luke 22.49|]][[Luke 22.50|]][[Luke 22.51|]][[Luke 22.52|]][[Luke 22.53|]]; [[John 18.1|John 18:1–12]][[John 18.2|]][[John 18.3|]][[John 18.4|]][[John 18.5|]][[John 18.6|]][[John 18.7|]][[John 18.8|]][[John 18.9|]][[John 18.10|]][[John 18.11|]][[John 18.12|]]) and trials (see [[Luke 22.63|Luke 22:63–71]][[Luke 22.64|]][[Luke 22.65|]][[Luke 22.66|]][[Luke 22.67|]][[Luke 22.68|]][[Luke 22.69|]][[Luke 22.70|]][[Luke 22.71|]]; [[John 18.19|John 18:19–24]][[John 18.20|]][[John 18.21|]][[John 18.22|]][[John 18.23|]][[John 18.24|]]), note what He did to stay true to His mission but also turn away wrath. You might choose a passage that is especially meaningful to you and ponder how you can follow the Savior’s example in your own difficult situations.

### The Savior’s “kingdom is not of this world.”

As a political leader, Pontius Pilate was familiar with the power and kingdoms of this world. But Jesus spoke of a different kind of kingdom. What evidence have you seen that the Lord’s “kingdom is not of this world”? ([[John 18.36|John 18:36]]).

What else stands out to you about Jesus’s words to Pilate?

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[Luke 22.39|Luke 22:39–44]][[Luke 22.40|]][[Luke 22.41|]][[Luke 22.42|]][[Luke 22.43|]][[Luke 22.44|]]
### I can follow Jesus’s example by doing what Heavenly Father wants, even when it’s hard.

- To help your children understand Jesus’s request that His Father “remove this cup” ([[Luke 22.42|Luke 22:42]]), you could give them something sour or bitter in a cup, like vinegar or lemon juice. They could talk about how hard it would be to drink something like that. As you read together [[Luke 22.42|verse 42]], talk about what the “cup” might represent (see [[D&C 19.16|Doctrine and Covenants 19:16–19]][[D&C 19.17|]][[D&C 19.18|]][[D&C 19.19|]]). Why was Jesus willing to suffer for us?

- You could ask your children to repeat the words “Not my will, but thine, be done” ([[Luke 22.42|Luke 22:42]]) and talk about what that means. Maybe they could name some things Heavenly Father asks us to do that are hard. After each example, you could invite them to say again, “Not my will, but thine, be done.” It might be good to share experiences when doing what Heavenly Father wants, even when it was hard, brought blessings.

### Jesus suffered for me because He loves me and wants to help me.

- Consider what you can do to create a reverent and worshipful spirit as you study [[Luke 22.39|Luke 22:39–46]][[Luke 22.40|]][[Luke 22.41|]][[Luke 22.42|]][[Luke 22.43|]][[Luke 22.44|]][[Luke 22.45|]][[Luke 22.46|]] together. You might play or sing together some of your children’s favorite hymns about the Savior, such as “[Gethsemane](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/music/hymns-for-home-and-church/gethsemane?lang=eng)” (*Hymns—For Home and Church*, Gospel Library). Or you and your children could read or watch “[Jesus in Gethsemane](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/new-testament-scripture-stories/43-garden-of-gethsemane?lang=eng)” (in *New Testament Stories*) or look at one of the pictures in this outline. Invite your children to point out details in the pictures that stand out to them.> 2:23


- You could ask your children to make a list of things that cause people sadness or pain. They might include experiences they’ve had. Then you could read together [[Alma 7.11|Alma 7:11–12]][[Alma 7.12|]] and look for words that describe what Jesus did for us—and why He did it. You could also pass around a picture of Jesus and, as each child holds the picture, say, “Jesus suffered because He loves \[child’s name\].” Invite your children to repeat these words with you.
