# September 27–October 3. “Gather Together in One All Things in Christ”: Ephesians

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/40?lang=eng)

“In time past,” Paul observed, Jews and Gentiles were divided by tradition and culture. Gentiles, including many in Ephesus, felt like “strangers from the covenants of promise, having no hope, and without God in the world” ([[Ephesians 2.11|Ephesians 2:11–12]][[Ephesians 2.12|]]). It was as if a wall separated God’s children—from each other and, ultimately, from Him. Jesus Christ came to break down that wall (see [[Ephesians 2.13|Ephesians 2:13–14]][[Ephesians 2.14|]]). He “abolished … enmity” and made the two groups one, “that he might reconcile both unto God” ([[Ephesians 2.15|Ephesians 2:15–16]][[Ephesians 2.16|]]). The Savior’s work of breaking down walls continues because human beings keep finding ways to divide ourselves. But there’s something unique about His work today. We’re living in what Paul called “the dispensation of the fulness of times,” and the reuniting and gathering of God’s children is happening on a larger scale than ever before. This is the time when, as Paul prophesied, God is gathering “in one *all things* in Christ” ([[Ephesians 1.10|Ephesians 1:10]]; emphasis added)—all truth, all believers, all the covenant people, all keys, all generations, the living and the dead, throughout all time.

For more about the epistle to the Ephesians, see Guide to the Scriptures, “[[GS Ephesians, Epistle to|Ephesians, Epistle to]],” Gospel Library.

## Ideas for Learning at Home and at Church

[[Ephesians 1.10|Ephesians 1:10]]
### “In the dispensation of the fulness of times,” God will “gather together in one all things in Christ.”

Why do you think our time is called “the dispensation of the fulness of times”? Look for insights about this dispensation as you read the following scriptures: [[Ephesians 4.13|Ephesians 4:13]]; [[2 Nephi 30.7|2 Nephi 30:7–8]][[2 Nephi 30.8|]]; [[D&C 110.11|Doctrine and Covenants 110:11–16]][[D&C 110.12|]][[D&C 110.13|]][[D&C 110.14|]][[D&C 110.15|]][[D&C 110.16|]]; [[D&C 112.30|112:30–32]][[D&C 112.31|]][[D&C 112.32|]]; [[D&C 128.18|128:18–21]][[D&C 128.19|]][[D&C 128.20|]][[D&C 128.21|]].

What might it mean to “gather together in one all things in Christ”? You could look for possible answers in Elder David A. Bednar’s message “[[2018 October - Gather Together in One All Things in Christ.md|Gather Together in One All Things in Christ]]” (*Ensign* or *Liahona*, Nov. 2018, 21–24).

See also “Dispensations: The Pattern of Apostasy and Restoration” (video), Gospel Library.

### Christ’s Church is founded on prophets and apostles, and Jesus Christ is the chief cornerstone.

If someone asked you why we need prophets and apostles, what would you say? Look for possible answers in [[Ephesians 2.19|Ephesians 2:19–22]][[Ephesians 2.20|]][[Ephesians 2.21|]][[Ephesians 2.22|]]; [[Ephesians 3.1|3:1–7]][[Ephesians 3.2|]][[Ephesians 3.3|]][[Ephesians 3.4|]][[Ephesians 3.5|]][[Ephesians 3.6|]][[Ephesians 3.7|]]; [[Ephesians 4.11|4:11–16]][[Ephesians 4.12|]][[Ephesians 4.13|]][[Ephesians 4.14|]][[Ephesians 4.15|]][[Ephesians 4.16|]]. As you listen to general conference this weekend, consider how our prophets and apostles fulfill the purposes Paul described. For example, how do they help you not be “carried about with every wind of doctrine”? How do they help us grow “in the unity of the faith, and of the knowledge of the Son of God”?

How is Jesus Christ like a cornerstone for His Church? How is He like a cornerstone for your life? Ponder how you can build your life on the Savior and His apostles and prophets.

### Jesus Christ’s example can help me strengthen my family relationships.

It’s important to note that Paul’s words in [[Ephesians 5.22|Ephesians 5:22–24]][[Ephesians 5.23|]][[Ephesians 5.24|]] were written in the context of social customs of his era. Prophets and apostles today teach that men are not superior to women and that spouses are meant to be “equal partners” (“[The Family: A Proclamation to the World](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/the-family-a-proclamation-to-the-world/the-family-a-proclamation-to-the-world?lang=eng),” Gospel Library).

With that in mind, as you read [[Ephesians 5.21|Ephesians 5:21–33]][[Ephesians 5.22|]][[Ephesians 5.23|]][[Ephesians 5.24|]][[Ephesians 5.25|]][[Ephesians 5.26|]][[Ephesians 5.27|]][[Ephesians 5.28|]][[Ephesians 5.29|]][[Ephesians 5.30|]][[Ephesians 5.31|]][[Ephesians 5.32|]][[Ephesians 5.33|]]; [[Ephesians 6.1|6:1–4]][[Ephesians 6.2|]][[Ephesians 6.3|]][[Ephesians 6.4|]], think about how following the Savior’s example can strengthen your relationships with your family members. How does Christ show His love for the people of His Church? What does this imply about how spouses, as equal partners, should treat each other? What other messages do you find for yourself in these verses? What do you think it means to be raised “in the nurture and admonition of the Lord”?

### The armor of God will help protect me from evil.

As you read [[Ephesians 6.10|Ephesians 6:10–18]][[Ephesians 6.11|]][[Ephesians 6.12|]][[Ephesians 6.13|]][[Ephesians 6.14|]][[Ephesians 6.15|]][[Ephesians 6.16|]][[Ephesians 6.17|]][[Ephesians 6.18|]], consider why Paul identified these specific pieces of armor. The following questions might help:

- **“Loins girt about with truth”:** This piece of armor is like a belt tied around the waist. The phrase *girt about* can mean “fastened” or “surrounded.” How do you surround yourself with truth?

- **“Breastplate of righteousness”:** What does a breastplate protect? How has righteousness protected you?

- **“Feet shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace”:** What could feet symbolize?

- **“Shield of faith”:** How has your faith shielded you from Satan’s “fiery darts”?

- **“Helmet of salvation”:** What does a helmet protect? What might that represent?

- **“Sword of the Spirit”:** Why do you think the Spirit and the word of God can be compared to a sword?

See also [[2 Nephi 1.23|2 Nephi 1:23]]; [[D&C 27.15|Doctrine and Covenants 27:15–18]][[D&C 27.16|]][[D&C 27.17|]][[D&C 27.18|]]; “The Armor of God” (video), Gospel Library.

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[Ephesians 2.20|Ephesians 2:20–22]][[Ephesians 2.21|]][[Ephesians 2.22|]]; [[Ephesians 4.11|4:11–14]][[Ephesians 4.12|]][[Ephesians 4.13|]][[Ephesians 4.14|]]
### Jesus Christ’s Church is founded on prophets and apostles, and Jesus is the chief cornerstone.

- Before reading [[Ephesians 2.20|Ephesians 2:20–22]][[Ephesians 2.21|]][[Ephesians 2.22|]]; [[Ephesians 4.11|4:11–14]][[Ephesians 4.12|]][[Ephesians 4.13|]][[Ephesians 4.14|]], maybe you and your children could look at a cornerstone and foundation (see the image below; see also “In the Unity of the Faith,” in *New Testament Stories*). You could talk about why buildings need strong foundations. How are Jesus and His Apostles like a strong foundation for us in His Church? Your children could use blocks to build a church together and talk about the blessings of having prophets and apostles. Maybe they could build while listening to general conference, adding a block every time an Apostle teaches about Jesus Christ.> 2:19


- You could help your children find words in the [[Articles of Faith 1.6|sixth article of faith]] that are also in [[Ephesians 4.11|Ephesians 4:11–14]][[Ephesians 4.12|]][[Ephesians 4.13|]][[Ephesians 4.14|]]. Tell them how prophets and apostles have helped you grow in “the knowledge of the Son of God.”

- To teach your children about the blessing of having Jesus Christ’s Church on the earth, you could sing together “[The Church of Jesus Christ](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/the-church-of-jesus-christ?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 77). Share with each other why you’re thankful to belong to the Savior’s Church.

[[Ephesians 6.1|Ephesians 6:1–3]][[Ephesians 6.2|]][[Ephesians 6.3|]]
### Heavenly Father wants me to obey my parents.

- As you read [[Ephesians 6.1|Ephesians 6:1–3]][[Ephesians 6.2|]][[Ephesians 6.3|]] together, discuss what it means to “obey” and “honour” both Heavenly Father and our parents. Your children might draw a picture of a time when they obeyed their parents. Let them share how they were blessed for obeying and how they felt when they obeyed.

[[Ephesians 6.10|Ephesians 6:10–18]][[Ephesians 6.11|]][[Ephesians 6.12|]][[Ephesians 6.13|]][[Ephesians 6.14|]][[Ephesians 6.15|]][[Ephesians 6.16|]][[Ephesians 6.17|]][[Ephesians 6.18|]]
### The armor of God can protect me spiritually from evil.

- As you read together [[Ephesians 6.10|Ephesians 6:10–18]][[Ephesians 6.11|]][[Ephesians 6.12|]][[Ephesians 6.13|]][[Ephesians 6.14|]][[Ephesians 6.15|]][[Ephesians 6.16|]][[Ephesians 6.17|]][[Ephesians 6.18|]], your children could look at a picture of a person wearing armor (in this outline or in “The Whole Armor of God,” in *New Testament Stories*). They could point to each piece of armor as it is mentioned, and you could talk about how God has kept you and your family safe from spiritual danger.> 2:4


- Perhaps you and your children could find items to represent the pieces of armor mentioned in [[Ephesians 6.14|Ephesians 6:14–17]][[Ephesians 6.15|]][[Ephesians 6.16|]][[Ephesians 6.17|]] (for example, a hat or an apron). Or they could make simple pieces of armor from paper. Let your children take turns putting on the “armor.” How do we put on the armor of God?

> **Children often learn best when multiple senses are involved.** All children are different, and as they grow, their needs change. Using different teaching methods will help you meet your children’s diverse needs and keep their attention. As you study the armor of God in [[Ephesians 6.10|Ephesians 6:10–18]][[Ephesians 6.11|]][[Ephesians 6.12|]][[Ephesians 6.13|]][[Ephesians 6.14|]][[Ephesians 6.15|]][[Ephesians 6.16|]][[Ephesians 6.17|]][[Ephesians 6.18|]], consider different ways you could help your children ponder the meaning of each piece. What could they touch or wear that might help them understand the spiritual significance of Paul’s teachings?
