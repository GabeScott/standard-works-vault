# June 14–20. “It Is Finished”: Matthew 27; Mark 15; Luke 23; John 19

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/25?lang=eng)

In every word and deed, Jesus Christ exemplified pure love, or charity (see [[1 Corinthians 13.1|1 Corinthians 13]][[1 Corinthians 13.2|]][[1 Corinthians 13.3|]][[1 Corinthians 13.4|]][[1 Corinthians 13.5|]][[1 Corinthians 13.6|]][[1 Corinthians 13.7|]][[1 Corinthians 13.8|]][[1 Corinthians 13.9|]][[1 Corinthians 13.10|]][[1 Corinthians 13.11|]][[1 Corinthians 13.12|]][[1 Corinthians 13.13|]]). At no time was this more evident than during the final hours of His mortal life. Jesus was silent and dignified when falsely accused. Why? Because charity “is not easily provoked” ([[1 Corinthians 13.5|1 Corinthians 13:5]]). He submitted to scourging, mocking, and crucifixion—all of which He could have ended at any time, but He didn’t. Why? Because “charity suffereth long” and “beareth all things” ([[1 Corinthians 13.4|1 Corinthians 13:4, 7]][[1 Corinthians 13.7|]]). He showed compassion toward His mother and mercy toward His crucifiers—even during His own suffering. Why? Because charity “seeketh not her own” ([[1 Corinthians 13.5|1 Corinthians 13:5]]). At the end of His life, Jesus was doing what He had done throughout His life—teaching us by showing us. This is why another term for charity is “the pure love of Christ” ([[Moroni 7.47|Moroni 7:47]]).

### As a disciple of Christ, I am devoted to Him, even during difficult times.

### The Savior opened the way for me to return to my Heavenly Father.

Perhaps the Savior’s most poignant moment on the cross came when He cried out, “My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?” ([[Matthew 27.46|Matthew 27:46]]; [[Mark 15.34|Mark 15:34]]). As you ponder His plea, you might study President Jeffrey R. Holland’s comments on this topic in “[[2009 April - None Were with Him.md|None Were with Him]]” (*Ensign* or *Liahona*, May 2009, 87–88).

You might also think about times when you have felt distant from God. Then you could read these reassuring promises and ponder how Jesus Christ has helped you feel closer to God:

- [[Mosiah 15.23|Mosiah 15:23]]

- [[Alma 42.23|Alma 42:23]]

- [[Helaman 14.15|Helaman 14:15]]

- [[D&C 88.63|Doctrine and Covenants 88:63]]; [[D&C 122.7|122:7–9]][[D&C 122.8|]][[D&C 122.9|]]

What can you do to receive this gift the Savior has given you?

### Because Jesus died for me, I can repent and improve.

- After reviewing the events of the Crucifixion, you and your children could read [[D&C 19.16|Doctrine and Covenants 19:16]]. Why did Jesus suffer for us? You might also invite your children to use this verse to complete the following sentence: “I, , have  these things for , that they might not  if they would .”

> **Don’t worry about covering everything.** Instead, focus on your children’s needs. As you keep those needs in mind, the Spirit will help you identify which principles, stories, or scriptures will be especially meaningful to them.

### I can forgive others as Jesus did.

- Consider describing how the soldiers were unkind to Jesus (see [[Matthew 27.26|Matthew 27:26–37]][[Matthew 27.27|]][[Matthew 27.28|]][[Matthew 27.29|]][[Matthew 27.30|]][[Matthew 27.31|]][[Matthew 27.32|]][[Matthew 27.33|]][[Matthew 27.34|]][[Matthew 27.35|]][[Matthew 27.36|]][[Matthew 27.37|]]), and then read [[Luke 23.34|Luke 23:34]]. To help your children understand what it means to forgive, you might tell them about a time when someone forgave you (or you forgave someone else). Or you could watch with them the video “The Goal: A Story of Faith, Friendship and Forgiveness” (Gospel Library). You could also read together the definition of “[[GS Forgive|Forgive]]” in Guide to the Scriptures (Gospel Library). Help your children see that the Savior’s sacrifice for our sins makes forgiveness possible.
