# December 6–12. “They Overcame … by the Blood of the Lamb”: Revelation 6–14

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/50?lang=eng)

Imagine a woman “travailing in birth, and pained to be delivered.” Now imagine “a great red dragon, having seven heads and ten horns” hovering over the woman, poised to “devour her child as soon as it was born” ([[Revelation 12.2|Revelation 12:2–4]][[Revelation 12.3|]][[Revelation 12.4|]]). With these images, John was trying to describe the peril that the Church and kingdom of God would face. For the Christians who experienced intense persecution in John’s day, victory over evil might not have seemed likely. This victory can also be hard to imagine in our day, when the adversary is at “war with the saints” and has “power … over all kindreds, and tongues, and nations” ([[Revelation 13.7|Revelation 13:7]]). But John’s revelation gloriously shows that good will prevail over evil. Babylon will fall. And the people of God will come “out of great tribulation” with robes of white—not because their robes were never stained but because the people will “have washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb” ([[Revelation 7.14|Revelation 7:14]]).

### John saw many events of earth’s history, especially the latter days.

In these chapters you’ll read about a book with seven seals. The Lord revealed to Joseph Smith that this book represents the story of the earth’s “temporal existence,” with each seal representing a thousand years (see [[D&C 77.6|Doctrine and Covenants 77:6–7]][[D&C 77.7|]]). John summarizes the events of the first four seals in only eight verses ([[Revelation 6.1|Revelation 6:1–8]][[Revelation 6.2|]][[Revelation 6.3|]][[Revelation 6.4|]][[Revelation 6.5|]][[Revelation 6.6|]][[Revelation 6.7|]][[Revelation 6.8|]]). The next three verses describe the fifth seal ([[Revelation 6.9|verses 9–11]][[Revelation 6.10|]][[Revelation 6.11|]]). The events of the last two seals take up most of the rest of the book of Revelation. In other words, the main focus of John’s vision is the last days—our days. As you read, ponder why it is valuable to know what John wrote about the latter days. Here are a few ideas that might help:

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[Revelation 7.9|Revelation 7:9, 13–17]][[Revelation 7.13|]][[Revelation 7.14|]][[Revelation 7.15|]][[Revelation 7.16|]][[Revelation 7.17|]]
### Jesus Christ helps me be forgiven and comforts me.

- You might introduce these verses by showing your children two white pieces of paper or cloth and letting them get one of them dirty by marking it with a pen or putting dirt on it. Then, as you read [[Revelation 7.9|Revelation 7:9, 13–14]][[Revelation 7.13|]][[Revelation 7.14|]] together, your children could point to the clean paper or cloth every time they hear the word “white.” Explain that white clothes represent forgiveness of sin and remind us that because of Jesus Christ, we can be forgiven as we repent.

- Perhaps your children could draw pictures of the beautiful promises they find in [[Revelation 7.15|Revelation 7:15–17]][[Revelation 7.16|]][[Revelation 7.17|]]. Or you could give them papers cut into the shape of teardrops. On one side, they could write or draw something that makes them sad. On the other side, they could write or draw how Jesus comforts them.

[[Revelation 12.7|Revelation 12:7–11]][[Revelation 12.8|]][[Revelation 12.9|]][[Revelation 12.10|]][[Revelation 12.11|]]
### I showed faith in Jesus Christ in the premortal life.

- [[Revelation 12.7|Revelation 12:7–11]][[Revelation 12.8|]][[Revelation 12.9|]][[Revelation 12.10|]][[Revelation 12.11|]] describes the War in Heaven. You can help your children understand this event by sharing parts of “Jesus Christ Will Come Again” (in *New Testament Stories*). Emphasize that we all chose to follow Jesus Christ in the premortal life. To understand what it means to follow someone, your children could take turns being the “leader” while the others follow by doing whatever the leader does.> 2:51


- Your children could also draw three columns and label them “Before this life,” “During this life,” and “Both.” You could prepare strips of paper that state truths about the premortal life and about mortal life, such as *We have bodies*, *We don’t have bodies*, *We live with God*, *We are at war with Satan*, *We exercise faith in Jesus Christ*, and *We follow God’s plan*. You can find other examples in Guide to the Scriptures, “[[GS Premortal Life|Premortal Life]]” (Gospel Library) or the songs “[I Lived in Heaven](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/i-lived-in-heaven?lang=eng)” and “[I Will Follow God’s Plan](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/i-will-follow-gods-plan?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 4, 164–65). Let the children take turns picking a paper strip and deciding which column it belongs in. Share your confidence that the children can continue to show faith in Christ.

- Why is it important to know about what happened in the War in Heaven? To answer this question, you and your children could read about how knowing about the premortal life affected Elder Ahmad S. Corbitt when he was young (see “[[2021 April - You Can Gather Israel!.md|You Can Gather Israel!]],” *Liahona*, May 2021, 61).

> **Invite your children to restate what they are learning.** When we explain a scripture story or gospel principle in our own words, we can understand it better and remember it longer. After reading [[Revelation 12.7|Revelation 12:7–11]][[Revelation 12.8|]][[Revelation 12.9|]][[Revelation 12.10|]][[Revelation 12.11|]] with your children, you could write down the words *War in Heaven*, *dragon*, *cast out*, *Lamb*, and *testimony*. Then you could help your children summarize the verses using these words. Invite them to share what they learn about Jesus Christ (the Lamb) from these verses.
