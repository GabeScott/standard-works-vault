# Thoughts to Keep in Mind: Reading the Epistles

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/31a?lang=eng)

Normally it’s considered impolite to read someone else’s mail. But in the case of the New Testament epistles—letters that the Lord’s Apostles wrote to members of His early Church—reading them is highly encouraged. The 21 epistles in the New Testament contain precious truths about the gospel of Jesus Christ, along with powerful testimonies of some of His special witnesses. Thanks in part to divine intervention, these letters have been preserved for nearly 2,000 years and translated into hundreds of languages. Why? Surely part of the reason is so that you can read these inspired letters today.

But reading the epistles is different from reading other books in the New Testament. For one thing, the epistles don’t tell a story. Reading them is more like listening to a conversation—and hearing only one side of it. Most of the epistles were written in response to a specific question or problem. We don’t know for certain what the problem was, though sometimes we can infer it from the content of the epistle.

For example, it seems that Paul was concerned about Jewish Christians insisting that Gentile Christians conform to traditions of the law of Moses. So in several of his epistles (to the Romans and Galatians, especially), Paul emphasized strongly that salvation does not come from obedience to the law of Moses but from Jesus Christ.

To cite another example, it appears that some Saints in Corinth were questioning whether Jesus Christ had really been resurrected (see [[1 Corinthians 15.1|1 Corinthians 15]][[1 Corinthians 15.2|]][[1 Corinthians 15.3|]][[1 Corinthians 15.4|]][[1 Corinthians 15.5|]][[1 Corinthians 15.6|]][[1 Corinthians 15.7|]][[1 Corinthians 15.8|]][[1 Corinthians 15.9|]][[1 Corinthians 15.10|]][[1 Corinthians 15.11|]][[1 Corinthians 15.12|]][[1 Corinthians 15.13|]][[1 Corinthians 15.14|]][[1 Corinthians 15.15|]][[1 Corinthians 15.16|]][[1 Corinthians 15.17|]][[1 Corinthians 15.18|]][[1 Corinthians 15.19|]][[1 Corinthians 15.20|]][[1 Corinthians 15.21|]][[1 Corinthians 15.22|]][[1 Corinthians 15.23|]][[1 Corinthians 15.24|]][[1 Corinthians 15.25|]][[1 Corinthians 15.26|]][[1 Corinthians 15.27|]][[1 Corinthians 15.28|]][[1 Corinthians 15.29|]][[1 Corinthians 15.30|]][[1 Corinthians 15.31|]][[1 Corinthians 15.32|]][[1 Corinthians 15.33|]][[1 Corinthians 15.34|]][[1 Corinthians 15.35|]][[1 Corinthians 15.36|]][[1 Corinthians 15.37|]][[1 Corinthians 15.38|]][[1 Corinthians 15.39|]][[1 Corinthians 15.40|]][[1 Corinthians 15.41|]][[1 Corinthians 15.42|]][[1 Corinthians 15.43|]][[1 Corinthians 15.44|]][[1 Corinthians 15.45|]][[1 Corinthians 15.46|]][[1 Corinthians 15.47|]][[1 Corinthians 15.48|]][[1 Corinthians 15.49|]][[1 Corinthians 15.50|]][[1 Corinthians 15.51|]][[1 Corinthians 15.52|]][[1 Corinthians 15.53|]][[1 Corinthians 15.54|]][[1 Corinthians 15.55|]][[1 Corinthians 15.56|]][[1 Corinthians 15.57|]][[1 Corinthians 15.58|]]). So Paul boldly declared His witness that Jesus did rise from the dead, and “death is swallowed up in victory” ([[1 Corinthians 15.54|1 Corinthians 15:54]]). The Apostle John, in his epistles, seems to be responding to a related doctrinal error: the idea that Christ never had a body at all—that He was a spirit only and just *seemed* to have a physical form. So John bore witness of that “which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled” ([[1 John 1.1|1 John 1:1]]).

In some ways, the problems and questions those early Saints had might seem quite different from ours. You might find a few things in the epistles that just don’t feel relevant to today. But you’ll find much more that feels like Paul and the other Apostles could just as easily have been writing to you. With a little pondering, and with the guidance of the Spirit, you can, as Nephi suggested, “liken all scriptures” to yourself ([[1 Nephi 19.23|1 Nephi 19:23]]). Maybe you’re not tempted to rely on the law of Moses and its traditions for salvation. But do you have other traditions that you cling to—perhaps something that prevents you from putting your full trust in Jesus Christ?

At its core, the Apostles’ message to the Saints in these epistles was to live the gospel of Jesus Christ, even when social or cultural forces tried to pull them away. That’s a message all of us need. And surely that’s one reason God has preserved these precious letters for you.
