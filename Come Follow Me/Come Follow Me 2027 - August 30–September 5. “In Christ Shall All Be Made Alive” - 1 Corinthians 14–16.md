# August 30–September 5. “In Christ Shall All Be Made Alive”: 1 Corinthians 14–16

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/36?lang=eng)

Because the gospel of Jesus Christ was relatively new in places like Corinth, Paul was probably used to correcting doctrinal misunderstandings. But the error in Corinth was bigger than most—Church members were saying “there is no resurrection of the dead” ([[1 Corinthians 15.12|1 Corinthians 15:12]]). This was no minor difference of opinion. This is the doctrine “wherein \[we\] stand” ([[1 Corinthians 15.1|verse 1]]). “In Christ,” Paul declared, “shall *all* be made alive” ([[1 Corinthians 15.22|verse 22]]; emphasis added). The Savior’s Resurrection gives life to every person and to every good work. If it weren’t for His Resurrection, then why preach the gospel? Why bother doing family history research and being baptized for the dead? Why build temples and receive the ordinances of eternal life? If everything ends at death, then everything is vain. If hope is for this life only, then hope is misery. If we live eternally as spirits only, we can never become like God and receive His joy. But Christ *is* risen. Our hope in Him is both eternal and joyful. And because of this, we can be “steadfast, unmovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord,” because we “know that \[our\] labour is not in vain” ([[1 Corinthians 15.58|1 Corinthians 15:58]]).

### “Let all things be done unto edifying.”

In Paul’s day, it seems some Church members were manifesting the spiritual gift of tongues by speaking “in an unknown tongue” ([[1 Corinthians 14.2|1 Corinthians 14:2]]). This is different from the way this gift was manifest at the day of Pentecost (see [[Acts 2.1|Acts 2:1–11]][[Acts 2.2|]][[Acts 2.3|]][[Acts 2.4|]][[Acts 2.5|]][[Acts 2.6|]][[Acts 2.7|]][[Acts 2.8|]][[Acts 2.9|]][[Acts 2.10|]][[Acts 2.11|]]) and from the way we usually experience it today. Paul was concerned that speaking in unknown languages was not accomplishing the purpose of gathering as Saints: “that the church may receive edifying” ([[1 Corinthians 14.5|1 Corinthians 14:5]]), or building up. As you read [[1 Corinthians 14.1|1 Corinthians 14]][[1 Corinthians 14.2|]][[1 Corinthians 14.3|]][[1 Corinthians 14.4|]][[1 Corinthians 14.5|]][[1 Corinthians 14.6|]][[1 Corinthians 14.7|]][[1 Corinthians 14.8|]][[1 Corinthians 14.9|]][[1 Corinthians 14.10|]][[1 Corinthians 14.11|]][[1 Corinthians 14.12|]][[1 Corinthians 14.13|]][[1 Corinthians 14.14|]][[1 Corinthians 14.15|]][[1 Corinthians 14.16|]][[1 Corinthians 14.17|]][[1 Corinthians 14.18|]][[1 Corinthians 14.19|]][[1 Corinthians 14.20|]][[1 Corinthians 14.21|]][[1 Corinthians 14.22|]][[1 Corinthians 14.23|]][[1 Corinthians 14.24|]][[1 Corinthians 14.25|]][[1 Corinthians 14.26|]][[1 Corinthians 14.27|]][[1 Corinthians 14.28|]][[1 Corinthians 14.29|]][[1 Corinthians 14.30|]][[1 Corinthians 14.31|]][[1 Corinthians 14.32|]][[1 Corinthians 14.33|]][[1 Corinthians 14.34|]][[1 Corinthians 14.35|]][[1 Corinthians 14.36|]][[1 Corinthians 14.37|]][[1 Corinthians 14.38|]][[1 Corinthians 14.39|]][[1 Corinthians 14.40|]], ponder questions like these:

### How does the statement about women in these verses apply today?

In Paul’s day, there were different expectations about how women participated in society, including in church meetings. Whatever the teachings in [[1 Corinthians 14.34|1 Corinthians 14:34–35]][[1 Corinthians 14.35|]] meant in Paul’s day, they shouldn’t be understood to mean that women cannot speak and lead in the Church today (see Joseph Smith Translation, 1 Corinthians 14:34 \[in [[1 Corinthians 14.34|1 Corinthians 14:34]], footnote *b*\]). President Russell M. Nelson said to the women of the Church today: “We … need your strength, your conversion, your conviction, your ability to lead, your wisdom, and your voices. The kingdom of God is not and cannot be complete without women who make sacred covenants and then keep them, women who can speak with the power and authority of God!” (“[[2015 October - A Plea to My Sisters.md|A Plea to My Sisters]],” *Ensign* or *Liahona*, Nov. 2015, 96).

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[1 Corinthians 15.12|1 Corinthians 15:12–22]][[1 Corinthians 15.13|]][[1 Corinthians 15.14|]][[1 Corinthians 15.15|]][[1 Corinthians 15.16|]][[1 Corinthians 15.17|]][[1 Corinthians 15.18|]][[1 Corinthians 15.19|]][[1 Corinthians 15.20|]][[1 Corinthians 15.21|]][[1 Corinthians 15.22|]]
### Because Jesus Christ was resurrected, I will be too.

- You and your children might read together [[1 Corinthians 15.12|1 Corinthians 15:12–22]][[1 Corinthians 15.13|]][[1 Corinthians 15.14|]][[1 Corinthians 15.15|]][[1 Corinthians 15.16|]][[1 Corinthians 15.17|]][[1 Corinthians 15.18|]][[1 Corinthians 15.19|]][[1 Corinthians 15.20|]][[1 Corinthians 15.21|]][[1 Corinthians 15.22|]], searching for answers to the question “What would happen if there were no resurrection?” Or they could take turns completing this phrase: “Because Jesus Christ was resurrected, .” (See also “In Christ All Shall Be Made Alive,” in *New Testament Stories*.)> 2:21


- Maybe an object lesson would help your children understand resurrection. You could use an item of clothing, like a glove or jacket, to represent our bodies. When we are alive, our spirits are in our bodies, and we can move (put the item of clothing on). At death, our spirits leave, and our bodies cannot move (remove the item of clothing). In the resurrection, our spirits and bodies come back together (put the item of clothing back on). Let your children take turns using the object lesson to teach about resurrection. They could also sing “[Did Jesus Really Live Again?](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/did-jesus-really-live-again?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 64).

[[1 Corinthians 15.21|1 Corinthians 15:21–22; 40–42]][[1 Corinthians 15.22|]][[1 Corinthians 15.40|]][[1 Corinthians 15.41|]][[1 Corinthians 15.42|]]
### Because Jesus was resurrected, I can be resurrected and receive a celestial body.

- Do you have any objects that could help your children understand what Paul taught about “corruptible” and “incorruptible” bodies? (see [[1 Corinthians 15.52|1 Corinthians 15:52–54]][[1 Corinthians 15.53|]][[1 Corinthians 15.54|]]). Maybe something that shows wear or deterioration would work, like a rusty piece of metal or a frayed cloth. This could help you discuss how earthly things, including our physical bodies, grow old and wear down. Then you could read together [[1 Corinthians 15.21|1 Corinthians 15:21–22]][[1 Corinthians 15.22|]] to find out how Jesus Christ saved us from this problem.

- You could also show pictures of the sun, moon, and stars to help your children understand what a resurrected body might be like. Maybe they could put the pictures in order from the brightest to the least bright. You could then read [[1 Corinthians 15.40|1 Corinthians 15:40–42]][[1 Corinthians 15.41|]][[1 Corinthians 15.42|]] together (see also the Joseph Smith Translation in [[JST 1 Corinthians 15.40|verse 40]], footnote *a*; [[D&C 76.69|Doctrine and Covenants 76:69–70]][[D&C 76.70|]]) or watch the video “The Kingdoms of Glory” (Gospel Library) and discuss what the sun, moon, and stars represent in the Resurrection.

[[1 Corinthians 15.29|1 Corinthians 15:29]]
### I can be baptized for my ancestors in the temple because of Jesus Christ.

- To teach your children why baptism for the dead is needed, you could help them think of things that little children can’t do for themselves. Who helps them? This could lead to a conversation about ancestors who need our help because they died without being baptized. Then you could read [[1 Corinthians 15.29|1 Corinthians 15:29]] to find out how we can help them.

- Consider sharing a picture of a loved one who died without receiving the restored gospel of Jesus Christ. You could show how they’re connected to you on your family tree and explain how you can take their name to the temple so they can be baptized and confirmed (see [familysearch.org/temple/ordinances-ready](https://www.familysearch.org/en/temple/ordinances-ready)).
