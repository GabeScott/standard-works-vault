# March 8–14. “I Will Give You Rest”: Matthew 11–12; Luke 11

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/11?lang=eng)

In many ways, the Pharisees and scribes had made worshipping Jehovah burdensome. They often emphasized strict rules over eternal truths. For example, rules about the Sabbath day, which was meant to be a day of rest, had become a heavy burden.

And then Jehovah Himself came among His people. He taught them that the true purpose of God’s law is not to create burdens but to relieve them. He taught that God gives us commandments, including the one to honor the Sabbath, not to oppress us but to bless us. Yes, the way to God is strait and narrow, but Jesus taught that we need not walk it alone. “Come unto me,” He pleaded. His invitation, to all who feel “heavy laden” for any reason, is to stand beside Him, to bind ourselves to Him, and to let Him share our burdens. His promise is “Ye shall find rest unto your souls.” Compared to the alternatives—trying to carry on alone or relying on mortal solutions—His “yoke is easy, and \[His\] burden is light” ([[Matthew 11.28|Matthew 11:28–30]][[Matthew 11.29|]][[Matthew 11.30|]]).

## Ideas for Learning at Home and at Church

[[Matthew 11.28|Matthew 11:28–30]][[Matthew 11.29|]][[Matthew 11.30|]]
### Jesus Christ will give me rest as I yoke myself to Him.

If you carry a burden of any kind, then the Savior’s invitation in [[Matthew 11.28|Matthew 11:28–30]][[Matthew 11.29|]][[Matthew 11.30|]] is addressed to you. Read it as though He were speaking to you personally.

Here are some questions that can help you ponder His invitation:

- When the Savior says, “Come unto me,” what do you think He’s asking you to do?

- What do you think Jesus means by “rest”? Look for the “special kind of rest” President Russell M. Nelson describes in “[[2022 October - Overcome the World and Find Rest.md|Overcome the World and Find Rest]]” (*Liahona*, Nov. 2022, 95–98). You could also explore how President Camille N. Johnson describes the rest the Savior offers in “[[2023 April - Jesus Christ Is Relief.md|Jesus Christ Is Relief]]” (*Liahona*, May 2023, 81–83).

- Why do you think Jesus uses the word “yoke” to describe the help He offers? How would you describe His yoke? (See Emily Belle Freeman, “[[2023 October - Walking in Covenant Relationship with Christ.md|Walking in Covenant Relationship with Christ]],” *Liahona*, Nov. 2023, 76–79.)

- What have you learned of the Savior (see [[Matthew 11.29|verse 29]]) as you’ve been yoked with Him through your covenants?

Perhaps, as you’ve pondered these verses, you’ve felt inspired to do something to accept the Savior’s invitation. Record your impressions and act on them.

### The Sabbath is a day to do good.

The Pharisees’ teachings differed from the Savior’s in many ways, but especially in how to honor the Sabbath day. As you read [[Matthew 12.1|Matthew 12:1–13]][[Matthew 12.2|]][[Matthew 12.3|]][[Matthew 12.4|]][[Matthew 12.5|]][[Matthew 12.6|]][[Matthew 12.7|]][[Matthew 12.8|]][[Matthew 12.9|]][[Matthew 12.10|]][[Matthew 12.11|]][[Matthew 12.12|]][[Matthew 12.13|]], consider how your attitudes and actions regarding the Sabbath align with the Savior’s teachings. You could ponder statements like these:

“I will have mercy, and not sacrifice” ([[Matthew 12.7|verse 7]]; see also [[Hosea 6.6|Hosea 6:6]]).

“The Son of man is Lord even of the sabbath day” ([[Matthew 12.8|verse 8]]).

“It is lawful to do well on the sabbath days” ([[Matthew 12.12|verse 12]]).

- How might these teachings influence the way you approach the Sabbath?

- How has honoring the Sabbath affected your relationship with the Savior?

See also [[Mark 2.23|Mark 2:23–28]][[Mark 2.24|]][[Mark 2.25|]][[Mark 2.26|]][[Mark 2.27|]][[Mark 2.28|]]; [[Mark 3.1|3:1–5]][[Mark 3.2|]][[Mark 3.3|]][[Mark 3.4|]][[Mark 3.5|]]; “Upon My Holy Day—Getting Closer to God,” “Upon My Holy Day—Honoring the Sabbath,” “Upon My Holy Day—Rest and Renewal” (videos), ChurchofJesusChrist.org; Topics and Questions, “[[GS Sabbath Day|Sabbath Day]],” Gospel Library.

> **Asking questions.** Whether you’re studying on your own, with your family, or in a Church class, a thoughtful question can lead to valuable spiritual insights. Give yourself (or your class) plenty of time to ponder the questions in *Come, Follow Me*. That pondering time can become sacred moments of personal revelation. ### My words and actions reflect what is in my heart.

One of the Savior’s main criticisms of the Pharisees was that they tried to appear righteous but their intentions were not pure. As you study the Savior’s warnings to the Pharisees in [[Matthew 12.34|Matthew 12:34–37]][[Matthew 12.35|]][[Matthew 12.36|]][[Matthew 12.37|]] and [[Luke 11.33|Luke 11:33–44]][[Luke 11.34|]][[Luke 11.35|]][[Luke 11.36|]][[Luke 11.37|]][[Luke 11.38|]][[Luke 11.39|]][[Luke 11.40|]][[Luke 11.41|]][[Luke 11.42|]][[Luke 11.43|]][[Luke 11.44|]], ponder the connection between your heart and your actions.

- What does the phrase “good treasure of the heart” mean to you? ([[Matthew 12.35|Matthew 12:35]]).

- How do our words justify or condemn us? (see [[Matthew 12.37|Matthew 12:37]]).

- What might it mean for your eye to be “single”? ([[Luke 11.34|Luke 11:34]]).

### My prayers are important to God.

After observing the Savior praying, one of His disciples said, “Lord, teach us to pray.” Jesus did that and more—He taught precious truths about prayer. Consider listing the truths you find in [[Luke 11.1|Luke 11:1–13]][[Luke 11.2|]][[Luke 11.3|]][[Luke 11.4|]][[Luke 11.5|]][[Luke 11.6|]][[Luke 11.7|]][[Luke 11.8|]][[Luke 11.9|]][[Luke 11.10|]][[Luke 11.11|]][[Luke 11.12|]][[Luke 11.13|]]. ## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[Matthew 11.28|Matthew 11:28–30]][[Matthew 11.29|]][[Matthew 11.30|]]
### Jesus will help me as I come unto Him.

- To help your children visualize the Savior’s teachings in [[Matthew 11.28|Matthew 11:28–30]][[Matthew 11.29|]][[Matthew 11.30|]], you could show them the picture at the end of this outline. Help them find the yoke in the picture, and ask them to point to it when they hear that word as you read the verses. You could also invite them to take turns trying to pull something heavy, first by themselves and then with help. What are some of the burdens we carry? How does Jesus help us?

- Perhaps you and your children could read the story about the boy at the temple open house in President Russell M. Nelson’s message “[[2022 October - Overcome the World and Find Rest.md|Overcome the World and Find Rest]]” (*Liahona*, Nov. 2022, 95–96). What does this story teach us about the rest Jesus offers?

[[Matthew 12.1|Matthew 12:1–13]][[Matthew 12.2|]][[Matthew 12.3|]][[Matthew 12.4|]][[Matthew 12.5|]][[Matthew 12.6|]][[Matthew 12.7|]][[Matthew 12.8|]][[Matthew 12.9|]][[Matthew 12.10|]][[Matthew 12.11|]][[Matthew 12.12|]][[Matthew 12.13|]]
### The Sabbath is a day to do good things that bring me closer to God.

- Inspired by the Savior’s example in [[Matthew 12.1|Matthew 12:1–13]][[Matthew 12.2|]][[Matthew 12.3|]][[Matthew 12.4|]][[Matthew 12.5|]][[Matthew 12.6|]][[Matthew 12.7|]][[Matthew 12.8|]][[Matthew 12.9|]][[Matthew 12.10|]][[Matthew 12.11|]][[Matthew 12.12|]][[Matthew 12.13|]], your children could make a list of ways you can “do well on the sabbath” ([[Matthew 12.12|verse 12]]). Be sure to include opportunities to serve others. It could be helpful to keep your list in a container labeled “Do Well” and refer to it on future Sundays.

- You could also show a calendar to your children and help them find the Sabbath day. What do we do on other days of the week? What can we do to make the Sabbath different from other days? (see [[Isaiah 58.13|Isaiah 58:13–14]][[Isaiah 58.14|]]).

- Your children could look for things we do to honor the Lord on the Sabbath in “[As I Keep the Sabbath Day](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/music/hymns-for-home-and-church/as-i-keep-the-sabbath-day?lang=eng)” (*Hymns—For Home and Church*, Gospel Library). Maybe they could also do actions to represent things they can do to honor the Lord on His holy day.

- How can we explain to other people why the Sabbath day is special to us? The video “Miguel’s Sabbath Lesson” (Gospel Library) could provide some ideas.> 8:2


[[Luke 11.11|Luke 11:11–13]][[Luke 11.12|]][[Luke 11.13|]]
### I can ask Heavenly Father to bless me with His Spirit.

- An object lesson could help you illustrate the Savior’s teachings in [[Luke 11.11|Luke 11:11–13]][[Luke 11.12|]][[Luke 11.13|]]. For example, you could place a stone inside a bread bag or put a picture of a scorpion inside an egg carton and invite your children to guess what is inside. Then you could read [[Luke 11.11|Luke 11:11–13]][[Luke 11.12|]][[Luke 11.13|]] together to find out how Jesus used objects like these to teach about prayer and the Holy Spirit. What did He want us to understand?

[[Luke 11.37|Luke 11:37–42]][[Luke 11.38|]][[Luke 11.39|]][[Luke 11.40|]][[Luke 11.41|]][[Luke 11.42|]]
### Both my actions and my heart must be pure.

- Perhaps your children could discuss [[Luke 11.37|Luke 11:37–42]][[Luke 11.38|]][[Luke 11.39|]][[Luke 11.40|]][[Luke 11.41|]][[Luke 11.42|]] while washing dishes together. You could talk about why it would be a bad idea to wash only the outsides of bowls and cups. You could then relate this to the need to be righteous not just in our outward deeds but also in our inward thoughts and feelings.
