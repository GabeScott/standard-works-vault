# April 12–18. “What Shall I Do to Inherit Eternal Life?”: Matthew 18; Luke 10

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/16?lang=eng)

When you ask the Lord a question, you might receive an answer you did not expect. Who is my neighbor? Anyone who needs your help and love. Who is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven? A child. Is it enough to forgive someone seven times? No, you should forgive seventy times seven. (See [[Luke 10.29|Luke 10:29–37]][[Luke 10.30|]][[Luke 10.31|]][[Luke 10.32|]][[Luke 10.33|]][[Luke 10.34|]][[Luke 10.35|]][[Luke 10.36|]][[Luke 10.37|]]; [[Matthew 18.4|Matthew 18:4, 21–22]][[Matthew 18.21|]][[Matthew 18.22|]].) Unexpected answers from the Lord can invite us to change the way we think, feel, and act. If you really want to learn from the Savior, He will teach you how to live in ways that lead to eternal life.

### The Savior’s forgiveness can inspire me to forgive others.

Peter’s suggestion that he could forgive someone seven times might seem generous, but Jesus taught a higher law. His response—“I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven” ([[Matthew 18.22|Matthew 18:22]])—was teaching not about numbers but about developing a Christlike attitude of forgiveness.

As you read the parable of the unmerciful servant, ponder questions like these:

- Who could the king, the unmerciful servant, and the fellow servant represent?

- What could the debts represent?

- When have you felt God’s mercy and compassion? Is there someone who needs to feel mercy and compassion from you?

See also Kristin M. Yee, “[[2022 October - Beauty for Ashes - The Healing Path of Forgiveness.md|Beauty for Ashes: The Healing Path of Forgiveness]],” *Liahona*, Nov. 2022, 36–38; “Forgive Every One Their Trespasses: The Parable of the Unmerciful Servant” (video), Gospel Library.

### “Love … thy neighbour as thyself.”

The commandments to love God and love your neighbor were not new to the people of Jesus Christ’s time; they were part of the law of Moses (see [[Deuteronomy 6.5|Deuteronomy 6:5]]; [[Leviticus 19.18|Leviticus 19:18]]). What was new—at least to the lawyer who questioned Jesus—was the Savior’s definition of *neighbor*. As you study [[Luke 10.25|Luke 10:25–37]][[Luke 10.26|]][[Luke 10.27|]][[Luke 10.28|]][[Luke 10.29|]][[Luke 10.30|]][[Luke 10.31|]][[Luke 10.32|]][[Luke 10.33|]][[Luke 10.34|]][[Luke 10.35|]][[Luke 10.36|]][[Luke 10.37|]], consider asking yourself, “Who is *my* neighbor? How are the commandments to love God and love my neighbor related?”

It might help you to know that during Christ’s time, the Jews and Samaritans had disliked and distrusted each other for a long time. Generally, both groups tried to avoid interaction. It also might help to know that Jewish priests and Levites had a special responsibility to serve God and their community.

- Why do you think Jesus chose a priest, a Levite, and a Samaritan to illustrate the commandments to love God and to love our neighbor?

- How would you have reacted to this parable if you were the lawyer?

Since all scriptures testify of Jesus Christ, consider how He might be represented in this parable. Elder Gerrit W. Gong’s message “[[2021 April - Room in the Inn.md|Room in the Inn]]” can give you ideas (*Liahona*, May 2021, 24–27). What other aspects of “our eternal journey” do you see in this parable?

- How would you summarize what the Lord is teaching you in this parable?

- How can you follow the Savior’s invitation to “go, and do thou likewise”?

See also “Parable of the Good Samaritan” (video), Gospel Library.

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[Matthew 18.21|Matthew 18:21–35]][[Matthew 18.22|]][[Matthew 18.23|]][[Matthew 18.24|]][[Matthew 18.25|]][[Matthew 18.26|]][[Matthew 18.27|]][[Matthew 18.28|]][[Matthew 18.29|]][[Matthew 18.30|]][[Matthew 18.31|]][[Matthew 18.32|]][[Matthew 18.33|]][[Matthew 18.34|]][[Matthew 18.35|]]
### Because Jesus forgives me, I want to forgive others.

- To help your children visualize [[Matthew 18.21|Matthew 18:21–35]][[Matthew 18.22|]][[Matthew 18.23|]][[Matthew 18.24|]][[Matthew 18.25|]][[Matthew 18.26|]][[Matthew 18.27|]][[Matthew 18.28|]][[Matthew 18.29|]][[Matthew 18.30|]][[Matthew 18.31|]][[Matthew 18.32|]][[Matthew 18.33|]][[Matthew 18.34|]][[Matthew 18.35|]], you could share with them “The Parable of the Servant Who Would Not Forgive” from *New Testament Stories*. Or you could write or draw a large amount of money and a small amount of money and ask your children to imagine that two people owed you these amounts. Which debt would be easier to forgive? They could think about that while you read the parable together. What does Jesus want us to learn from this story?> 2:41


- You could also talk about times when you or your children needed to forgive someone—or when you needed forgiveness. How did the Savior help you in these situations?

- Perhaps your children would benefit from practicing asking for forgiveness and extending it. Maybe you could let them role-play scenarios that might happen in a family or with friends.

[[Luke 10.25|Luke 10:25–37]][[Luke 10.26|]][[Luke 10.27|]][[Luke 10.28|]][[Luke 10.29|]][[Luke 10.30|]][[Luke 10.31|]][[Luke 10.32|]][[Luke 10.33|]][[Luke 10.34|]][[Luke 10.35|]][[Luke 10.36|]][[Luke 10.37|]]
### Everyone is my neighbor, and Jesus wants me to love them.

- You could begin a discussion about [[Luke 10.25|Luke 10:25–37]][[Luke 10.26|]][[Luke 10.27|]][[Luke 10.28|]][[Luke 10.29|]][[Luke 10.30|]][[Luke 10.31|]][[Luke 10.32|]][[Luke 10.33|]][[Luke 10.34|]][[Luke 10.35|]][[Luke 10.36|]][[Luke 10.37|]] by asking your children, “Who is your neighbor?” Then you could read the verses to find out how Jesus answered this question (see also “The Parable of the Good Samaritan,” in *New Testament Stories*).> 2:49


- To bring the story of the good Samaritan to life, consider inviting your children to act it out. (It could be helpful to explain that most Jews and Samaritans didn’t like each other in Jesus’s day.) Ask your children what they think the parable teaches us. How can we follow the example of the good Samaritan? Consider singing a song about kindness, like “[Jesus Said Love Everyone](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/jesus-said-love-everyone?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 61).

> **Help learners feel hope that they can become like Heavenly Father and Jesus Christ.** You can build confidence in those you teach by helping them see the progress they are making in living the gospel. Think about how they may already be living the principles you discuss. As you teach, share ways you’ve noticed they’ve been striving to be like Jesus Christ. For example, you could share ways you’ve seen them being a good Samaritan to others.
