# May 24–30. “In Remembrance”: Matthew 26; Mark 14; John 13

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/22?lang=eng)

The day before He died, Jesus gave His disciples something to remember Him by. He blessed bread, broke it, and gave it to them to eat. Then He blessed wine and gave it to them to drink (see [[Matthew 26.26|Matthew 26:26–28]][[Matthew 26.27|]][[Matthew 26.28|]]).

That happened about 2,000 years ago, in a place most of us will never see, in a language few of us can understand. But now, every Sunday in our own meeting places, priesthood holders, authorized to act in the name of Jesus Christ, do what He once did. They bless bread and water and give it to each of us, His disciples. It’s a simple act—can there be anything simpler than eating bread and drinking water? But that bread and water are sacred to us because they help us remember Him. As we partake of them, we make a covenant with Him. That covenant is our way of saying “I’ll never forget Him”—not just “I’ll never forget what I’ve read about His teachings and His life” but rather “I’ll never forget what He did for me,” “I’ll never forget how He rescues me when I cry out for help,” and “I’ll never forget His promises to me and my commitment to serve and obey Him always.”

### “She is come … to anoint my body to the burying.”

With a humble act of worship, the woman described in these verses showed that she knew who Jesus was and what He was about to do (see [[Matthew 26.12|Matthew 26:12]]). Why do you think her actions were so meaningful to the Savior? (see [[Matthew 26.13|verse 13]]). What impresses you about the woman and her faith?

### “Lord, is it I?”

Imagine the feeling in the room when the Savior announced that one of His disciples was going to betray Him (see [[Matthew 26.20|Matthew 26:20–22]][[Matthew 26.21|]][[Matthew 26.22|]]; [[Mark 14.17|Mark 14:17–19]][[Mark 14.18|]][[Mark 14.19|]]). What would you have thought or done if you had been there? What do you learn about the disciples from their question to the Lord in these verses?

Consider situations or relationships in your life in which you should ask, “Lord, is it I?”

See also Dieter F. Uchtdorf, “[[2014 October - “Lord, Is It I”.md|Lord, Is It I]]?,” *Ensign* or *Liahona*, Nov. 2014, 56–59.

### The sacrament is an opportunity to remember the Savior.

How would you explain the sacred ordinance of the sacrament to someone who isn’t familiar with it? Think about this as you read [[Matthew 26.26|Matthew 26:26–29]][[Matthew 26.27|]][[Matthew 26.28|]][[Matthew 26.29|]] and [[Mark 14.22|Mark 14:22–25]][[Mark 14.23|]][[Mark 14.24|]][[Mark 14.25|]]. You might think of things you have that help you remember someone important to you. Why do you think Jesus chose the sacrament for us to remember Him?

Perhaps you can list some questions that someone might have about the sacrament. For example:

- Why are bread and water such powerful symbols of Jesus Christ?

- What do you promise as you partake of the sacrament? What promise are you offered?

- How do you make the sacrament a meaningful experience?

### The Savior is my example of humbly serving others.

In Jesus’s time, washing another person’s feet was a task for servants, not leaders. But Jesus wanted His disciples to think differently about what it means to lead and to serve.

- What messages do you find in the Savior’s words and actions in [[John 13.1|John 13:1–17]][[John 13.2|]][[John 13.3|]][[John 13.4|]][[John 13.5|]][[John 13.6|]][[John 13.7|]][[John 13.8|]][[John 13.9|]][[John 13.10|]][[John 13.11|]][[John 13.12|]][[John 13.13|]][[John 13.14|]][[John 13.15|]][[John 13.16|]][[John 13.17|]]?

- Notice what Jesus knew and felt as He did this act of service for His Apostles (see [[John 13.1|verses 1 and 3]][[John 13.3|]]). What do you know and feel that inspires you to serve others?

Washing others’ feet is not a customary way to serve others today. But consider other ways you can follow the Savior’s example of humble service.

[[John 13.34|John 13:34–35]][[John 13.35|]]
### My love for others shows that I am a true disciple of Jesus Christ.

Earlier, Jesus had given a commandment to “love thy neighbour as thyself” ([[Matthew 22.39|Matthew 22:39]]). Now He gave “a new commandment” in [[John 13.34|John 13:34–35]][[John 13.35|]]. What is the difference between these commandments? What do you think it means to love others as Jesus loves you? How is His love different from other kinds of love? (See President Jeffrey R. Holland’s answer to the question “How did Jesus love?” in “[[2024 October - “I Am He”.md|I Am He]],” *Liahona*, Nov. 2024, 78.)

As you consider what you have learned about the Savior’s life, you may want to write *As I Have Loved You* on one side of a paper and list examples that illustrate Jesus’s love. Then you could write *Love One Another* on the other side and list ways you can love the way He did.

You might also ask yourself, “Do people know I am a disciple of Jesus Christ? How can I make Christlike love my defining characteristic?”

[[John 13.34|John 13:34–35]][[John 13.35|]]
### Disciples of Jesus Christ love others as He did.

- One way to help your children learn the Savior’s teachings in [[John 13.34|John 13:34–35]][[John 13.35|]] is to teach them the song “[Love One Another](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/love-one-another?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 136–37). Help them find similarities between the scripture passage and the words of the song. How do people know that we follow Jesus Christ? How does He want His followers to be known? Maybe you could think of people you know who love as Jesus did or people who need to feel the Savior’s love. How can we share His love with them?
