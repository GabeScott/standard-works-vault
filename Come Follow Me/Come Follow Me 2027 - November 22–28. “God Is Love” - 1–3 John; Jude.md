# November 22–28. “God Is Love”: 1–3 John; Jude

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/48?lang=eng)

When John and Jude wrote their epistles, false teachers were claiming Jesus Christ had not actually “come in the flesh”—that He had only seemed to have a physical body (see, for example, [[1 John 4.1|1 John 4:1–3]][[1 John 4.2|]][[1 John 4.3|]]; [[2 John 1.7|2 John 1:7]]). To the Apostle John, of course, this idea was ridiculous. He responded the way Apostles do: by sharing his personal witness of the Savior. He told of “that which … we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, of the Word of life” ([[1 John 1.1|1 John 1:1]]). John went on to teach about something else he had witnessed firsthand: God’s love (see [[John 13.23|John 13:23]]; [[John 20.2|20:2]]).

Then John did another thing that Apostles often do—he invited the Saints to gain their own witness and experience God’s love for themselves. After all, the purpose of his witness, he explained, was not to put himself above others. Rather, John said, “Declare we unto you, that ye also may have fellowship with us: and truly our fellowship is with the Father, and with his Son Jesus Christ” ([[1 John 1.3|1 John 1:3]]).

For more about these epistles, see Guide to the Scriptures, “[[GS John, Son of Zebedee|John, Son of Zebedee]]” and “[[GS Jude|Jude]],” Gospel Library.

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[1 John 1.5|1 John 1:5–7]][[1 John 1.6|]][[1 John 1.7|]]; [[1 John 2.8|2:8–11]][[1 John 2.9|]][[1 John 2.10|]][[1 John 2.11|]]
### God is my light.

- To help your children ponder John’s teachings about darkness and light, you could let them try walking in a dark room and then in the light. Then, as you read together [[1 John 1.5|1 John 1:5–7]][[1 John 1.6|]][[1 John 1.7|]]; [[1 John 2.8|2:8–11]][[1 John 2.9|]][[1 John 2.10|]][[1 John 2.11|]], help them see how following God’s commandments is like walking in the light.

- You could ask your children to find things that give light. Talk with them about the benefits of light, such as helping plants grow, allowing us to see, and giving warmth. They could take turns shining a light at a picture of Jesus Christ as they read [[1 John 1.5|1 John 1:5]].

[[1 John 2.3|1 John 2:3–5]][[1 John 2.4|]][[1 John 2.5|]]; [[1 John 5.2|5:2–3]][[1 John 5.3|]]
### I keep God’s commandments because I love Him.

- Elder Gerrit W. Gong’s wife, Susan, once asked some children, “How do you know your parents and families love you?” (“[[2023 October - Love Is Spoken Here.md|Love Is Spoken Here]],” *Liahona*, Nov. 2023, 111). Maybe your children could read the answers and offer their own. Then they could ponder, “How does God know that I love Him?” To find out how we can show that we love God, read together [[1 John 2.3|1 John 2:3–5]][[1 John 2.4|]][[1 John 2.5|]]; [[1 John 5.2|5:2–3]][[1 John 5.3|]].

- Maybe you could give your children a paper with *Because I love God …* written at the top. They could draw pictures of themselves obeying a commandment (see “Love God, Love Your Neighbor,” in *For the Strength of Youth: A Guide for Making Choices*, 28–32).

[[1 John 4.7|1 John 4:7–11, 20–21]][[1 John 4.8|]][[1 John 4.9|]][[1 John 4.10|]][[1 John 4.11|]][[1 John 4.20|]][[1 John 4.21|]]
### “If God so loved us, we ought also to love one another.”

- As you read [[1 John 4.7|1 John 4:7–11, 20–21]][[1 John 4.8|]][[1 John 4.9|]][[1 John 4.10|]][[1 John 4.11|]][[1 John 4.20|]][[1 John 4.21|]] together, you could help your children find a phrase that they feel is important. Then you could share stories from your lives when someone showed love to you.

- You and your children could also think of situations where someone might need to feel your love. For example, perhaps someone new has begun attending their school or ward. How might this person feel? How can we help them feel God’s love? Maybe you could sing a song like “[Where Love Is](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/where-love-is?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 138–39) and discuss how you can help make your home or ward a place where love is.

[[3 John 1.3|3 John 1:3–4]][[3 John 1.4|]]
### I will “walk in truth.”

- After reading [[3 John 1.3|3 John 1:3–4]][[3 John 1.4|]], you and your children could talk about what it might mean to “walk in truth.” You could tell your children about a time when you felt joy when you saw them walk in truth. Your children might enjoy writing about or drawing truths they have learned on paper footprints. They could use these footprints to make a path you can walk on together.
