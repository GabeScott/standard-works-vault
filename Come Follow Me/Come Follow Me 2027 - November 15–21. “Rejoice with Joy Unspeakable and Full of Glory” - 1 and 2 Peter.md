# November 15–21. “Rejoice with Joy Unspeakable and Full of Glory”: 1 and 2 Peter

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/47?lang=eng)

You remember Peter. He was the fisherman from Galilee who, when he first met Jesus, fell at His feet and said, “Depart from me; for I am a sinful man, O Lord.” Jesus answered, “Fear not; from henceforth thou shalt catch men” ([[Luke 5.8|Luke 5:8, 10]][[Luke 5.10|]]). Later, Peter was the one who stepped out of a boat and walked on water toward Jesus—until the strong winds frightened him. But as soon as he began to sink, he cried, “Lord, save me” ([[Matthew 14.30|Matthew 14:30]]). Peter was the one who insisted that even if everyone else was offended because of Jesus, “yet will I never be offended” ([[Matthew 26.33|Matthew 26:33]]). Then, later that night, Peter denied Him three times. It was Jesus who gave this imperfect but purehearted disciple the name Peter, which means “rock” ([[Matthew 16.18|Matthew 16:18]])—and eventually, Peter did become the solid, dependable leader Jesus always knew he could be.

Near the end of his life, Peter wrote to tell the Saints not to fear “the trial of \[their\] faith,” because it leads to “praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ.” So “rejoice with joy unspeakable,” he said ([[1 Peter 1.7|1 Peter 1:7–8]][[1 Peter 1.8|]])—because, as Peter knew for himself, we can trust the Lord’s “great and precious promises” ([[2 Peter 1.4|2 Peter 1:4]]).

For more about Peter’s epistles, see Guide to the Scriptures, “[[GS Peter|Peter]],” Gospel Library.

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[1 Peter 1.6|1 Peter 1:6–7]][[1 Peter 1.7|]]; [[1 Peter 3.12|3:12–17]][[1 Peter 3.13|]][[1 Peter 3.14|]][[1 Peter 3.15|]][[1 Peter 3.16|]][[1 Peter 3.17|]]; [[1 Peter 4.13|4:13–14, 16]][[1 Peter 4.14|]][[1 Peter 4.16|]]
### Jesus Christ can help me find joy even during hard times.

- To introduce Peter’s teachings about joy and trials, you could help your children list some hard things people have to go through. Then you could read [[1 Peter 1.6|1 Peter 1:6–7]][[1 Peter 1.7|]]; [[1 Peter 3.12|3:12–17]][[1 Peter 3.13|]][[1 Peter 3.14|]][[1 Peter 3.15|]][[1 Peter 3.16|]][[1 Peter 3.17|]]; [[1 Peter 4.13|4:13–14, 16]][[1 Peter 4.14|]][[1 Peter 4.16|]] together, looking for something that would help someone who is going through those hard times. You and your children could share experiences when the Savior helped you during a “trial of your faith” ([[1 Peter 1.7|1 Peter 1:7]]).

[[1 Peter 2.9|1 Peter 2:9–12]][[1 Peter 2.10|]][[1 Peter 2.11|]][[1 Peter 2.12|]]
### Heavenly Father wants me to be an example to others.

- Maybe you and your children could think of examples or find pictures of things that stand out from their surroundings (such as a temple that stands out from the buildings around it or a mountain that rises above a valley). Then you could read [[1 Peter 2.9|1 Peter 2:9–12]][[1 Peter 2.10|]][[1 Peter 2.11|]][[1 Peter 2.12|]]. How does Heavenly Father want us to stand out? Tell your children about some “good works” you have seen them do, and explain how these good works “glorify God”([[1 Peter 2.12|1 Peter 2:12]]). Singing “[I’m Gonna Live So God Can Use Me](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/music/hymns-for-home-and-church/im-gonna-live-so-god-can-use-me?lang=eng)” (*Hymns—For Home and Church*, Gospel Library) might also inspire your children.

[[1 Peter 3.15|1 Peter 3:15]]
### I can always be ready to share the gospel.

- Share with each other some experiences when someone asked you a question about the Savior or His Church. How did you answer? Then you could read together [[1 Peter 3.15|1 Peter 3:15]]. With your children, plan ways to explain how Jesus gives you hope (for ideas, see Jeffrey R. Holland, “[[2022 April - Fear Not - Believe Only!.md|Fear Not: Believe Only!]],” *Liahona*, May 2022, [[2022 April - Fear Not - Believe Only!.md|35, third paragraph]]). Maybe you could practice answering questions about Jesus. What can we do to “be ready always” to share the gospel?

> **“Be ready always.”** “Teaching opportunities … frequently occur in informal, everyday moments. … Because such moments are unplanned, you can’t prepare for them the way you would prepare for a traditional lesson. However, you can prepare *yourself* by being sensitive to the Spirit and striving to ‘be ready always’ ([[1 Peter 3.15|1 Peter 3:15]]). Any moment may become a teaching or learning moment” (*Teaching in the Savior’s Way*, [31](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/teaching-in-the-saviors-way-2022/12-part-3/13-suggestions-for-a-variety-of-teaching?lang=eng&id=title5-p7#title5)).
