# November 9–15. “There Is No Other God That Can Deliver”: Daniel 1–7

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-old-testament-2026/46?lang=eng)

Most likely no one will threaten to throw you into a fiery furnace or a den of lions because of your faith in Jesus Christ. But none of us get through this life without a trial of faith. So we can all benefit from the examples of people like Daniel, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego, who were taken captive as young men by the mighty Babylonian Empire (see [[2 Kings 24.10|2 Kings 24:10–16]][[2 Kings 24.11|]][[2 Kings 24.12|]][[2 Kings 24.13|]][[2 Kings 24.14|]][[2 Kings 24.15|]][[2 Kings 24.16|]]). Surrounded by an unfamiliar culture with different values, they faced great temptations to abandon their beliefs and righteous traditions. Yet they remained true to their covenants. How did they do it? By doing those small and simple things God asks of all of us—praying, fasting, choosing good friends, trusting in Him, and being a light to others. Like Joseph in Egypt and Esther in Persia, Daniel and his friends in Babylon kept their faith in God, and God worked miracles that still inspire believers to this day.

For an overview of the book of Daniel, see “[[BD Daniel|Daniel]]” in the Bible Dictionary.

### I can trust in the Lord when my faith is tried.

In a sense, we all live in Babylon. We often face temptations to compromise our standards and turn away from faith in Christ. As you read [[Daniel 1.1|Daniel 1]][[Daniel 1.2|]][[Daniel 1.3|]][[Daniel 1.4|]][[Daniel 1.5|]][[Daniel 1.6|]][[Daniel 1.7|]][[Daniel 1.8|]][[Daniel 1.9|]][[Daniel 1.10|]][[Daniel 1.11|]][[Daniel 1.12|]][[Daniel 1.13|]][[Daniel 1.14|]][[Daniel 1.15|]][[Daniel 1.16|]][[Daniel 1.17|]][[Daniel 1.18|]][[Daniel 1.19|]][[Daniel 1.20|]][[Daniel 1.21|]], [[Daniel 3.1|3]][[Daniel 3.2|]][[Daniel 3.3|]][[Daniel 3.4|]][[Daniel 3.5|]][[Daniel 3.6|]][[Daniel 3.7|]][[Daniel 3.8|]][[Daniel 3.9|]][[Daniel 3.10|]][[Daniel 3.11|]][[Daniel 3.12|]][[Daniel 3.13|]][[Daniel 3.14|]][[Daniel 3.15|]][[Daniel 3.16|]][[Daniel 3.17|]][[Daniel 3.18|]][[Daniel 3.19|]][[Daniel 3.20|]][[Daniel 3.21|]][[Daniel 3.22|]][[Daniel 3.23|]][[Daniel 3.24|]][[Daniel 3.25|]][[Daniel 3.26|]][[Daniel 3.27|]][[Daniel 3.28|]][[Daniel 3.29|]][[Daniel 3.30|]], and [[Daniel 6.1|6]][[Daniel 6.2|]][[Daniel 6.3|]][[Daniel 6.4|]][[Daniel 6.5|]][[Daniel 6.6|]][[Daniel 6.7|]][[Daniel 6.8|]][[Daniel 6.9|]][[Daniel 6.10|]][[Daniel 6.11|]][[Daniel 6.12|]][[Daniel 6.13|]][[Daniel 6.14|]][[Daniel 6.15|]][[Daniel 6.16|]][[Daniel 6.17|]][[Daniel 6.18|]][[Daniel 6.19|]][[Daniel 6.20|]][[Daniel 6.21|]][[Daniel 6.22|]][[Daniel 6.23|]][[Daniel 6.24|]][[Daniel 6.25|]][[Daniel 6.26|]][[Daniel 6.27|]][[Daniel 6.28|]], note the ways in which Daniel, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego were pressured to do things they knew were wrong. How did they respond to these situations? (see [[Daniel 1.10|Daniel 1:10–13]][[Daniel 1.11|]][[Daniel 1.12|]][[Daniel 1.13|]]; [[Daniel 3.15|3:15–18]][[Daniel 3.16|]][[Daniel 3.17|]][[Daniel 3.18|]]; [[Daniel 6.10|6:10]]). What were the results of their faith? What do you learn from these experiences that can help you trust in the Lord when you face opposition? Also consider the following questions:

- When have you felt pressured to do something you knew was wrong? How has the Lord blessed you for keeping His commandments?

- What if your faith doesn’t lead to the miracles you seek? (for an example, see [[Alma 14.8|Alma 14:8–13]][[Alma 14.9|]][[Alma 14.10|]][[Alma 14.11|]][[Alma 14.12|]][[Alma 14.13|]]). Based on what you read in [[Daniel 3.13|Daniel 3:13–18]][[Daniel 3.14|]][[Daniel 3.15|]][[Daniel 3.16|]][[Daniel 3.17|]][[Daniel 3.18|]], how do you think Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-nego would have answered this question? How can their example affect how you approach your trials?

- How can your righteous choices lead others to greater faith in the Lord? (see [[Daniel 2.47|Daniel 2:47]]; [[Daniel 3.28|3:28–29]][[Daniel 3.29|]]). Ponder the effects that your choices may be having on others.

See also Dieter F. Uchtdorf, “[[2015 October - Be Not Afraid, Only Believe.md|Be Not Afraid, Only Believe]],” *Ensign* or *Liahona*, Nov. 2015, 76–79.

> **Try to involve everyone.** The Savior “inviteth … all to come unto him and partake of his goodness” ([[2 Nephi 26.33|2 Nephi 26:33]]). If you are teaching your family or a class, ponder ways you can give everyone an opportunity to participate in some way. For example, you could give each person a passage of scripture, a section of a conference message, or a verse from a hymn to ponder and comment on. Don’t pressure anyone to participate, but give them opportunities.

### The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints is the kingdom of God on earth.

Through revelation, Daniel saw that Nebuchadnezzar’s dream foretold future worldly kingdoms, as well as the future kingdom of God, which “shall never be destroyed” ([[Daniel 2.44|Daniel 2:44]]). “The Church is that prophesied latter-day kingdom,” Elder D. Todd Christofferson taught, “not created by man but set up by the God of heaven and rolling forth as a stone ‘cut out of the mountain without hands’ to fill the earth” (“[[2015 October - Why the Church.md|Why the Church]],” *Ensign* or *Liahona*, Nov. 2015, 111). Think about God’s latter-day kingdom as you read the descriptions of the stone in [[Daniel 2.34|Daniel 2:34–35, 44–45]][[Daniel 2.35|]][[Daniel 2.44|]][[Daniel 2.45|]]. What is your role in helping this prophecy come to pass?

### Jesus Christ is the Son of the Eternal Father.

During the Savior’s earthly ministry, many Jews considered the title “Son of man” from [[Daniel 7.13|Daniel 7:13]] to refer to the coming Messiah. What do you learn about the Messiah from [[Daniel 7.13|Daniel 7:13–14]][[Daniel 7.14|]]? (see also [[Moses 6.57|Moses 6:57]]).

The Savior often referred to himself as “the Son of man.” Consider reviewing some examples: [[Matthew 25.31|Matthew 25:31]]; [[Mark 9.31|Mark 9:31]]; [[Mark 10.45|10:45]]. What is He teaching about Himself in these verses? In [[Mark 14.61|Mark 14:61–64]][[Mark 14.62|]][[Mark 14.63|]][[Mark 14.64|]], Jesus used this title on the last day of His mortal life. Note the reaction from the people who heard His declaration. Help them understand the stone in the dream represents The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. What do we learn about the Church from this dream? Your children could name some things Heavenly Father did to set up His Church in the latter days. If they need help, you could show pictures of events from the Restoration of the Church (see *Gospel Art Book*, [nos. 90–95](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/gospel-art-book/church-history?lang=eng&id=figure4_p1-figure9_p1#figure4_p1)).

### Heavenly Father wants me to pray often.

- Why was praying to Heavenly Father so important to Daniel? Perhaps you and your children could talk about that question as you read [[Daniel 6.1|Daniel 6]][[Daniel 6.2|]][[Daniel 6.3|]][[Daniel 6.4|]][[Daniel 6.5|]][[Daniel 6.6|]][[Daniel 6.7|]][[Daniel 6.8|]][[Daniel 6.9|]][[Daniel 6.10|]][[Daniel 6.11|]][[Daniel 6.12|]][[Daniel 6.13|]][[Daniel 6.14|]][[Daniel 6.15|]][[Daniel 6.16|]][[Daniel 6.17|]][[Daniel 6.18|]][[Daniel 6.19|]][[Daniel 6.20|]][[Daniel 6.21|]][[Daniel 6.22|]][[Daniel 6.23|]][[Daniel 6.24|]][[Daniel 6.25|]][[Daniel 6.26|]][[Daniel 6.27|]][[Daniel 6.28|]] together. Then you could tell each other why praying is important to you. Your children might enjoy drawing pictures of themselves praying in various situations. They could use their drawings to teach each other that we can pray to Heavenly Father no matter where we are or what we need.
