# August 2–8. “The Power of God unto Salvation”: Romans 1–6

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/32?lang=eng)

By the time Paul wrote his epistle to Roman Church members, the Church of Jesus Christ had grown far beyond a small band of believers from Galilee. There were congregations of Christians almost everywhere the Apostles could reasonably travel—including Rome, the capital of a powerful empire. Still, compared to the vastness of the Roman empire, the Church was small and faced great opposition. Some of it came from outside the Church in the form of persecution from nonbelievers. And some of the hardship came from inside the Church, as certain members placed more faith in their own works than in Jesus Christ. In such conditions, some might feel “ashamed of the gospel of Christ”—but, of course, not Paul. He knew and testified that true power, “the power of God unto salvation,” is found only in the gospel of Jesus Christ ([[Romans 1.16|Romans 1:16]]).

For more about the Epistle to the Romans, see Guide to the Scriptures, “[[GS Romans, Epistle to|Romans, Epistle to]],” Gospel Library.

### My inward commitment to Jesus Christ motivates my outward actions.

Some Christians in Rome believed that their obedience to the law of Moses brought salvation. It may seem like this problem no longer applies, since we don’t live the law of Moses anymore. But the temptation to trust our own works isn’t unique to Paul’s time. As you read Paul’s writings, especially [[Romans 2.17|Romans 2:17–29]][[Romans 2.18|]][[Romans 2.19|]][[Romans 2.20|]][[Romans 2.21|]][[Romans 2.22|]][[Romans 2.23|]][[Romans 2.24|]][[Romans 2.25|]][[Romans 2.26|]][[Romans 2.27|]][[Romans 2.28|]][[Romans 2.29|]], think about your efforts to live the Savior’s teachings. Are your outward actions, such as taking the sacrament or attending the temple, strengthening your faith in Christ? (see [[Alma 25.15|Alma 25:15–16]][[Alma 25.16|]]).

Here’s an exercise that could help you relate to Paul’s teachings. In [[Romans 2.28|Romans 2:28–29]][[Romans 2.29|]], try replacing the word *Jew* with *Latter-day Saint* and the word *circumcision* with *the covenant*. What has helped you align your outward actions with “the heart \[and\] the spirit” of the gospel? ([[Romans 2.29|Romans 2:29]]).

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[Romans 1.16|Romans 1:16–17]][[Romans 1.17|]]
### I am not ashamed of the gospel of Jesus Christ.

- As you and your children read [[Romans 1.16|Romans 1:16]] together, help them find words that show how Paul felt about the Savior’s gospel. Maybe they could repeat together Paul’s statement “I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ.” You might want to discuss what it means to be “not ashamed” of something—sharing some examples could help. How do we act when we’re ashamed? How do we act when we’re not ashamed? What can we do to show that we’re not ashamed of Jesus and His gospel?

- You and your children could look for (or draw) pictures of people living by faith and hide them around the room. Then your children could take turns finding and describing the pictures. What can we do to show that we have faith in Jesus Christ?

[[Romans 3.23|Romans 3:23–24]][[Romans 3.24|]]
### We all need Jesus Christ in order to be forgiven of our sins.

- You could write phrases from [[Romans 3.23|Romans 3:23–24]][[Romans 3.24|]] on a piece of paper and invite your children to put the phrases in the correct order. You could also ask them to find in these verses (1) a problem we all have and (2) God’s solution. (See also “Relying on Jesus Christ for Salvation,” in *New Testament Stories*.) Explain that “grace” in [[Romans 3.24|verse 24]] means the Savior’s gift of help, which makes it possible for us to receive forgiveness for our sins and become better.> 1:58


- To illustrate our need for the Savior’s grace, you could place an object in a high place that your children can’t reach by themselves. Read what Paul taught in [[Romans 3.23|Romans 3:23]]. Then help them reach the object. What can we do together with the Savior that we cannot do by ourselves?

### When I am baptized, I begin a new life as a follower of Jesus Christ.

- As you read together [[Romans 6.3|Romans 6:3–6]][[Romans 6.4|]][[Romans 6.5|]][[Romans 6.6|]], your children could look for what baptism symbolizes. To help, you could share Sister J. Anette Dennis’s explanation in “[[2024 April - Put Ye On the Lord Jesus Christ.md|Put Ye On the Lord Jesus Christ]]” (*Liahona*, May 2024, 10–11). It may help to show a picture of a baptism or a video like “The Baptism of Jesus” (Gospel Library). How is being baptized similar to death and resurrection?> 3:18


- You or your children could also share memories about the day you were baptized or other baptisms you’ve attended. How has your baptism covenant helped you become more like Jesus Christ? As part of this discussion, you could sing together “When I Am Baptized” (*Children’s Songbook*, 103).
