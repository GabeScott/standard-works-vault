# January 18–24. “Prepare Ye the Way of the Lord”: Matthew 3; Mark 1; Luke 3

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/04?lang=eng)

Change is coming. Among John the Baptist’s many important messages as he prepared “the way of the Lord” ([[Mark 1.3|Mark 1:3]]), this one stands out. The mountains that seem so permanent and immovable? They’ll be brought low. The crooked things in your life? They’ll be straightened out. The rough times you’re going through? They’ll be made smooth. (See [[Luke 3.5|Luke 3:5]]; see also [[Isaiah 40.4|Isaiah 40:4]].) That’s the kind of change Jesus Christ and His gospel can bring. So prepare to receive Him. Make way for Him in your life—and since the shortest distance between two points is a straight line, make the path between you and the Savior as straight as possible. Let Him change you. Change can be difficult, but in this case it’s also joyful. Another word for this change is *repentance*, and “the baptism of repentance” was the essence of John’s message ([[Mark 1.4|Mark 1:4]]).

For more about Mark, see Guide to the Scriptures, “[[GS Mark|Mark]],” Gospel Library.

### Repentance is a mighty change of mind and heart.

The mission of John the Baptist was to prepare the people’s hearts to receive the Savior and become more like Him. As you study his message in [[Matthew 3.1|Matthew 3:1–12]][[Matthew 3.2|]][[Matthew 3.3|]][[Matthew 3.4|]][[Matthew 3.5|]][[Matthew 3.6|]][[Matthew 3.7|]][[Matthew 3.8|]][[Matthew 3.9|]][[Matthew 3.10|]][[Matthew 3.11|]][[Matthew 3.12|]]; [[Mark 1.1|Mark 1:1–8]][[Mark 1.2|]][[Mark 1.3|]][[Mark 1.4|]][[Mark 1.5|]][[Mark 1.6|]][[Mark 1.7|]][[Mark 1.8|]]; and [[Luke 3.2|Luke 3:2–18]][[Luke 3.3|]][[Luke 3.4|]][[Luke 3.5|]][[Luke 3.6|]][[Luke 3.7|]][[Luke 3.8|]][[Luke 3.9|]][[Luke 3.10|]][[Luke 3.11|]][[Luke 3.12|]][[Luke 3.13|]][[Luke 3.14|]][[Luke 3.15|]][[Luke 3.16|]][[Luke 3.17|]][[Luke 3.18|]], look for the symbols he used to help people understand what it means to repent. Fruit and wheat are two examples (see [[Luke 3.9|Luke 3:9, 17]][[Luke 3.17|]]), but there are many others.

- What do these images teach you about repentance?

- In [[Luke 3.7|Luke 3:7–14]][[Luke 3.8|]][[Luke 3.9|]][[Luke 3.10|]][[Luke 3.11|]][[Luke 3.12|]][[Luke 3.13|]][[Luke 3.14|]], what changes did John invite the people to make to prepare to receive Christ?

### Receiving the Holy Ghost is like a baptism of fire.

The baptism that John offered was important, but even he knew there was something else to come that was just as important.

As you read [[Matthew 3.11|Matthew 3:11]] and [[Luke 3.16|Luke 3:16]], ponder why John calls receiving the Holy Ghost a baptism “with fire” (see also [[2 Nephi 31.17|2 Nephi 31:17]]).

- What might fire represent? (See [[3 Nephi 9.20|3 Nephi 9:20]]; “Baptism of the Holy Ghost” \[video\], Gospel Library.)> 1:23


Note in [[Matthew 3.16|Matthew 3:16–17]][[Matthew 3.17|]] how the Holy Ghost was manifest at Jesus’s baptism.

- What does the symbolism of a dove suggest to you about the Holy Ghost?

- How would you help a person being baptized understand that receiving the gift of the Holy Ghost is just as important as their baptism?

- What are you doing to continually receive the Holy Ghost in your life?

### The members of the Godhead are three separate beings.

The Bible clearly teaches that the members of the Godhead are three separate beings. The accounts of the Savior’s baptism are one example.
