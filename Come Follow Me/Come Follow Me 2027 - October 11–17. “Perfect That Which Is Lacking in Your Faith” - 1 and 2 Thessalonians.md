# October 11–17. “Perfect That Which Is Lacking in Your Faith”: 1 and 2 Thessalonians

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/42?lang=eng)

In Thessalonica, people accused Paul and Silas of turning “the world upside down” ([[Acts 17.6|Acts 17:6]]). Their preaching angered certain leaders, and these leaders stirred the people into an uproar (see [[Acts 17.1|Acts 17:1–10]][[Acts 17.2|]][[Acts 17.3|]][[Acts 17.4|]][[Acts 17.5|]][[Acts 17.6|]][[Acts 17.7|]][[Acts 17.8|]][[Acts 17.9|]][[Acts 17.10|]]). As a result, Paul and Silas had to leave Thessalonica. But Paul worried about the new Thessalonian converts and the persecution they were facing. He sent Timothy to find out how they were doing, and Timothy “brought \[back\] good tidings of \[their\] faith and charity” (see [[1 Thessalonians 3.5|1 Thessalonians 3:5–6]][[1 Thessalonians 3.6|]]). In fact, the Thessalonian Saints were known as examples “to all that believe” ([[1 Thessalonians 1.7|1 Thessalonians 1:7]]). Paul was relieved, but he knew that faithfulness in the past is not sufficient for spiritual survival in the future, and he was worried about the influence of false teachers (see [[2 Thessalonians 2.2|2 Thessalonians 2:2–3]][[2 Thessalonians 2.3|]]). No matter where we are spiritually, we can always “perfect that which is lacking in \[our\] faith” and “increase more and more” ([[1 Thessalonians 3.10|1 Thessalonians 3:10]]; [[1 Thessalonians 4.10|4:10]]).

### Servants of the Lord serve with sincerity and love.

To open his first epistle to the Thessalonians, Paul described how he and his companions had approached their service in Thessalonica. As you read [[1 Thessalonians 1.1|1 Thessalonians 1:1–5]][[1 Thessalonians 1.2|]][[1 Thessalonians 1.3|]][[1 Thessalonians 1.4|]][[1 Thessalonians 1.5|]]; [[1 Thessalonians 2.1|2:1–13]][[1 Thessalonians 2.2|]][[1 Thessalonians 2.3|]][[1 Thessalonians 2.4|]][[1 Thessalonians 2.5|]][[1 Thessalonians 2.6|]][[1 Thessalonians 2.7|]][[1 Thessalonians 2.8|]][[1 Thessalonians 2.9|]][[1 Thessalonians 2.10|]][[1 Thessalonians 2.11|]][[1 Thessalonians 2.12|]][[1 Thessalonians 2.13|]], think of your own assignments to serve the Lord—in His Church, in your family, or anywhere. Try listing principles you find that can help you serve better. When have you seen these principles make a difference in the Lord’s work?

For more about these epistles, see *Guide to the Scriptures*, “[[GS Thessalonians, Epistles to|Thessalonians, Epistles to]],” Gospel Library.

How can the Savior help us become holy? How did He show us the importance of work? As disciples of Christ, we don’t just follow principles, we follow Him! What else can you do to help those you teach focus on Christ? ### As I follow Jesus Christ, He can make me holy.

Paul taught the Thessalonian Saints that “God hath not called us unto uncleanness, but unto holiness” ([[1 Thessalonians 4.7|1 Thessalonians 4:7]]). As you read [[1 Thessalonians 3.9|1 Thessalonians 3:9–13]][[1 Thessalonians 3.10|]][[1 Thessalonians 3.11|]][[1 Thessalonians 3.12|]][[1 Thessalonians 3.13|]]; [[1 Thessalonians 4.1|4:1–12]][[1 Thessalonians 4.2|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.3|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.4|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.5|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.6|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.7|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.8|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.9|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.10|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.11|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.12|]], ponder ways you can increase in holiness through Christ. For example:

- What do you learn from these verses about words like “holiness” and “sanctification”? How can you receive the Savior’s sanctifying power? What role do ordinances and covenants play in our efforts to become more holy?

- You could also ponder the hymn “[More Holiness Give Me](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/hymns/more-holiness-give-me?lang=eng)” (*Hymns*, no. 131) and look for words and phrases in [[1 Thessalonians 3.9|1 Thessalonians 3:9–13]][[1 Thessalonians 3.10|]][[1 Thessalonians 3.11|]][[1 Thessalonians 3.12|]][[1 Thessalonians 3.13|]]; [[1 Thessalonians 4.1|4:1–12]][[1 Thessalonians 4.2|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.3|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.4|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.5|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.6|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.7|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.8|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.9|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.10|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.11|]][[1 Thessalonians 4.12|]] that you could use to write additional verses to the hymn.

- Ponder what it takes to develop a skill or become proficient in something. What does it take to become holy?

- Sister Wendy W. Nelson suggested asking, “What would a holy \[person\] do?” (“[One Question That Can Change Your Life](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/broadcasts/worldwide-devotional-for-young-adults/2022/05/11nelson?lang=eng)” \[worldwide devotional for young adults, May 15, 2022\], Gospel Library). How might you apply this question in your effort to become holy through the Savior’s power?

### An apostasy, or falling away from truth, was prophesied to precede the Second Coming.

For the Thessalonian Saints, life as a Christian was so hard that many of them believed the Savior’s Second Coming must be near. But Paul knew that before Jesus returned, there would be an apostasy, or a “falling away” from the truth (see [[2 Thessalonians 2.1|2 Thessalonians 2:1–4]][[2 Thessalonians 2.2|]][[2 Thessalonians 2.3|]][[2 Thessalonians 2.4|]]). Consider deepening your understanding of the Great Apostasy—and your appreciation for the Restoration—by pondering some of the following scripture passages:

- *Scriptures that foretold the Apostasy:* [[Isaiah 24.5|Isaiah 24:5]]; [[Amos 8.11|Amos 8:11–12]][[Amos 8.12|]]; [[Matthew 24.4|Matthew 24:4–14]][[Matthew 24.5|]][[Matthew 24.6|]][[Matthew 24.7|]][[Matthew 24.8|]][[Matthew 24.9|]][[Matthew 24.10|]][[Matthew 24.11|]][[Matthew 24.12|]][[Matthew 24.13|]][[Matthew 24.14|]]; [[2 Timothy 4.3|2 Timothy 4:3–4]][[2 Timothy 4.4|]]

- *Scriptures that show the Apostasy was already beginning in Paul’s time:* [[Acts 20.28|Acts 20:28–30]][[Acts 20.29|]][[Acts 20.30|]]; [[Galatians 1.6|Galatians 1:6–7]][[Galatians 1.7|]]; [[1 Timothy 1.5|1 Timothy 1:5–7]][[1 Timothy 1.6|]][[1 Timothy 1.7|]]

- *Scriptures about the Restoration:* [[D&C 12.1|Doctrine and Covenants 12]][[D&C 12.2|]][[D&C 12.3|]][[D&C 12.4|]][[D&C 12.5|]][[D&C 12.6|]][[D&C 12.7|]][[D&C 12.8|]][[D&C 12.9|]]; [[D&C 13.1|13]]; [[D&C 110.1|110]][[D&C 110.2|]][[D&C 110.3|]][[D&C 110.4|]][[D&C 110.5|]][[D&C 110.6|]][[D&C 110.7|]][[D&C 110.8|]][[D&C 110.9|]][[D&C 110.10|]][[D&C 110.11|]][[D&C 110.12|]][[D&C 110.13|]][[D&C 110.14|]][[D&C 110.15|]][[D&C 110.16|]]; [[D&C 128.19|128:19–20]][[D&C 128.20|]]

If someone you love is experiencing a “falling away,” consider these hopeful words from Sister Tamara W. And through this lens of love, they’ll see who they *will* become. … Thankfully, the God we worship is *not* bound by time. He sees who our loved ones really are and who *we* really are. So He’s patient with us, hoping we’ll be patient with each other” (“[[2023 October - Seeing God’s Family through the Overview Lens.md|Seeing God’s Family through the Overview Lens]],” *Liahona*, Nov. 2023, 68).

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[1 Thessalonians 5.1|1 Thessalonians 5:1–6]][[1 Thessalonians 5.2|]][[1 Thessalonians 5.3|]][[1 Thessalonians 5.4|]][[1 Thessalonians 5.5|]][[1 Thessalonians 5.6|]]
### I can prepare for the Second Coming of Jesus Christ by following God’s commandments.

- Perhaps you (or someone you know) can share what it’s like to wait for a baby to be born. How did you get ready? How did you know when it was almost time? Then, as you read [[1 Thessalonians 5.2|1 Thessalonians 5:2–3]][[1 Thessalonians 5.3|]] together, you can compare that experience to waiting for Jesus Christ to come again.

- You could ask your children to imagine that the Savior will visit your class or home today. How could they prepare for His visit? Consider sharing Elder D. Martin Goury’s story about preparing to meet the Savior in “[[2024 October - Be Thou Clean.md|Be Thou Clean]]” (*Liahona*, Nov. 2024, 42). Encourage your children to choose one thing they will do to prepare for the Savior’s return. A song like “[When He Comes Again](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/when-he-comes-again?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 82–83) could help them think about this. (See also “[Children of Light](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/new-testament-scripture-stories/66-children-of-light?lang=eng),” in *New Testament Stories*.)> 1:36


[[2 Thessalonians 2.1|2 Thessalonians 2:1–3]][[2 Thessalonians 2.2|]][[2 Thessalonians 2.3|]]
### Paul taught that an apostasy would happen before Jesus Christ comes again.

- As you read [[2 Thessalonians 2.1|2 Thessalonians 2:1–3]][[2 Thessalonians 2.2|]][[2 Thessalonians 2.3|]] together, help your children discover what has to happen before “the day of Christ,” or the Second Coming. To learn about this “falling away,” your children could look in “[[GS Apostasy|Apostasy]]” in the Guide to the Scriptures (Gospel Library).

- It might be fun for your children to build a tower out of plastic cups or blocks. The cups or blocks could represent things such as gospel truths, baptism, the gift of the Holy Ghost, the priesthood, temple sealings, and prophets. To illustrate how these things were lost after Jesus’s Apostles died, your children could knock the tower down. Then, to illustrate that Jesus Christ brought back or restored His Church, your children could rebuild the tower.

[[2 Thessalonians 3.7|2 Thessalonians 3:7–13]][[2 Thessalonians 3.8|]][[2 Thessalonians 3.9|]][[2 Thessalonians 3.10|]][[2 Thessalonians 3.11|]][[2 Thessalonians 3.12|]][[2 Thessalonians 3.13|]]
### Heavenly Father wants me to learn to work.

- As you and your children read [[2 Thessalonians 3.7|2 Thessalonians 3:7–13]][[2 Thessalonians 3.8|]][[2 Thessalonians 3.9|]][[2 Thessalonians 3.10|]][[2 Thessalonians 3.11|]][[2 Thessalonians 3.12|]][[2 Thessalonians 3.13|]], help them look for the problem the Thessalonian Saints were facing. Why does Heavenly Father want us to work? What would happen if we never learned to work hard? Let your children take turns acting out simple chores while the other children guess what they are doing.

- You could invite your children to talk about a time when they worked hard on a task, a project, or a goal. How did they feel when they completed it? What does it mean to “be not weary in well doing”? ([[2 Thessalonians 3.13|2 Thessalonians 3:13]]; see also [[D&C 64.33|Doctrine and Covenants 64:33–34]][[D&C 64.34|]]).
