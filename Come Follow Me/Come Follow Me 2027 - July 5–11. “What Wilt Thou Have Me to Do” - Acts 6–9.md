# July 5–11. “What Wilt Thou Have Me to Do?”: Acts 6–9

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/28?lang=eng)

If anyone seemed like an unlikely candidate for conversion, it was probably Saul—a Pharisee with a reputation for persecuting Christians. So when the Lord told a disciple named Ananias to seek out Saul and offer him a blessing, Ananias was understandably hesitant. “Lord,” he said, “I have heard by many of this man, how much evil he hath done to thy saints” ([[Acts 9.13|Acts 9:13]]). But the Lord knew Saul’s heart and his potential, and He had a mission in mind for Saul: “He is a chosen vessel unto me, to bear my name before the Gentiles, and kings, and the children of Israel” ([[Acts 9.15|Acts 9:15]]). So Ananias obeyed, and when he found this former persecutor, he called him “Brother Saul” ([[Acts 9.17|Acts 9:17]]). If Saul could change so completely and Ananias could welcome him so freely, then should we ever consider anyone an unlikely candidate for change—including ourselves?

### The Holy Ghost can help me receive the Savior and His servants.

In his words to the Jewish leaders, Stephen—“a man full of faith and of the Holy Ghost” ([[Acts 6.5|Acts 6:5]])—reviewed Israelite history to point out that this wasn’t a new problem; God’s chosen servants have been rejected throughout history, and they still are today. ### Because of Jesus Christ, anyone can change.

Saul’s conversion seems very dramatic; he went from imprisoning Christians to testifying of Christ. For most people, conversion is more gradual. Still, there are things we can learn from Saul about conversion to Christ. As you study [[Acts 8.1|Acts 8:1–3]][[Acts 8.2|]][[Acts 8.3|]] and [[Acts 9.1|9:1–31]][[Acts 9.2|]][[Acts 9.3|]][[Acts 9.4|]][[Acts 9.5|]][[Acts 9.6|]][[Acts 9.7|]][[Acts 9.8|]][[Acts 9.9|]][[Acts 9.10|]][[Acts 9.11|]][[Acts 9.12|]][[Acts 9.13|]][[Acts 9.14|]][[Acts 9.15|]][[Acts 9.16|]][[Acts 9.17|]][[Acts 9.18|]][[Acts 9.19|]][[Acts 9.20|]][[Acts 9.21|]][[Acts 9.22|]][[Acts 9.23|]][[Acts 9.24|]][[Acts 9.25|]][[Acts 9.26|]][[Acts 9.27|]][[Acts 9.28|]][[Acts 9.29|]][[Acts 9.30|]][[Acts 9.31|]], ponder Jesus Christ’s ability to help you change. These questions might help:

- Notice how the Lord involved Ananias in Saul’s conversion. How has He involved others in your conversion? How has He involved you in the conversion of others?

- What do you learn about the Savior from this story?

### The Holy Ghost helps me guide others to Jesus Christ.

What do you learn about helping others come to Jesus Christ from the account in [[Acts 8.26|Acts 8:26–39]][[Acts 8.27|]][[Acts 8.28|]][[Acts 8.29|]][[Acts 8.30|]][[Acts 8.31|]][[Acts 8.32|]][[Acts 8.33|]][[Acts 8.34|]][[Acts 8.35|]][[Acts 8.36|]][[Acts 8.37|]][[Acts 8.38|]][[Acts 8.39|]]? How can you help guide others to Jesus as Philip did?

Elder Ulisses Soares said that this account “is a reminder of the divine mandate we all have to seek to learn and to teach one another the gospel of Jesus Christ. … We are sometimes like the Ethiopian—we need the help of a faithful and inspired teacher; and we are sometimes like Philip—we need to teach and strengthen others in their conversion” (“[[2019 April - How Can I Understand.md|How Can I Understand?]],” *Ensign* or *Liahona*, May 2019, 6).

> **Testify of Jesus Christ.** As a teacher, you can help learners build their faith in the Savior, just as Philip did for the Ethiopian man (see [[Acts 8.26|Acts 8:26–37]][[Acts 8.27|]][[Acts 8.28|]][[Acts 8.29|]][[Acts 8.30|]][[Acts 8.31|]][[Acts 8.32|]][[Acts 8.33|]][[Acts 8.34|]][[Acts 8.35|]][[Acts 8.36|]][[Acts 8.37|]]). To do this, you must have your own faith-building experiences in the scriptures. The witness you share can be a powerful force in helping learners strengthen their testimonies of Jesus Christ. (See *Teaching in the Savior’s Way*, 16–17, 20–21.)
