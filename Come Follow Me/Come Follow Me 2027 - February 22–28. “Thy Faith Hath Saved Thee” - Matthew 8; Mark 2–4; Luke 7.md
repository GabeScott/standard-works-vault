# February 22–28. “Thy Faith Hath Saved Thee”: Matthew 8; Mark 2–4; Luke 7

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/09?lang=eng)

One of the clearest messages in the New Testament is that Jesus Christ is a healer. In story after story, the Savior heals the sick and afflicted—from a woman with a fever to a widow’s son who had died. Why the emphasis on physical healing? What messages might there be for us in these miracles? Certainly, one is that Jesus Christ is the Son of God, with power over all things, including the power to heal our physical pain. But another meaning is found in His words to the skeptical scribes: “That ye may know that the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins” ([[Mark 2.10|Mark 2:10]]). So when you read about Jesus healing someone who is blind or deaf, you might think of the healing—both spiritual and physical—that you can receive from the Savior and hear Him say to you, “Thy faith hath saved thee” ([[Luke 7.50|Luke 7:50]]).

### Jesus Christ is a healer.

These chapters record many instances of the Savior’s miraculous healings. Here are some examples, but there are many more:

- A man with leprosy ([[Matthew 8.1|Matthew 8:1–4]][[Matthew 8.2|]][[Matthew 8.3|]][[Matthew 8.4|]])

- A centurion’s servant ([[Matthew 8.5|Matthew 8:5–13]][[Matthew 8.6|]][[Matthew 8.7|]][[Matthew 8.8|]][[Matthew 8.9|]][[Matthew 8.10|]][[Matthew 8.11|]][[Matthew 8.12|]][[Matthew 8.13|]]; [[Luke 7.1|Luke 7:1–10]][[Luke 7.2|]][[Luke 7.3|]][[Luke 7.4|]][[Luke 7.5|]][[Luke 7.6|]][[Luke 7.7|]][[Luke 7.8|]][[Luke 7.9|]][[Luke 7.10|]])

- Peter’s mother-in-law ([[Matthew 8.14|Matthew 8:14–15]][[Matthew 8.15|]])

- A man with palsy ([[Mark 2.1|Mark 2:1–12]][[Mark 2.2|]][[Mark 2.3|]][[Mark 2.4|]][[Mark 2.5|]][[Mark 2.6|]][[Mark 2.7|]][[Mark 2.8|]][[Mark 2.9|]][[Mark 2.10|]][[Mark 2.11|]][[Mark 2.12|]])

- A man with a withered hand ([[Mark 3.1|Mark 3:1–5]][[Mark 3.2|]][[Mark 3.3|]][[Mark 3.4|]][[Mark 3.5|]])

What others do you find?

As you study each account, look for possible messages for you. You might ask yourself: What does the account teach about the Savior? What does it teach about exercising faith in Him? What does He want me to learn from this miracle?

### Jesus Christ came not to condemn sinners but to heal them.

Can you see yourself in [[Mark 2.15|Mark 2:15–17]][[Mark 2.16|]][[Mark 2.17|]] and [[Luke 7.36|Luke 7:36–50]][[Luke 7.37|]][[Luke 7.38|]][[Luke 7.39|]][[Luke 7.40|]][[Luke 7.41|]][[Luke 7.42|]][[Luke 7.43|]][[Luke 7.44|]][[Luke 7.45|]][[Luke 7.46|]][[Luke 7.47|]][[Luke 7.48|]][[Luke 7.49|]][[Luke 7.50|]]? For example, are your thoughts and actions ever like those of Simon the Pharisee? How would you describe the difference between the way Jesus saw sinners and the way Pharisees like Simon saw them? You might also ponder how you are like the woman described in [[Luke 7.36|Luke 7:36–50]][[Luke 7.37|]][[Luke 7.38|]][[Luke 7.39|]][[Luke 7.40|]][[Luke 7.41|]][[Luke 7.42|]][[Luke 7.43|]][[Luke 7.44|]][[Luke 7.45|]][[Luke 7.46|]][[Luke 7.47|]][[Luke 7.48|]][[Luke 7.49|]][[Luke 7.50|]].

- What do you learn from her about faith, love, and humility?

- How can you show greater appreciation for Jesus Christ’s power to cleanse and forgive you?

- You could sing with your children “The Miracle” (*Hymns—For Home and Church*, Gospel Library). It’s important to point out that sometimes we don’t receive the miracles we hope for, but we can continue to trust the Lord no matter what.

### Jesus loves me even when I sin, and He will forgive me as I repent.

- Here are some questions you and your children could think about as you read [[Mark 2.15|Mark 2:15–17]][[Mark 2.16|]][[Mark 2.17|]] or [[Luke 7.36|Luke 7:36–50]][[Luke 7.37|]][[Luke 7.38|]][[Luke 7.39|]][[Luke 7.40|]][[Luke 7.41|]][[Luke 7.42|]][[Luke 7.43|]][[Luke 7.44|]][[Luke 7.45|]][[Luke 7.46|]][[Luke 7.47|]][[Luke 7.48|]][[Luke 7.49|]][[Luke 7.50|]]: How does Jesus feel about sin? ### When I am afraid or in danger, Jesus can help me feel peace.

- Your children might enjoy pretending they are in a boat in a stormy sea while someone reads [[Mark 4.35|Mark 4:35–38]][[Mark 4.36|]][[Mark 4.37|]][[Mark 4.38|]]. Then, as someone reads [[Mark 4.39|verse 39]], they could pretend to be in a boat in a calm sea. (See also “Jesus Calms the Storm,” in *New Testament Stories*.)
