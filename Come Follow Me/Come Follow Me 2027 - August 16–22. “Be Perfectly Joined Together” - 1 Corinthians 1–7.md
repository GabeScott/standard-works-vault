# August 16–22. “Be Perfectly Joined Together”: 1 Corinthians 1–7

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/34?lang=eng)

During the months Paul spent in Corinth, “many of the Corinthians hearing \[him\] believed, and were baptized” ([[Acts 18.8|Acts 18:8]]). So it must have been heartbreaking for Paul to hear, just a few years later, that there were “divisions” and “contentions” among the Corinthian Saints and that in his absence they began to heed the “wisdom of this world” ([[1 Corinthians 1.10|1 Corinthians 1:10–11, 20]][[1 Corinthians 1.11|]][[1 Corinthians 1.20|]]). In response, Paul wrote the letter we now call 1 Corinthians. It is full of profound doctrine, yet Paul seemed disappointed that the Saints were not ready to receive all the doctrine he wanted to give them. “I, brethren, could not speak unto you as unto spiritual,” he lamented, “for ye are yet carnal” ([[1 Corinthians 3.1|1 Corinthians 3:1–3]][[1 Corinthians 3.2|]][[1 Corinthians 3.3|]]). As we prepare to read Paul’s words, it might be helpful to examine our own readiness to receive truth. That includes, in part, overcoming “divisions”—within ourselves, with our fellow Saints, and with God.

For more about Paul’s epistles to the Corinthians, see Guide to the Scriptures, “[[GS Corinthians, Epistles to|Corinthians, Epistles to]].”

## Ideas for Learning at Home and at Church

[[1 Corinthians 1.10|1 Corinthians 1:10–17]][[1 Corinthians 1.11|]][[1 Corinthians 1.12|]][[1 Corinthians 1.13|]][[1 Corinthians 1.14|]][[1 Corinthians 1.15|]][[1 Corinthians 1.16|]][[1 Corinthians 1.17|]]; [[1 Corinthians 3.1|3:1–11]][[1 Corinthians 3.2|]][[1 Corinthians 3.3|]][[1 Corinthians 3.4|]][[1 Corinthians 3.5|]][[1 Corinthians 3.6|]][[1 Corinthians 3.7|]][[1 Corinthians 3.8|]][[1 Corinthians 3.9|]][[1 Corinthians 3.10|]][[1 Corinthians 3.11|]]
### The members of Christ’s Church strive to be united.

We don’t know all the details about the lack of unity among the Corinthian Saints, but we know what unity feels like in our own relationships. Have you ever belonged to a club, group, team, or other organization that had a great sense of unity? Why did this group feel so united? Ponder this as you read [[1 Corinthians 1.10|1 Corinthians 1:10–17]][[1 Corinthians 1.11|]][[1 Corinthians 1.12|]][[1 Corinthians 1.13|]][[1 Corinthians 1.14|]][[1 Corinthians 1.15|]][[1 Corinthians 1.16|]][[1 Corinthians 1.17|]]; [[1 Corinthians 3.1|3:1–11]][[1 Corinthians 3.2|]][[1 Corinthians 3.3|]][[1 Corinthians 3.4|]][[1 Corinthians 3.5|]][[1 Corinthians 3.6|]][[1 Corinthians 3.7|]][[1 Corinthians 3.8|]][[1 Corinthians 3.9|]][[1 Corinthians 3.10|]][[1 Corinthians 3.11|]]. What insights do you gain about how to achieve unity in your family or ward?

Paul used the image of a building to encourage unity in [[1 Corinthians 3.9|1 Corinthians 3:9–17]][[1 Corinthians 3.10|]][[1 Corinthians 3.11|]][[1 Corinthians 3.12|]][[1 Corinthians 3.13|]][[1 Corinthians 3.14|]][[1 Corinthians 3.15|]][[1 Corinthians 3.16|]][[1 Corinthians 3.17|]]. How does this analogy help you better understand unity? In what sense are we “God’s building”? ([[1 Corinthians 3.9|1 Corinthians 3:9]]).

See also Ulisses Soares, “[[2023 October - Brothers and Sisters in Christ.md|Brothers and Sisters in Christ]],” *Liahona*, Nov. 2023, 70–73.

### The wisdom of God is greater than the wisdom of the world.

While it’s good—even encouraged—to seek wisdom wherever we can find it (see [[2 Nephi 9.29|2 Nephi 9:29]]; [[D&C 88.118|Doctrine and Covenants 88:118]]), Paul gave some strongly worded warnings about flawed human wisdom, which he called “the wisdom of this world” ([[1 Corinthians 1.20|1 Corinthians 1:20]]). Ponder what this phrase might mean. How is it different from the “wisdom of God”? ([[1 Corinthians 1.21|1 Corinthians 1:21]]). As you read [[1 Corinthians 1.17|1 Corinthians 1:17–31]][[1 Corinthians 1.18|]][[1 Corinthians 1.19|]][[1 Corinthians 1.20|]][[1 Corinthians 1.21|]][[1 Corinthians 1.22|]][[1 Corinthians 1.23|]][[1 Corinthians 1.24|]][[1 Corinthians 1.25|]][[1 Corinthians 1.26|]][[1 Corinthians 1.27|]][[1 Corinthians 1.28|]][[1 Corinthians 1.29|]][[1 Corinthians 1.30|]][[1 Corinthians 1.31|]]; [[1 Corinthians 2.1|2]][[1 Corinthians 2.2|]][[1 Corinthians 2.3|]][[1 Corinthians 2.4|]][[1 Corinthians 2.5|]][[1 Corinthians 2.6|]][[1 Corinthians 2.7|]][[1 Corinthians 2.8|]][[1 Corinthians 2.9|]][[1 Corinthians 2.10|]][[1 Corinthians 2.11|]][[1 Corinthians 2.12|]][[1 Corinthians 2.13|]][[1 Corinthians 2.14|]][[1 Corinthians 2.15|]][[1 Corinthians 2.16|]]; and [[1 Corinthians 3.18|3:18–20]][[1 Corinthians 3.19|]][[1 Corinthians 3.20|]], search for words like *wise* and *foolish*.

- What do these verses teach about being wise?

- What are things about the gospel that might seem foolish to some people? How do these things demonstrate the wisdom of God?

[[1 Corinthians 2.9|1 Corinthians 2:9–16]][[1 Corinthians 2.10|]][[1 Corinthians 2.11|]][[1 Corinthians 2.12|]][[1 Corinthians 2.13|]][[1 Corinthians 2.14|]][[1 Corinthians 2.15|]][[1 Corinthians 2.16|]]
### I must rely on the Holy Ghost in order to understand the things of God.

If you wanted to learn more about something like automotive mechanics or modern architecture, how would you do it? According to [[1 Corinthians 2.9|1 Corinthians 2:9–16]][[1 Corinthians 2.10|]][[1 Corinthians 2.11|]][[1 Corinthians 2.12|]][[1 Corinthians 2.13|]][[1 Corinthians 2.14|]][[1 Corinthians 2.15|]][[1 Corinthians 2.16|]], how is learning “the things of God” different from learning the “things of a man”? Why must we have the Holy Ghost in order to understand the things of God? After reading these verses, what do you feel you should do to understand spiritual things more fully?

See also Takashi Wada, “[[2024 October - The Words of Christ and the Holy Ghost Will Lead Us to the Truth.md|The Words of Christ and the Holy Ghost Will Lead Us to the Truth]],” *Liahona*, Nov. 2024, 105–7; “Speak to Us, Lord,” *Hymns—For Home and Church*, Gospel Library.

### My body is sacred.

Most people in Corinth felt that sexual immorality was acceptable and that their bodies were made primarily for pleasure. In other words, Corinth was not that different from the world today. What do you find in [[1 Corinthians 6.9|1 Corinthians 6:9–20]][[1 Corinthians 6.10|]][[1 Corinthians 6.11|]][[1 Corinthians 6.12|]][[1 Corinthians 6.13|]][[1 Corinthians 6.14|]][[1 Corinthians 6.15|]][[1 Corinthians 6.16|]][[1 Corinthians 6.17|]][[1 Corinthians 6.18|]][[1 Corinthians 6.19|]][[1 Corinthians 6.20|]] that could help you explain to others why you want to live a chaste life? You might also find insights in the August 2020 [special edition](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/liahona/2020/08/contents?lang=eng) of the *Ensign* or *Liahona*, which contains many articles dealing with sexual purity for people of all ages.

Comparing our bodies to temples, as Paul did, can be an effective way to learn about the sacredness of our bodies. Ponder these questions:

- Why are temples sacred? How is my body like a temple?

- What do you think it means to be “bought with a price”? ([[1 Corinthians 6.20|verse 20]]; see also [[D&C 18.10|Doctrine and Covenants 18:10–13]][[D&C 18.11|]][[D&C 18.12|]][[D&C 18.13|]]).

- What can I do to treat my body like a temple? (For ideas, see “Your Body Is Sacred,” in *For the Strength of Youth: A Guide for Making Choices*, 50–56.)

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[1 Corinthians 1.23|1 Corinthians 1:23–25]][[1 Corinthians 1.24|]][[1 Corinthians 1.25|]]
### Being wise means trusting in God’s wisdom.

- As you and your children read [[1 Corinthians 1.23|1 Corinthians 1:23–25]][[1 Corinthians 1.24|]][[1 Corinthians 1.25|]], you could help them find the words *wisdom* and *foolishness*. You might explain that many people thought Paul’s teachings were foolish, but Paul explained that believing Christ’s gospel is true wisdom. Perhaps you could help your children role-play how they could respond to people who think God’s commandments are “foolish”—such as the commandment to keep the Word of Wisdom or obey the law of tithing. For example, they could testify of the blessings that come from living these teachings.

[[1 Corinthians 2.11|1 Corinthians 2:11–14]][[1 Corinthians 2.12|]][[1 Corinthians 2.13|]][[1 Corinthians 2.14|]]
### The Holy Ghost teaches me truth.

- Perhaps you and your children could find objects or pictures that represent ways we learn about the world (such as a school, book, or smartphone). Then you could read together [[1 Corinthians 2.11|1 Corinthians 2:11, 14]][[1 Corinthians 2.14|]] to find out how we learn about God. Maybe you could tell each other about a time when you learned something through the Spirit of God.

- Here are some other scriptures related to [[1 Corinthians 2.11|1 Corinthians 2:11–14]][[1 Corinthians 2.12|]][[1 Corinthians 2.13|]][[1 Corinthians 2.14|]]: [[1 Nephi 10.17|1 Nephi 10:17]]; [[Moroni 10.3|Moroni 10:3–5]][[Moroni 10.4|]][[Moroni 10.5|]]; [[D&C 8.2|Doctrine and Covenants 8:2–3]][[D&C 8.3|]]. Your children could share what they learn from these scriptures about how the Holy Ghost teaches us (see also “[Let the Holy Spirit Guide](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/hymns/let-the-holy-spirit-guide?lang=eng),” *Hymns*, no. 143). Then you could share with each other why you are grateful for the Holy Ghost.

[[1 Corinthians 3.10|1 Corinthians 3:10–11]][[1 Corinthians 3.11|]]
### Jesus Christ is my foundation.

- To help your children visualize [[1 Corinthians 3.11|1 Corinthians 3:11]], it could be fun to build something together with blocks, cups, or other simple objects. Experiment with different foundations of varying stability. This could prompt discussion about making Jesus Christ our foundation as we “build” our lives. How do we do that?

- You could also sing together “[The Wise Man and the Foolish Man](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/the-wise-man-and-the-foolish-man?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 281); your children might enjoy doing actions to go along with the words. Maybe your children could write “Jesus Christ is my foundation” on small stones to remind them what they learned.

[[1 Corinthians 6.19|1 Corinthians 6:19–20]][[1 Corinthians 6.20|]]
### My body is like a temple.

- At the end of this outline is a picture of various temples. You and your children could look at that picture while you read together [[1 Corinthians 6.19|1 Corinthians 6:19–20]][[1 Corinthians 6.20|]]. You could talk about why temples are sacred and why our bodies are sacred. How do we show that temples are sacred to us? How do we show that our bodies are sacred to us?

- Your children might benefit from studying “Your Body Is Sacred” (in *For the Strength of Youth: A Guide for Making Choices*, 50–56). Invite them to share what they learn about how to treat their bodies like a sacred gift. You may also want to share what President Russell M. Nelson said when he called our bodies “your personal temple” (“[[2019 April - We Can Do Better and Be Better.md|We Can Do Better and Be Better]],” *Ensign* or *Liahona*, May 2019, 68). Maybe you could set a goal together to improve how you care for your bodies.
