# September 20–26. “Walk in the Spirit”: Galatians

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/39?lang=eng)

The gospel of Jesus Christ offers new life, hope for the future, and freedom from sin. Even so, sometimes it’s hard to let go of the old life, the past, and even our sins. Paul perceived some of that in the Saints in Galatia. They had accepted the Savior’s gospel, but some of them were insisting that certain traditions of the law of Moses were still required for salvation. Paul called these traditions the “yoke of bondage” ([[Galatians 5.1|Galatians 5:1]]). Anytime we place our trust in something other than Christ—even something good like the law of Moses—it’s as if we are rejecting the Savior’s freedom and “desire again to be in bondage” ([[Galatians 4.9|Galatians 4:9]]). So Paul’s urgent call to the Galatians and to all Christians is to come back to “the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free” ([[Galatians 5.1|Galatians 5:1]]). It’s not enough to be “called unto liberty” ([[Galatians 5.13|Galatians 5:13]]); we must also “stand fast” in it ([[Galatians 5.1|Galatians 5:1]]) by relying on Christ.

For more about the Epistle to the Galatians, see Guide to the Scriptures, “[[GS Galatians, Epistle to|Galatians, Epistle to]],” Gospel Library.

### Jesus Christ makes me free.

Studying any book of scripture is easier when we know why it was written. For this reason, it might be good to begin your study of Galatians with questions like these: What was Paul’s purpose in writing this epistle? What problem was he trying to solve? Look for clues in [[Galatians 1.6|Galatians 1:6–7]][[Galatians 1.7|]]; [[Galatians 3.1|3:1–5]][[Galatians 3.2|]][[Galatians 3.3|]][[Galatians 3.4|]][[Galatians 3.5|]]; [[Galatians 4.8|4:8–21]][[Galatians 4.9|]][[Galatians 4.10|]][[Galatians 4.11|]][[Galatians 4.12|]][[Galatians 4.13|]][[Galatians 4.14|]][[Galatians 4.15|]][[Galatians 4.16|]][[Galatians 4.17|]][[Galatians 4.18|]][[Galatians 4.19|]][[Galatians 4.20|]][[Galatians 4.21|]].

How might Paul’s concerns apply to you? For example, Paul observed that some Galatian Saints were still insisting on old traditions from the law of Moses. You might ponder what false traditions or other “yokes of bondage” might exist in your life.

- What might be preventing you from experiencing the freedom that the gospel offers?

- According to [[Galatians 5.1|Galatians 5:1, 13–14]][[Galatians 5.13|]][[Galatians 5.14|]], how can you find freedom from these attitudes and actions?

- How have the Savior and His gospel “made \[you\] free”? ([[Galatians 5.1|Galatians 5:1]]).

[[Galatians 2.20|Galatians 2:20]]
### “Christ liveth in me.”

What do you think it means to be “crucified with Christ”? How is your life different when He lives “in \[you\]”?

See also Ulisses Soares, “[[2022 April - In Awe of Christ and His Gospel.md|In Awe of Christ and His Gospel]],” *Liahona*, May 2022, 115–17.

[[Galatians 3.11|Galatians 3:11]]
### “The just shall live by faith.”

What does it look like, in your life, to “live by faith”? How is it different from living in other ways? Ponder what you could do to live more completely “by faith.”

[[Galatians 4.1|Galatians 4:1–7]][[Galatians 4.2|]][[Galatians 4.3|]][[Galatians 4.4|]][[Galatians 4.5|]][[Galatians 4.6|]][[Galatians 4.7|]]
### Through Jesus Christ, I am an heir of the Father.

As you study [[Galatians 4.1|Galatians 4:1–7]][[Galatians 4.2|]][[Galatians 4.3|]][[Galatians 4.4|]][[Galatians 4.5|]][[Galatians 4.6|]][[Galatians 4.7|]], ponder the differences between a king’s servants and his children. What opportunities or potential does a king’s child have that a servant does not? What do these verses teach about your relationship with Heavenly Father?

### If I sow “to the Spirit,” I will reap the “fruit of the Spirit.”

In [[Galatians 5.1|Galatians 5]][[Galatians 5.2|]][[Galatians 5.3|]][[Galatians 5.4|]][[Galatians 5.5|]][[Galatians 5.6|]][[Galatians 5.7|]][[Galatians 5.8|]][[Galatians 5.9|]][[Galatians 5.10|]][[Galatians 5.11|]][[Galatians 5.12|]][[Galatians 5.13|]][[Galatians 5.14|]][[Galatians 5.15|]][[Galatians 5.16|]][[Galatians 5.17|]][[Galatians 5.18|]][[Galatians 5.19|]][[Galatians 5.20|]][[Galatians 5.21|]][[Galatians 5.22|]][[Galatians 5.23|]][[Galatians 5.24|]][[Galatians 5.25|]][[Galatians 5.26|]], Paul describes a conflict that takes place in all of us—“the Spirit against the flesh” ([[Galatians 5.17|verse 17]]). Because “Christ hath made us free” ([[Galatians 5.1|verse 1]]), you get to decide who wins that battle in your own life. As you read [[Galatians 5.13|verses 13–26]][[Galatians 5.14|]][[Galatians 5.15|]][[Galatians 5.16|]][[Galatians 5.17|]][[Galatians 5.18|]][[Galatians 5.19|]][[Galatians 5.20|]][[Galatians 5.21|]][[Galatians 5.22|]][[Galatians 5.23|]][[Galatians 5.24|]][[Galatians 5.25|]][[Galatians 5.26|]], think about what you can do to help the Spirit win. For example, how can you weaken “the works of the flesh” ([[Galatians 5.19|verses 19–21]][[Galatians 5.20|]][[Galatians 5.21|]]) and nourish “the fruit of the Spirit”? ([[Galatians 5.22|verses 22–23]][[Galatians 5.23|]]).

Or, to think of it another way, Paul speaks of this conflict in terms of sowing and reaping (see [[Galatians 6.7|Galatians 6:7–10]][[Galatians 6.8|]][[Galatians 6.9|]][[Galatians 6.10|]]). What seeds can you sow in order to reap the fruit of the Spirit?

[[Galatians 6.7|Galatians 6:7–10]][[Galatians 6.8|]][[Galatians 6.9|]][[Galatians 6.10|]] also has a message for those who are “weary in well doing.” Maybe you’ve felt that way when your efforts to sow in the Spirit haven’t produced the fruit you were hoping for. What is the Lord’s message to you in these verses?

See also [[Alma 32.28|Alma 32:28, 41–43]][[Alma 32.41|]][[Alma 32.42|]][[Alma 32.43|]]; [[D&C 64.32|Doctrine and Covenants 64:32–34]][[D&C 64.33|]][[D&C 64.34|]]; “[Let the Holy Spirit Guide](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/hymns/let-the-holy-spirit-guide?lang=eng),” *Hymns*, no. 143.

[[Galatians 6.1|Galatians 6:1–2]][[Galatians 6.2|]]
### Bearing others’ burdens fulfills the law of Christ.

Reading these verses might inspire you to think about burdens people carry—literal and figurative. What do you think Paul might mean by “the law of Christ”? ([[Galatians 6.2|verse 2]]). And how does bearing each other’s burdens fulfill it?

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[Galatians 3.26|Galatians 3:26–29]][[Galatians 3.27|]][[Galatians 3.28|]][[Galatians 3.29|]]
### Being baptized makes us “one in Christ.”

- To illustrate [[Galatians 3.26|Galatians 3:26–29]][[Galatians 3.27|]][[Galatians 3.28|]][[Galatians 3.29|]], you could give your children slips of paper and invite them to write names or labels that apply to them (similar to those in [[Galatians 3.28|verse 28]]). On the other side of their papers, invite them to write *Jesus Christ*. They could then bring their papers together and attach them to a picture of the Savior. Share with each other what it means to you to be “one in Christ” when we are baptized.

[[Galatians 5.16|Galatians 5:16]]
### I can “walk in the Spirit.”

- Maybe you could read [[Galatians 5.16|Galatians 5:16]] while taking a walk together. As you walk, you could talk about what it means to “walk in the Spirit.” You could also sing together “[Teach Me to Walk in the Light](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/hymns/teach-me-to-walk-in-the-light?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 177). You might share President Emily Belle Freeman’s story about walking the “Jesus Trail” in Israel (see “[[2023 October - Walking in Covenant Relationship with Christ.md|Walking in Covenant Relationship with Christ]],” *Liahona*, Nov. 2023, 76–77) and talk about how you’ll help each other walk in the Spirit.

> **Any moment can become a teaching moment.** “You may have a scheduled home evening lesson or family scripture study, but teaching opportunities in the family frequently occur in informal, everyday moments—while eating a meal, doing chores, playing a game, traveling to work or school, reading a book, or watching a movie together. … Because such moments are unplanned, you can’t prepare for them the way you would prepare for a traditional lesson. However, you can prepare yourself by being sensitive to the Spirit” (*Teaching in the Savior’s Way*, [31](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/teaching-in-the-saviors-way-2022/12-part-3/13-suggestions-for-a-variety-of-teaching?lang=eng&id=title5#title5)).


[[Galatians 5.22|Galatians 5:22–23]][[Galatians 5.23|]]
### I can recognize the Spirit by its fruit.

- If your children have questions about how to recognize the Spirit, [[Galatians 5.22|Galatians 5:22–23]][[Galatians 5.23|]] can help. After reading these verses, you and your children could taste (or look at) several kinds of fruit and describe how each fruit tastes. Talking about the different flavors of fruits could give you an opportunity to talk about different ways we experience the Holy Ghost. Share with each other some ways you have noticed the Holy Ghost in your life.

- You could label some fruit (or pictures of fruit) with words Paul used to describe the fruit of the Spirit in [[Galatians 5.22|Galatians 5:22–23]][[Galatians 5.23|]]. Then your children could each select one, and you could discuss ways to “grow” this fruit of the Spirit in our lives (for ideas, see “Walk in God’s Light,” in *For the Strength of Youth: A Guide for Making Choices*, 22–26). After the discussion, maybe you could enjoy a fruit salad together.

[[Galatians 6.7|Galatians 6:7–9]][[Galatians 6.8|]][[Galatians 6.9|]]
### I reap what I sow.

- As you read [[Galatians 6.7|Galatians 6:7–9]][[Galatians 6.8|]][[Galatians 6.9|]], your children could pretend to plant a seed whenever they hear “sow” and pretend to pick fruit from a plant whenever they hear “reap.” Then you could ask your children about their favorite fruits or vegetables and talk about what it takes to grow a plant that produces that food. That could lead to a conversation about the blessings you and your children hope for (the fruit you hope to “reap”) and what you need to do to receive them (the seeds you need to “sow”).

- Your children might also enjoy matching seeds with the fruits they grow into. Then you could provide them with a list of actions and a list of consequences, and they could match items from the two lists. Share with your children some of the “fruit” that you have received by following the Savior and His gospel.
