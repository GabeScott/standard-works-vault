# May 3–9. “What Lack I Yet?”: Matthew 19–20; Mark 10; Luke 18

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/19?lang=eng)

If you had the chance to ask Jesus a question, what would you ask? When one young man got that chance, he asked, “What good thing shall I do, that I may have eternal life?” ([[Matthew 19.16|Matthew 19:16]]). The Savior’s answer showed both appreciation for the good things the young man had already done and loving encouragement to do more. When we ask, in our own way, “What lack I yet?” ([[Matthew 19.20|Matthew 19:20]]), the Lord can give us answers that are as personal as His response to the young man. Whatever the Lord asks us to do, acting on His answer requires that we trust Him more than our own righteousness (see [[Luke 18.9|Luke 18:9–14]][[Luke 18.10|]][[Luke 18.11|]][[Luke 18.12|]][[Luke 18.13|]][[Luke 18.14|]]) and that we “receive the kingdom of God as a little child” ([[Luke 18.17|Luke 18:17]]).

### Marriage between man and woman is essential to God’s plan.

When the Pharisees asked Jesus a question about marriage, their purpose was to test Him, not learn from Him. Even so, there’s much we can learn from Jesus’s answer. After reading [[Matthew 19.3|Matthew 19:3–9]][[Matthew 19.4|]][[Matthew 19.5|]][[Matthew 19.6|]][[Matthew 19.7|]][[Matthew 19.8|]][[Matthew 19.9|]] and [[Mark 10.2|Mark 10:2–12]][[Mark 10.3|]][[Mark 10.4|]][[Mark 10.5|]][[Mark 10.6|]][[Mark 10.7|]][[Mark 10.8|]][[Mark 10.9|]][[Mark 10.10|]][[Mark 10.11|]][[Mark 10.12|]], you could list statements that you feel summarize the Lord’s views on marriage. You could do the same with “[The Family: A Proclamation to the World](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/the-family-a-proclamation-to-the-world/the-family-a-proclamation-to-the-world?lang=eng)” (Gospel Library).

- How does your knowledge of the Father’s plan of salvation affect the way you think and feel about marriage? Why are these views important to you and those you love?

- How can the Savior help marriages and families when they struggle?

See also Topics and Questions, “[Marriage](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/gospel-topics/marriage-study-guide?lang=eng),” Gospel Library.

### Following Jesus Christ requires sacrifice.

Even if you aren’t rich, young, or a man, perhaps you can see yourself in the rich young man who asked Jesus how to receive eternal life. As you read his story, you might ask yourself:

- Have I ever felt the way he might have felt?

- What evidence do I find of his faithfulness and sincerity?

- If I were this young man, what might be different about this story? What would I say to Jesus? What might He say to me?

This account may prompt you to ask, “What lack I yet?” ([[Matthew 19.20|Matthew 19:20]]). How does the Lord make up for what you lack? (see [[Ether 12.27|Ether 12:27]]).

See also Jeffrey R. Holland, “[[2021 October - The Greatest Possession.md|The Greatest Possession]],” *Liahona*, Nov. 2021, 8–10; “Christ and the Rich Young Ruler” (video), Gospel Library.

### The Lord offers the fulness of His blessings to all.

Like many of Jesus’s parables, the one in [[Matthew 20.1|Matthew 20:1–16]][[Matthew 20.2|]][[Matthew 20.3|]][[Matthew 20.4|]][[Matthew 20.5|]][[Matthew 20.6|]][[Matthew 20.7|]][[Matthew 20.8|]][[Matthew 20.9|]][[Matthew 20.10|]][[Matthew 20.11|]][[Matthew 20.12|]][[Matthew 20.13|]][[Matthew 20.14|]][[Matthew 20.15|]][[Matthew 20.16|]] has a surprise ending. Often, surprises like that are meant to help us change our thinking about something. As you read [[Matthew 20.1|Matthew 20:1–16]][[Matthew 20.2|]][[Matthew 20.3|]][[Matthew 20.4|]][[Matthew 20.5|]][[Matthew 20.6|]][[Matthew 20.7|]][[Matthew 20.8|]][[Matthew 20.9|]][[Matthew 20.10|]][[Matthew 20.11|]][[Matthew 20.12|]][[Matthew 20.13|]][[Matthew 20.14|]][[Matthew 20.15|]][[Matthew 20.16|]], you might ask yourself, “What does the Lord want me to change about my thinking?”

Here are some other questions that could help you ponder this parable:

- What does this parable teach you about the kingdom of heaven?

- If the “householder” represents Christ, what do you learn about Him from this parable?

- Before Jesus told this parable, Peter said the disciples had “forsaken all” to follow Him. Peter then asked, “What shall we have therefore?” ([[Matthew 19.27|Matthew 19:27]]). How does the parable answer Peter’s question?

See also Jeffrey R. Holland, “[[2012 April - The Laborers in the Vineyard.md|The Laborers in the Vineyard]],” *Ensign* or *Liahona*, May 2012, 31–33; “Laborers in the Vineyard” (video), Gospel Library.

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[Mark 10.6|Mark 10:6–9]][[Mark 10.7|]][[Mark 10.8|]][[Mark 10.9|]]
### Heavenly Father wants marriages to last forever.

- Do you have a wedding picture you could show your children—your own or from someone in your family? Maybe you could display it while you read [[Mark 10.6|Mark 10:6–9]][[Mark 10.7|]][[Mark 10.8|]][[Mark 10.9|]]. Your children could suggest phrases from these verses that go with the picture.

- Why does Heavenly Father want men and women to get married? You and your children could look for answers in “[The Family: A Proclamation to the World](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/the-family-a-proclamation-to-the-world/the-family-a-proclamation-to-the-world?lang=eng)” (Gospel Library).

- You could sing a song about the temple or eternal families, such as “[I Love to See the Temple](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/i-love-to-see-the-temple?lang=eng)” or “[Families Can Be Together Forever](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/families-can-be-together-forever?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 95, 188). Let your children hold up pictures of temples or families when they sing “temple” or “family.” What do we learn from these songs about how families can last forever?

[[Mark 10.13|Mark 10:13–16]][[Mark 10.14|]][[Mark 10.15|]][[Mark 10.16|]]
### Jesus wants little children to come to Him so He can bless them.

- After reading [[Mark 10.13|Mark 10:13–16]][[Mark 10.14|]][[Mark 10.15|]][[Mark 10.16|]], you could help your children retell the story in their own words. It might help to use “Jesus Blesses Little Children” (in *New Testament Stories*) or one of the pictures in this outline. Help your children imagine how they would feel if they were one of the children in this story. What would they say to Jesus? What do we learn about Jesus from this story?> 1:1


- Perhaps you and your children could think of times when you have felt Jesus’s love. This might be a good time to bear your testimony to your children that Jesus loves them and wants to bless them.

[[Matthew 19.16|Matthew 19:16–22]][[Matthew 19.17|]][[Matthew 19.18|]][[Matthew 19.19|]][[Matthew 19.20|]][[Matthew 19.21|]][[Matthew 19.22|]]
### Jesus can help me know what I need to do to follow Him.

- As you read the story in [[Mark 10.16|Mark 10:16–22]][[Mark 10.17|]][[Mark 10.18|]][[Mark 10.19|]][[Mark 10.20|]][[Mark 10.21|]][[Mark 10.22|]] with your children, you could invite them to look for the questions that the man asked Jesus (see also “Jesus and the Rich Young Man,” in *New Testament Stories*). Then they could find Jesus’s answers.> 2:7


- To help your children personalize the story, you could give each of them a piece of paper that says *What lack I yet?* Give them time to quietly think about what Jesus might say if they asked Him what they need to do to follow Him. Encourage them to write or draw one thing on their papers—it could be something different for each of them. You might suggest that your children keep their papers someplace where they will see them often. The last two paragraphs of Elder S. Mark Palmer’s message “[[2017 April - Then Jesus Beholding Him Loved Him.md|Then Jesus Beholding Him Loved Him]]” could help with this activity (*Ensign* or *Liahona*, May 2017, 116).
