# September 13–19. “God Loveth a Cheerful Giver”: 2 Corinthians 8–13

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/38?lang=eng)

Paul taught the Corinthians about a God of abundance. His “grace is sufficient,” Paul wrote ([[2 Corinthians 12.9|2 Corinthians 12:9]]). “And God is able to make all grace abound toward you; that ye, always having all sufficiency in all things, may abound to every good work” ([[2 Corinthians 9.8|2 Corinthians 9:8]]). We also know from the scriptures that God has filled the earth with good things—“there is enough and to spare” ([[D&C 104.17|Doctrine and Covenants 104:17]]). Why, then, are there so many in the world who don’t have enough, who are in desperate need, both spiritually and physically? Why do some seem to have so much while others have so little? We may not know all the reasons, but we do know what God wants us to do about it: “God loveth a cheerful giver” ([[2 Corinthians 9.7|2 Corinthians 9:7]]). Paul hoped to persuade the Corinthian Saints to share what God had so generously given them to help people in need. But this wasn’t just a request for donations; it was a call to become more Christlike. Whether your abundance is temporal or spiritual, Paul’s invitation is to give abundantly, freely—“not grudgingly, or of necessity” ([[2 Corinthians 9.7|2 Corinthians 9:7]]) but with love for God and neighbor. After all, it’s love that makes a giver cheerful.

[[2 Corinthians 10.3|2 Corinthians 10:3–6]][[2 Corinthians 10.4|]][[2 Corinthians 10.5|]][[2 Corinthians 10.6|]]
### I can bring my “every thought to the obedience of Christ.”

In [[2 Corinthians 10.3|2 Corinthians 10:3–6]][[2 Corinthians 10.4|]][[2 Corinthians 10.5|]][[2 Corinthians 10.6|]], Paul compared following Christ to declaring “war” against evil in our lives. As you read these verses, you might ask yourself:

- What “strong holds … against the knowledge of God” need to be pulled down in my life?

- What are “the weapons of … warfare” that God has given me to accomplish this?

- What does it mean to bring “into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ”?

See also [[D&C 6.36|Doctrine and Covenants 6:36]].

[[2 Corinthians 11.1|2 Corinthians 11:1–4]][[2 Corinthians 11.2|]][[2 Corinthians 11.3|]][[2 Corinthians 11.4|]]
### I can focus on “the simplicity that is in Christ.”

Paul urged the Corinthians not to leave “the simplicity that is in Christ” ([[2 Corinthians 11.3|2 Corinthians 11:3]]).

- What does that phrase mean to you?

- How have you found simplicity in Christ and His gospel?

- How might your mind “be corrupted from \[His\] simplicity”?

- What else do you learn from [[2 Nephi 33.6|2 Nephi 33:6]] and [[Jacob 4.14|Jacob 4:14]]?

To help you ponder “the simplicity that is in Christ,” you might try this: Imagine that a newspaper editor has invited you, as a believer in Jesus Christ, to write a headline for a story about the Savior’s gospel. What would you write?

Consider also this counsel from President Dieter F. Uchtdorf: “If you ever think that the gospel isn’t working so well for you, I invite you to step back, look at your life from a higher plane, and simplify your approach to discipleship. Focus on the basic doctrines, principles, and applications of the gospel. I promise that God will guide and bless you on your path to a fulfilling life, and the gospel will definitely work better for you” (“[[2015 October - It Works Wonderfully!.md|It Works Wonderfully!]],” *Ensign* or *Liahona*, Nov. 2015, 22). What do you feel inspired to do to “simplify your approach to discipleship”?

[[2 Corinthians 12.5|2 Corinthians 12:5–10]][[2 Corinthians 12.6|]][[2 Corinthians 12.7|]][[2 Corinthians 12.8|]][[2 Corinthians 12.9|]][[2 Corinthians 12.10|]]
### The Savior’s grace is sufficient to help me find strength in my weakness.

We don’t know what Paul’s “thorn in the flesh” was, but we all have our own thorns that we wish God would remove from our lives. Think about your thorns as you read [[2 Corinthians 12.5|2 Corinthians 12:5–10]][[2 Corinthians 12.6|]][[2 Corinthians 12.7|]][[2 Corinthians 12.8|]][[2 Corinthians 12.9|]][[2 Corinthians 12.10|]].

- What possible insights do you gain from these verses about prayers that seem to be unanswered?

- What do you learn about Jesus Christ?

- What does it mean to you that His “grace is sufficient” for you?

Why did Paul “glory in” or “take pleasure in infirmities”? ([[2 Corinthians 12.9|verses 9–10]][[2 Corinthians 12.10|]]). As you look back on your life, can you think of any “glorious” things that resulted from difficult times in your past? This might help you look forward to ways the Savior can turn your current difficulties into something glorious. You can find some helpful counsel and examples in Elder Gerrit W. Gong’s message “[[2024 April - All Things for Our Good.md|All Things for Our Good]],” *Liahona*, May 2024, 41–44.

See also [[Ether 12.27|Ether 12:27]]; Dieter F. Uchtdorf, “[[2015 April - The Gift of Grace.md|The Gift of Grace]],” *Ensign* or *Liahona*, May 2015, 107–10.

## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[2 Corinthians 9.6|2 Corinthians 9:6–9]][[2 Corinthians 9.7|]][[2 Corinthians 9.8|]][[2 Corinthians 9.9|]]
### I can be “a cheerful giver.”

- You and your children could repeat the phrase “God loveth a cheerful giver” ([[2 Corinthians 9.7|2 Corinthians 9:7]]) several times—first cheerfully, then with other attitudes, such as sad, tired, or angry. Then you could pretend to do a kind act of service—first cheerfully, then with other attitudes. This could lead to a conversation about what it means to be “a cheerful giver” and why God loves cheerful giving.

- Your children might enjoy making badges that say “I am a cheerful giver.” Whenever you see your children cheerfully doing something kind, you can award them a badge. They might also like to award badges to people they see being a cheerful giver.

- You could also sing a song about service, such as “[Fun to Do](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/fun-to-do?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 253). Help the children think of new verses to the song that describe a variety of ways to serve others.

[[2 Corinthians 10.3|2 Corinthians 10:3–7]][[2 Corinthians 10.4|]][[2 Corinthians 10.5|]][[2 Corinthians 10.6|]][[2 Corinthians 10.7|]]
### Jesus can help me win my battle against sin.

- How could you teach your children about our “warfare” against sin? Maybe they could build a fort or “strong hold” with chairs and blankets while you read [[2 Corinthians 10.3|2 Corinthians 10:3–7]][[2 Corinthians 10.4|]][[2 Corinthians 10.5|]][[2 Corinthians 10.6|]][[2 Corinthians 10.7|]] together. Then, as you talk about how to “pull down” things that lead us away from God, your children could knock over the stronghold you built. What are the spiritual “weapons” we use to win our spiritual battles?

[[2 Corinthians 12.7|2 Corinthians 12:7–10]][[2 Corinthians 12.8|]][[2 Corinthians 12.9|]][[2 Corinthians 12.10|]]
### Jesus Christ can make me strong, even when my trials make me feel weak.

- As you and your children read [[2 Corinthians 12.7|2 Corinthians 12:7–10]][[2 Corinthians 12.8|]][[2 Corinthians 12.9|]][[2 Corinthians 12.10|]], you could look at a plant with thorns and talk about what it would feel like to have a thorn stuck in their skin. Then you could talk about Paul’s “thorn in the flesh”—something difficult in his life that he asked God to remove. You might also share with each other difficult things in your lives. What did Paul learn from his experience? “Jesus Can Make Weak Things Strong” (in *New Testament Stories*) can help your children answer this question.> 1:29


- Elder Brook P. Hales, in his message “[[2024 October - Mortality Works!.md|Mortality Works!]],” shared three stories of people who had difficult trials (*Liahona*, Nov. 2024, 83–86). With the Lord’s help, their trials made them stronger. Maybe you and your children could find those stories and share some examples of your own.

- You could help your children to compare [[2 Corinthians 12.9|2 Corinthians 12:9–10]][[2 Corinthians 12.10|]] and [[Ether 12.27|Ether 12:27]]. What words or phrases are repeated? What do we learn about Jesus Christ from these verses? What do we learn about weakness?
