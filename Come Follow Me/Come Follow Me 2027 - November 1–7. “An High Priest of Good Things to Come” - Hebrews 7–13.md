# November 1–7. “An High Priest of Good Things to Come”: Hebrews 7–13

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/45?lang=eng)

Even faithful believers sometimes suffer “reproaches and afflictions” that can shake their confidence (see [[Hebrews 10.32|Hebrews 10:32–38]][[Hebrews 10.33|]][[Hebrews 10.34|]][[Hebrews 10.35|]][[Hebrews 10.36|]][[Hebrews 10.37|]][[Hebrews 10.38|]]). This happened to early Jewish converts to Christianity, and some were losing their faith. The message of the Epistle to the Hebrews was to stay true to their testimonies and remember the long tradition of faithful believers from Jewish history: Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Sara, Joseph, Moses—“a cloud of witnesses” that God’s promises are real and worth waiting for (see [[Hebrews 11.1|Hebrews 11]][[Hebrews 11.2|]][[Hebrews 11.3|]][[Hebrews 11.4|]][[Hebrews 11.5|]][[Hebrews 11.6|]][[Hebrews 11.7|]][[Hebrews 11.8|]][[Hebrews 11.9|]][[Hebrews 11.10|]][[Hebrews 11.11|]][[Hebrews 11.12|]][[Hebrews 11.13|]][[Hebrews 11.14|]][[Hebrews 11.15|]][[Hebrews 11.16|]][[Hebrews 11.17|]][[Hebrews 11.18|]][[Hebrews 11.19|]][[Hebrews 11.20|]][[Hebrews 11.21|]][[Hebrews 11.22|]][[Hebrews 11.23|]][[Hebrews 11.24|]][[Hebrews 11.25|]][[Hebrews 11.26|]][[Hebrews 11.27|]][[Hebrews 11.28|]][[Hebrews 11.29|]][[Hebrews 11.30|]][[Hebrews 11.31|]][[Hebrews 11.32|]][[Hebrews 11.33|]][[Hebrews 11.34|]][[Hebrews 11.35|]][[Hebrews 11.36|]][[Hebrews 11.37|]][[Hebrews 11.38|]][[Hebrews 11.39|]][[Hebrews 11.40|]]; [[Hebrews 12.1|12:1]]).

This tradition is yours too. It’s a heritage of faith shared by everyone who looks “unto Jesus \[as\] the author and finisher of our faith” ([[Hebrews 12.2|Hebrews 12:2]]). Because of Him, whenever adversity makes us want to “draw back” ([[Hebrews 10.38|Hebrews 10:38]]), we can instead “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith” ([[Hebrews 10.22|Hebrews 10:22]]). For us, as for the ancient Saints, Jesus Christ is our “high priest of good things to come” ([[Hebrews 9.11|Hebrews 9:11]]).

### The Melchizedek Priesthood points me to Jesus Christ.

For centuries the Jewish people had exercised the Levitical Priesthood, also known as the Aaronic Priesthood. But Jesus Christ restored the Melchizedek Priesthood, which offers even greater blessings. As you read [[Hebrews 7.1|Hebrews 7]][[Hebrews 7.2|]][[Hebrews 7.3|]][[Hebrews 7.4|]][[Hebrews 7.5|]][[Hebrews 7.6|]][[Hebrews 7.7|]][[Hebrews 7.8|]][[Hebrews 7.9|]][[Hebrews 7.10|]][[Hebrews 7.11|]][[Hebrews 7.12|]][[Hebrews 7.13|]][[Hebrews 7.14|]][[Hebrews 7.15|]][[Hebrews 7.16|]][[Hebrews 7.17|]][[Hebrews 7.18|]][[Hebrews 7.19|]][[Hebrews 7.20|]][[Hebrews 7.21|]][[Hebrews 7.22|]][[Hebrews 7.23|]][[Hebrews 7.24|]][[Hebrews 7.25|]][[Hebrews 7.26|]][[Hebrews 7.27|]][[Hebrews 7.28|]], ponder what these blessings are. You might consider how the Melchizedek Priesthood points us to Jesus Christ (see, for example, [[Hebrews 7.15|verses 15–16, 24–28]][[Hebrews 7.16|]][[Hebrews 7.24|]][[Hebrews 7.25|]][[Hebrews 7.26|]][[Hebrews 7.27|]][[Hebrews 7.28|]]). Think about Melchizedek Priesthood ordinances you have received. How have they pointed you to the Savior? What have they taught you about Him?

### Jesus Christ is my Mediator.

Jesus Christ is the Mediator, or agent, between us and Heavenly Father. Through Him, we can repent and be reconciled to God (see Guide to the Scriptures, “[[GS Mediator|Mediator]],” Gospel Library). [[Hebrews 8.6|Hebrews 8:6]] calls Christ “the mediator of a better covenant.” As you read [[Hebrews 8.6|Hebrews 8:6–13]][[Hebrews 8.7|]][[Hebrews 8.8|]][[Hebrews 8.9|]][[Hebrews 8.10|]][[Hebrews 8.11|]][[Hebrews 8.12|]][[Hebrews 8.13|]]; [[Hebrews 9.14|9:14–15]][[Hebrews 9.15|]], consider how the Savior brings you closer to God.

- What are the “better promises” the Lord offers you through His covenants? ([[Hebrews 8.6|Hebrews 8:6]]). How have you come to know that the Savior is your Mediator?

- How has the Lord put His covenant in your mind? How has He written it in your heart? (see [[Hebrews 8.10|Hebrews 8:10]]).

### Heavenly Father and Jesus Christ will help me and never leave me.

- Help your children find the Lord’s promise in [[Hebrews 13.5|Hebrews 13:5]]: “I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.” Maybe you could also help them memorize it. Share with each other how the Lord has kept this promise in your lives. How do you know when He is with you?

- Perhaps you could review with your children some of the stories they learned this year about the Savior helping others (see, for example, [[Luke 5.18|Luke 5:18–26]][[Luke 5.19|]][[Luke 5.20|]][[Luke 5.21|]][[Luke 5.22|]][[Luke 5.23|]][[Luke 5.24|]][[Luke 5.25|]][[Luke 5.26|]]; [[Matthew 14.15|Matthew 14:15–21]][[Matthew 14.16|]][[Matthew 14.17|]][[Matthew 14.18|]][[Matthew 14.19|]][[Matthew 14.20|]][[Matthew 14.21|]]). Then read [[Hebrews 13.6|Hebrews 13:6]] together, and share with each other a time when the Lord helped you. What can we ask the Lord to help us with? You could also sing together “[I Feel My Savior’s Love](https://churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/childrens-songbook/i-feel-my-saviors-love?lang=eng)” (*Children’s Songbook*, 74–75). Help your children recognize ways they can feel Jesus Christ’s love.
