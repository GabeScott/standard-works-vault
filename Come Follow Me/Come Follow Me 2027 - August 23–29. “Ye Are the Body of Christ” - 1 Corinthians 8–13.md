# August 23–29. “Ye Are the Body of Christ”: 1 Corinthians 8–13

[Link](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/come-follow-me-for-home-and-church-new-testament-2027/35?lang=eng)

In Paul’s time, Corinth was a diverse city, with people from all over the Roman Empire. That made it hard for Church members in Corinth to be united, so Paul encouraged them to find unity in their belief in Christ. This unity was to be more than just peaceful coexistence; Paul wasn’t asking them to just tolerate each other’s differences. Rather, he taught that when you join the Church of Jesus Christ, you are “baptized into one body,” and every body part is needed ([[1 Corinthians 12.13|1 Corinthians 12:13]]). When one member is lost, it’s like losing a limb. When one member suffers, we all feel it and try to help. In this kind of unity, differences are not just acknowledged but cherished, because without members of diverse gifts and abilities, the body would be limited. So whether you’ve always felt at home in the Church or you wonder if you belong, Paul’s message to you is that unity is not sameness. You need your fellow Saints, and your fellow Saints need you.

- What kinds of “escape” from temptation has God given you?

- What do you learn about God from these verses?

### The sacrament unifies the members of Christ’s Church.

The sacrament involves a personal commitment between you and the Lord. Yet it is also an experience you share with others. Read what Paul taught about the sacrament in [[1 Corinthians 10.16|1 Corinthians 10:16–17]][[1 Corinthians 10.17|]]; [[1 Corinthians 11.16|11:16–30]][[1 Corinthians 11.17|]][[1 Corinthians 11.18|]][[1 Corinthians 11.19|]][[1 Corinthians 11.20|]][[1 Corinthians 11.21|]][[1 Corinthians 11.22|]][[1 Corinthians 11.23|]][[1 Corinthians 11.24|]][[1 Corinthians 11.25|]][[1 Corinthians 11.26|]][[1 Corinthians 11.27|]][[1 Corinthians 11.28|]][[1 Corinthians 11.29|]][[1 Corinthians 11.30|]], and ponder these questions:

- How does the sacrament help “many” become “one” in Christ? ([[1 Corinthians 10.17|1 Corinthians 10:17]]).

- What helps you “examine \[your\]self” during the sacrament? ([[1 Corinthians 11.28|1 Corinthians 11:28]]).

Based on what you’ve read, what do you feel inspired to do to improve your experience with the sacrament?

### Everyone is needed in “the body of Christ.”

([[1 Corinthians 13.8|verse 8]]). Why are we “nothing” without it? ## Ideas for Teaching Children

[[1 Corinthians 11.23|1 Corinthians 11:23–29]][[1 Corinthians 11.24|]][[1 Corinthians 11.25|]][[1 Corinthians 11.26|]][[1 Corinthians 11.27|]][[1 Corinthians 11.28|]][[1 Corinthians 11.29|]]
### The sacrament points my mind to the Savior.

- As you read [[1 Corinthians 11.23|1 Corinthians 11:23–29]][[1 Corinthians 11.24|]][[1 Corinthians 11.25|]][[1 Corinthians 11.26|]][[1 Corinthians 11.27|]][[1 Corinthians 11.28|]][[1 Corinthians 11.29|]] together, you might talk about what it means to “examine” ourselves while taking the sacrament. Maybe your children could think of other people who examine things, like doctors, detectives, or scientists. When we partake of the sacrament, what helps us think about our relationship with the Lord and how He is helping us live our baptismal covenant?

- With your children, make a list of things you can think about during the sacrament. According to [[1 Corinthians 11.23|1 Corinthians 11:23–29]][[1 Corinthians 11.24|]][[1 Corinthians 11.25|]][[1 Corinthians 11.26|]][[1 Corinthians 11.27|]][[1 Corinthians 11.28|]][[1 Corinthians 11.29|]], what does the Lord want us to think about? You could even look for scriptures, hymns, or pictures that would help you and your children ponder these things.

[[1 Corinthians 12.1|1 Corinthians 12:1–11]][[1 Corinthians 12.2|]][[1 Corinthians 12.3|]][[1 Corinthians 12.4|]][[1 Corinthians 12.5|]][[1 Corinthians 12.6|]][[1 Corinthians 12.7|]][[1 Corinthians 12.8|]][[1 Corinthians 12.9|]][[1 Corinthians 12.10|]][[1 Corinthians 12.11|]]
### Heavenly Father has blessed me with spiritual gifts.

- You could start a discussion about gifts of the Spirit by talking with your children about gifts they have received. Maybe they could draw a picture of a favorite gift. Then you could read together [[1 Corinthians 12.7|1 Corinthians 12:7–11]][[1 Corinthians 12.8|]][[1 Corinthians 12.9|]][[1 Corinthians 12.10|]][[1 Corinthians 12.11|]] and list some of the spiritual gifts you find there.

- To help your children recognize their spiritual gifts, consider passing around sheets of paper, each with the name of one of your children at the top. Ask them to list on the paper the spiritual gifts they notice in that person. How can we use these gifts to bless Heavenly Father’s children?

### Every member is needed in the Savior’s Church.

- To visualize [[1 Corinthians 12.12|1 Corinthians 12:12–27]][[1 Corinthians 12.13|]][[1 Corinthians 12.14|]][[1 Corinthians 12.15|]][[1 Corinthians 12.16|]][[1 Corinthians 12.17|]][[1 Corinthians 12.18|]][[1 Corinthians 12.19|]][[1 Corinthians 12.20|]][[1 Corinthians 12.21|]][[1 Corinthians 12.22|]][[1 Corinthians 12.23|]][[1 Corinthians 12.24|]][[1 Corinthians 12.25|]][[1 Corinthians 12.26|]][[1 Corinthians 12.27|]], your children might enjoy trying to draw a body made only of eyes or ears (see [[1 Corinthians 12.17|verse 17]]). What would that body be unable to do? Then your children could draw another body, including the body parts mentioned in [[1 Corinthians 12.15|verses 15–24]][[1 Corinthians 12.16|]][[1 Corinthians 12.17|]][[1 Corinthians 12.18|]][[1 Corinthians 12.19|]][[1 Corinthians 12.20|]][[1 Corinthians 12.21|]][[1 Corinthians 12.22|]][[1 Corinthians 12.23|]][[1 Corinthians 12.24|]]. Why do we need every body part—including small parts like toes? Maybe they could put their names on the body parts, and you can talk about why each of them is needed in the Savior’s Church.
